## 1. [4] Appendix B: the trunk frame (spec §4.2)

- PDF pages 178–192 (printed 164–178) are diagrams; pages 185–186 (printed 171–172) draw lead/trail shoulder azimuth and elevation: a red arm line, a red reference line, a coloured arc. **No axes are labelled.**
- Appendix C `PolarShoulder()` (printed 185–188) is the only definition: joint centres `V_*_Shoulder_JC_Trunk`, `V_*_Elbow_JC_Trunk` (in the upper trunk's frame), vector = shoulder − elbow, θ = acos(Z/|r|), φ = atan2(Y, X) − 90°. Elevation's picture shows the arc between the trunk's long axis pointing down and the arm: θ = 0° is the arm hanging, as the spec has it.
- Axes inferred from [4]'s values (4.1.4–4.1.5, Appendix E): trail arm at launch, θ 71°, φ −78° ⇒ elbow → shoulder is +X with a little +Y ⇒ X = toward the lead side, and Y = backward in a right-handed frame with Z up. Check at contact: both shoulders' φ ≈ 0° (−1°, −9°) ⇒ elbow → shoulder points backward ⇒ elbows forward of the shoulders, as at contact they must be. The trail elbow's forward motion from −78° to −42° ("dropped and moved forward") and the lead arm's go the same way.
- Result: **reading 1 confirmed; nothing in the spec moves.** Not established: the frame's handedness is inferred, not drawn.

## 2. [4] Appendix E (spec §5.4)

- `analysis/research/fortenbaugh_appendix_e.py` extracts the 32 raster graphs (`pdfimages`, 1027×745 px, two per page from PDF page 234), finds the five event lines (−621, −340, −170, −93, 0 ms) for the time axis and the frame rows for the value axis, and takes the mean row of the thick curve's colour at each column. Graph titles identified by reading them (index 29 = bat azimuth, 28 elevation, 19 lag, 30 azimuth rate, 20–23 shoulders, 26–27 elbows, 9 pelvis X).
- Check against the text: azimuth −173.1° at −93 ms (text −175°), −9.7° at contact (−7°); elevation 39.1° and −29.7° (39°, −30°); lag 132.7° at −80 ms (133° at −80), 64.4° at contact (65°); rate 2,419°/s at −10 ms (2,435°), 1,250°/s at −93 ms (1,247°), 2,214°/s at contact (2,233°); shoulder angles within 1–2° of §4.1.3–4.1.5. So the reading error is about the pixel (table in the spec); the curve's own thickness adds ±1 px, and a time offset of 1–3 ms on steep parts (azimuth at contact reads 2.7° off the text).
- Not in Appendix E: the hands, the clavicle → wrist vector and its rate, the bat tip's speed. The lead shoulder's azimuth rate is there (342°/s peak, text).
- v3 against it: `analysis/research/swing_v3_vs_appendix_e.py` (needs the spec probe `sim/target/codex-probes/v3_review_probe.py`, not in git). Result in §5.4: azimuth within 8°; elevation 8–13° low in the last 80 ms; adding two measured elevation points (20.4° at −60, −11.1° at −30 ms) overshoots the sweet-spot speed by 6–11 mph at −30…−20 ms.
- Also read: pelvis X relative to home plate (cm): −51 at −1000 ms, −57 at −620, −34 at contact, −31 at +200 (sd about 10); the plate's reference point is unstated.
