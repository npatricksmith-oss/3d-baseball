## What it is and how far to trust it
- Journal paper, Human Performance Technologies (Jupiter, FL) and Good Samaritan Medical Center orthopaedic research lab (West Palm Beach, FL). Aim: a baseline "natural coordination" description of the swing for rehab and training. (Abstract, Methods)
- Subjects: 39 male pros tested (25 right-handed, 14 left-handed). Only right-handed batters with at least 100 at-bats and at least .250 in 1993 were kept: 7 subjects, mean average .293, mean 273 at-bats; 6 minor league and 1 major league. Mean weight printed as 844 N and mean height 1.83 m (the printed SDs, 224 N and 2.06 m, are not plausible, so treat the SDs as not legible). (Methods)
- Method: hitting off a batting tee, placed and set to each batter's preferred height and position to hit a line drive "up the middle"; the three best line drives per batter were averaged; results are the mean +/- SD across the 7. Six cameras at 200 Hz (Motion Analysis, Expert Vision), 23 reflective markers (body, bat handle just above the hands, bat barrel end, ball); two force plates (one per foot) at 1000 Hz; data low-pass filtered with a 4th-order zero-phase Butterworth at 13.3 Hz. (Methods, Results) The note does not say whether the filter ran through bat-ball contact; that it did (as [4]'s did) is an inference from a filter applied to the whole recorded swing, not something the paper states (body-motion 4.8).
- No pitcher, so no recognition, reaction or adjustment to a pitch (the authors chose the tee to remove those, Discussion). Fortenbaugh (source 4) later found live-pitch coil and timing differ from tee studies.
- Trust: medium. Good for the order of segment peaks, peak velocities and ground forces of a tee swing by pros. Limits: 7 subjects, tee not pitch, tables partly lost, most events are timed only as "seconds before ball contact" and some have no time at all (foot off and foot down have no time stated), and no raw SDs for several rows. The 13.3 Hz filter on 200 Hz data is low for a bat tip (peak bat speeds are probably smoothed).

## Frames and definitions (Parameter Description)
- Global frame: +X from home plate toward the pitching rubber, parallel to the batter's box surface; looking along +X, +Z is up and +Y is to the left. (So +X = toward the pitcher, the same as the sim's +Y; the paper's Y is to the batter's left looking at the pitcher.)
- Left foot is the front (lead) foot, right foot is back, because all subjects hit right-handed. Clockwise rotation (viewed as in Figure 2C, around the trunk axis) is the coil; counterclockwise is the swing direction.
- Segment rotation is measured about the trunk axis (mid-hips to mid-shoulders) against global X. Hips = vector right hip to left hip; shoulders = vector right shoulder to left shoulder; arms = vector mid-shoulders to mid-wrists. Positive degrees = clockwise coil (the hip, shoulder and arm values at foot off are all positive in the coil direction).
- Trunk orientation: angle of the trunk vector to global Z, in the median plane (+ flexion, - extension) and the frontal plane (+ right, - left).
- Knee and elbow angles: absolute angle between the proximal and distal segments; full anatomical extension = 0 deg, full flexion = 180 deg. So a straight leg is 0 and a bent knee is a larger number (the same scale as Fortenbaugh).
- Bat lag: absolute angle between the bat vector (handle to barrel) and the arm vector (mid-shoulders to mid-wrists); 180 deg means the bat is in line with the arms (fully extended).
- Stride length: the distance between the right toe and the left toe at foot down, in cm and as a percentage of hip width (right hip centre to left hip centre). It is not relative to height. Stride direction: angle of the vector from right toe to left toe against global X (+ closed, - open). Lead foot position: angle of the vector from left ankle to left forefoot against global X (+ closed, - open).
- Centre of pressure (COP, the two feet weighted by vertical force) minus centre of mass (COM, from Contini segment data) along X: negative means COP behind COM.
- Events: foot off = the instant the left (front) foot leaves the ground and the stride starts; foot down = the instant the left foot is in full contact, ending the stride and starting closed-chain energy transfer (heel contact); ball contact = bat meets ball. The swing as defined here runs from foot off to ball contact; follow-through is not measured.

## Coil and weight shift before foot off (Biomechanical Description)
- The swing starts with a weight shift toward the right (back) leg while the upper body rotates clockwise about the trunk axis, led by the arms and shoulders and closely followed by the hips. This is the coil. Then the front leg lifts (foot off).
- At foot off: right foot total force 102% BW, with 146 N of shear in -X (back, away from the pitcher) and 26 N in +Y; right knee 32 deg; COP 20 cm behind COM; arms rotated to 150 deg, shoulders 30 deg, hips 18 deg (all clockwise, from the paper's reference); trunk flexed 21 deg and tilted 6 deg to the left.
- Hip counter-direction timing: hips reach their maximum coil of 28 deg at 0.350 s before ball contact and then begin turning counterclockwise (toward the pitcher). Shoulders keep coiling to a maximum of 52 deg at 0.265 s before contact, then follow. The arms keep coiling until foot contact, reaching a maximum of 185 deg, then begin counterclockwise. (Foot off and foot down have no stated time before contact. Figure 3 lists the order: foot off, max hip rotation, max shoulder rotation, max arm rotation, foot down.) The discussion stresses the order of first counterclockwise movement: hips first, then shoulders, then arms.
- Weight shift at coil is linear too: the COP behind the COM plus the rear foot's backward shear drives the hitter toward the ball. The authors say COM can be 20 cm ahead of COP (Discussion).

## Stride and foot down (Foot Contact; Table 4)
- Stride length 85 cm (SD 12), or 380% of hip width (SD 75). That is toe-to-toe distance of the two feet at foot down, so it includes the starting spacing of the stance and is not the distance the lead foot travelled. Hip width implied by the two figures is about 22 cm (my division, 85/3.8; not stated by the paper).
- Stride direction 12 deg closed (SD 3). Lead foot angle 67 deg closed as it begins to contact the ground; 61 deg closed (SD 11) at foot down (Table 4). Lead foot toes point strongly toward the plate side.
- At foot down: left foot total force 123% BW (1007 N, SD 472 N; SD 63% BW), with 292 N shear in +X (toward the pitcher, i.e. braking) and 280 N in -Y; right foot has dropped to 58% BW with 80 N in -X and 184 N in +Y; COP has jumped to 20 cm ahead of COM. Left foot Z value and the rest of Table 1's cells: not legible. (The abstract's headline "123% of body weight" is the lead foot at foot down, not at contact.)
- From foot down the movement is closed chain. The authors say a hitter can stress rotation (COP and COM both between the feet, both feet shearing, large hip force couple) or stress linear movement (COP stays at the lead foot and the COM moves over the front leg, only the lead foot shears). They report large SDs in shear forces and COP-COM, so hitters differ here (Discussion).

## Segment peaks and timing (Foot Contact, Ball Contact; Table 2, Figure 3)
All times are before ball contact (BC = 0).
- Hips max rotational velocity 714 deg/s (SD 76) at -75 ms; the left knee extends and pushes the left hip back while the right leg pushes the right hip forward, a couple that accelerates the hips about the trunk.
- Shoulders max rotational velocity 937 deg/s (SD 102) at -65 ms. Arms max rotational velocity 1160 deg/s (SD 96) at -65 ms (the text gives one time, 0.065 s, for both).
- Bat linear velocity components: the forward-away-from-body component (negative Y, anterior) peaks at 19 m/s at -40 ms; the downward (negative Z) component peaks at 16 m/s (Figure 3 event 8 groups the maximum Y and Z components). Afterwards the bat's motion is dominated by +X, toward the ball, and Y and Z fall. (The text gives -40 ms for the Y peak only; the Z peak time is not stated separately.)
- Bat lag max rotational velocity (uncocking) 1588 deg/s (SD 162) at -20 ms.
- Max bat linear velocity 31 m/s (SD printed as "3 1 2", not legible) and max right (back) elbow extension velocity 948 deg/s, both at -15 ms. Max left (front) elbow extension velocity occurs after ball contact (Figure 3 event 13, no time given).
- Proximal-to-distal order (their Figure 3, with times where given): foot off, hip max coil (-350), shoulder max coil (-265), arm max coil (at foot down), hip max velocity (-75), shoulder and arm max velocity (-65), bat Y/Z linear (-40), bat lag velocity (-20), bat linear max and right elbow max (-15), X component of bat max, ball contact, left elbow max. Hip-to-shoulder gap of peak velocity is 10 ms, hip to bat about 60 ms. Shoulder and arm segment peaks coincide in the text.
- The authors say each segment should reach its peak only after the one before it; if a smaller segment peaks before the base segment, it loses the momentum.

## At ball contact (Ball Contact)
- Bat linear speed has fallen slightly to 29 m/s, mostly directed in +X toward the ball.
- Left (front) leg is a block: knee flexed 15 deg (0 = straight), total force 84% BW (709 N, SD 270; 84% SD 32), shear +X 153 N (SD 146) and +Y 28 N (SD 65) as read from the table.
- Right (back) leg is support only: knee flexed 45 deg, total force 16% BW.
- Trunk moved back through 30 deg to 9 deg of extension (from 21 deg flexion at foot off) and through 26 deg to the right to 20 deg of right lateral flexion (from 6 deg left). The authors describe the trunk as an extension of the front leg's block.
- Hip, shoulder, arm and bat lag angles at foot down and contact, elbow angles, trunk and COP-COM at the three events: Table 3 mostly not legible (values at foot off in the narrative are above). Bat lag angle at contact: not legible.

## Forces (Table 1)
- Lead foot: 123% BW at foot down, 84% BW at contact (narrative). Back foot: 102% BW at foot off, 58% at foot down, 16% at contact (narrative).
- Maximum vertical (Z) forces, peak lead force time, and force-time curves: not given in the text. The paper gives no peak lead force beyond the values at the three events.
- Ground reaction shear (back foot pushing in -X, lead foot braking in +X) is credited with driving the linear and rotational motion; lead and back foot shears form a force couple on the hips.

## Stance, hands, head, follow-through
- Stance setup (foot spacing, hand position, bat angle at setup), head orientation: not covered. Tee position was the batter's own choice.
- Hands: modelled only as the arm segment and the wrist-to-bat markers; no hand path reported.
- Follow-through: measured not at all; the authors point to Shaffer et al., Garhammer and DeRenne and say the body slows itself and the bat by eccentric contraction through the large muscle groups.
- Whole-swing duration from foot off: not given (see "Not covered").

## Disagreements with common coaching or expectation
- The paper says the swing begins with a hip, shoulder and arm coil and a rear-foot weight shift, and that the hitter should avoid excess clockwise coil, which can disrupt sequencing. (Discussion)
- Rotation versus linear: the paper does not choose; it says hitters differ in whether COP and COM stay between the feet (rotational style) or move over the front leg (linear style). (Discussion)
- The tee setting removes timing against a pitch; the numbers are an upper bound on how organised a swing can be without a pitcher to read.

## Quotes
- "Proper timing facilitates successively higher rotational velocities." (Discussion)
