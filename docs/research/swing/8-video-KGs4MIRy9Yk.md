## Phases (the coach's own definitions)
Three phases: "load, launch, and then connection" (2:38). "The swing is basically over at this point" after those three (2:40). Stride, foot plant, rotation sequence, contact and follow-through are not covered.
- **Load** (0:49-1:30): first and "most important" move is loading into the back hip / rear leg. Cue: bend at the waist and hinge, chest comes down over the back knee (1:03-1:13). From the back view the hands/knob go "down to the catcher" (1:15-1:22). Test: if correct you can balance and hold the position (1:24-1:30).
- **Launch position** ("hitting position", 1:32-2:06): says photos of top hitters at "launch position" show it (1:34-1:44, not verified here). Feet "as wide as the bat is" (1:46); hands over the back foot (1:50); knob pointed toward the catcher (1:55). Felt stretch under the lead arm and tension/load still in the hip (1:57-2:06).
- **Connection** (2:06-2:25): back side activates and connects; looks like "head, hand, shoulder, hip, knee, all in a line, connected" (2:19-2:25). Frame at 140 s shows a tilted body, head over the hands, bat across the lead shoulder (frame at 125 s: stance wide, hands at hip height, bat vertical).

## Stance and setup

- Frame 92 s ("hitting position"): feet about shoulder-plus wide, slight knee bend, hands near chest/rear shoulder, bat tilted back. Width rule: feet = bat length (not given in inches/cm).

## Disagreement with common expectation
- Describes the "launch position" as a static, balanced, hold-able position with weight over the back leg and hands over the back foot, with no stride mentioned. This is a load-centred model, not a stride/plant-centred one. Also treats a narrower-than-too-wide base as safer for load control. Both are one coach's opinion.
