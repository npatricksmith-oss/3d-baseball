# Source 2: OnBase University "Level 1 Hitting" manual (sportngin PDF)

## What it is
- Title: "Level 1 Hitting" coaching/instructor manual, OnBase University (OnBaseU), Version 3.0, (c) 2016/2017 (PDF created 2020-05-15), 114 pages. URL: https://cdn4.sportngin.com/attachments/document/1b76-2577303/ViewAttachment.pdf (fetched OK; local copy sim/target/research-dl/2/s.pdf, text s.txt).
- Authors/instructors: Dr. Greg Rose (TPI co-founder), Steve Johnson, Don Slaught (ex-MLB catcher, RightView Pro); PT/CSCS contributors.
- Method: a coaching and 2D-video-diagnosis manual built on "3D motion capture" kinematic-sequence research. It does NOT report subjects, sample size, level or measurement protocol (not given). Graphs (pp. 84-93) have no axis labels or numbers. It is a practitioner manual: trust the phase definitions and qualitative sequence (consistent with other sources); treat the few numbers as coaching rules of thumb, not measurements.
- Content covers: 11 key positions, "Big 13" faults (each with a video test), kinematic sequence, physical screening (injury/mobility tests), motor learning. Stance widths, angles, weights, bat speeds, peak angular velocities: not given.

## Phases (p. 8, own definitions)
Preparation: 1 Stance (start before any pitcher movement); 2 Negative Move (any move away from the pitcher, to the furthest point back); 3 Positive Move (move toward pitcher, ends at Toe Touch/Ready Position; hands separate from body here); 4 Toe Touch/Ready Position (where hitter can efficiently start the swing; some put the foot down early then make a positive move to Ready); 5 Heel Plant (front heel firmly planted). Swing: 6 First Move (first 3-4 in of hands moving forward); 7 Connection (hands pass in front of back shoulder); 8 Bat Lag (barrel enters strike zone); 9 Contact (closest frame); 10 Extension (both arms fully extended); 11 Finish (bat stops rotating around the body).
Timing (p. 24): "average MLB hitter" takes ten to twelve 60ths of a second to swing from Toe Touch/Ready to Contact (about 167-200 ms; 10/60 = 0.167 s, 12/60 = 0.200 s). Method for that figure not given.

## Stance and setup
- Not covered numerically. Stance is the starting position; hands/bat angle, foot width, weight split: not given.
- Posture (p. 12): from Negative Move to Ready the hitter has "Attacking Posture": slight forward lean of upper body toward pitcher; line from inside (near) eye to belt buckle slants down toward back foot; head (eyes/nose/chin) slightly ahead of belt buckle.
- Needs enough bend in knees and waist to leave room for the back elbow to pass the hips (p. 14, "space"); knee flex amount varies widely by player.
- Mobility benchmarks (screening): seated trunk rotation should pass 45 deg; typical 45-60 deg each way (p. 58). Pelvis rotation about one leg should pass 45 deg.

## Load, negative move, stride (positive move), foot plant
- Negative Move = load: weight into back foot, rotation into the back hip, no lateral sway: back knee must not move outside a vertical line through the middle of the back foot (p. 10; "if it moves one inch past, it is a sway"). Timing from stance to toe touch should be consistent so the only variable is pitch speed.
- Positive Move: linear weight shift from back leg to front leg; hands separate from the nose, i.e. hand-to-nose distance must grow from end of negative move to toe touch (stretch-shorten effect) (p. 16). Hands live and independent.
- Toe Touch/Ready: keep pressure between knees and feet; back heel stays down; front kneecap points inward, not out over the front foot (p. 18). Hitter must keep the ability to wait/adjust to off-speed ("store energy"). Trail elbow should be even with or in front of mid-hands, not sticking out behind (flying elbow, p. 26). Top hand must not be outside bottom hand (p. 28); bat keeps lag (hands lead bat). Hands even or inside the front elbow (closer to body) from Ready through about the first third of the swing (3-4 sixtieths of a second) (p. 24).
- Heel plant: posture reverses to "Backward Posture" (slight backward lean of upper body, belt buckle slightly ahead of inside eye) so the head stops and linear move converts to rotation (p. 12). Hanging back = heel behind a vertical line through outside of back foot, or weight still on back foot at contact (p. 20). Stride length, direction, speed, separation angle in degrees: not given.
- Front side after heel plant (p. 30): front knee shows maximum flex after the First Move and should not flex more after heel plant. Front hip must not slide toward the pitcher past a vertical line at the front hip drawn at end of First Move. "The best of the best will actually reduce the front knee flex (knee straighter) going into contact and the hip will get farther away from the line."

## Rotation and swing
- Sequence (pp. 83-85): pelvis starts first, thorax second, lead arm third, bat last; then deceleration in the same order: pelvis slows first, thorax second, lead arm third, bat last. Each segment peaks higher than the previous (pelvis < thorax < arm < bat); each slows as the next accelerates ("the torso grabs the pelvis..." sprinter analogy). The deceleration sequence is called the most useful predictor of efficiency; the transition (start) sequence is the other. Claim: all great hitters share this sequence despite different styles (p. 83); sequence = repeatability, energy transfer/deceleration = power.
- Graph p. 84 (examples of efficient hitting; read from image): four curves, pelvis (red) peaks smallest and earliest, thorax (green) next, lead arm (blue) next, barrel (yellow/brown) peaks highest by far and last, all in one narrow burst. Axes unlabelled; no values.
- Inefficient example (p. 87): pelvis fires much earlier than others; bat fires third and arm last (out of order).
- Back elbow initiates the arm swing and leads the hips to rotate; elbow then catches up and passes the back hip. At Connection hands, back elbow and the "stripe of the pants" (back hip) line up; elbow keeps going past the hip through extension (p. 22). Gate swing = elbow pinned at hip, body turns as one unit.
- Whip: both hands pull (back elbow leads), not push; hands in front of back elbow at connection = push (p. 34).
- Bat path: bat on plane of pitch as early and long as possible; sweet spot within 2-3 in below the ball line for about 3-4 ft ("big zone"). From bat lag through extension front elbow higher than hands, hands higher than barrel (p. 32). Bat should not go across or down through the ball line. On plane a little late = oppo, a little early = pull. Lag: hands lead the bat; loss of bat angle ("dumping") past 90 deg angle early is a fault (p. 9). Attack angle, bat speed, peak deg/s: not given.
- Lead arm/back arm roles beyond the above: not given. Head: stops at heel plant (head stops, rotation around fixed spine/head) (p. 12).

## Contact
- Only qualitative: weight transferred to front side, front side firm, back elbow past hip, bat on plane. Body position at contact, lead knee angle, extension angle, bat speed: not given. Mid-hands direction vs plate/body: pelvis should not move toward the plate (no daylight between vertical line behind pelvis and the pelvis from start of positive move to contact on a down-the-line view, p. 14).

## Follow-through and finish
- Extension: both arms fully extended after contact. Finish: bat stops rotating around body. Wrist roll, hands, balance, leaving box: not covered.

## Disagreements with common coaching / notable points
- Warns against cues "take your hands/knob to the ball", "keep hands inside the ball": produce push (p. 34).
- Says styles can differ widely (Bagwell, Youkilis) as long as the kinematic sequence is efficient; front knee flex amount is irrelevant as long as it does not increase after heel plant.
- Wants a slight backward upper-body lean at heel plant and forward ("attacking") lean before it: a posture reversal, not a constant spine angle.
- Hands should go away from the body (separate from nose) during the stride, rather than staying fixed.
- Front hip should not drift; best hitters straighten the front knee into contact.

## Quotes
- "The sequence of energy initiation for efficient hitters is: lower body starts, thorax moves next, lead arm goes third and the bat starts last." (p. 83)
- "Energy transfer or deceleration is the key to power." (p. 83)
