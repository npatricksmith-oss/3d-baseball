## Phases (his own definitions, 17:30-19:30, slides 1085/1160 s)
- Load: starts when centre of mass (COM) starts moving back; ends at rearmost COM point, when it begins to move forward.
- Stride: COM forward move until front heel strike (heel hits ground).
- Swing: front foot plant (heel strike) to contact.
- Timings in ms before contact: not given.

## Stance and setup
- Not covered as static numbers (no foot width/angle, knee/hip flexion, bat angle, hand position). Only that a few hitters preset weight over the back side (18:30-19:00).

## Load (19:30-29:00)
- Purpose: gather energy unilaterally on the back leg, start timing, get into an athletic position (20:00-20:30). Force-plate view: hitting is largely unilateral; during load all weight on back leg, after front heel down most weight shifts to the front and most hitters fully unweight the back leg (20:00).
- COM sways back before the front leg is lifted; "overwhelming majority" do. Goldschmidt about "three four inch" back; Harper "probably a foot, maybe even more" (22:00-22:30). Reason: lifting the front leg from 50/50 makes you fall forward. Flaw: COM too far forward when leg lifts, crash forward.
- Pelvis and torso coil together with minimal separation. End of load, best hitters average: torso coil ~8 deg, separation ~5 deg (slide 1440 s). Torso coil rises to ~20 deg average max later in stride (23:30-24:00). Example Dom: torso coil 26 deg (slide shows 26.6), pelvis coil 16.5, separation 10, called too much (24:00). Over-coiling is common in easy practice (flips, machine).
- Balance and starting early: good hitters are balanced and early, "almost can't start too early" (27:30-28:00).
- Drills mentioned: hook drill (front foot hooked behind back foot), step-back (28:30-29:30).

## Stride (30:00-40:00)
- Purpose: timing, adjustability, direction, not power creation (30:00). Cabrera clip: same load for high fastball and slow breaking ball; the stride then differs (hands higher / more forward bend).
- Stride is mostly linear; blend to rotation starts at foot plant when pelvis begins to turn (31:00).
- At heel strike: pelvis already rotating (open) into foot plant while torso resists (31:30-32:30). Dom: pelvis 16-17 deg at plant then opens ~10-11 deg more at heel strike; torso 41 deg; separation 24.4 deg (33:00-36:30).
- Separation (hip-shoulder, "X factor") at heel strike: average 18 deg +/-5 (slide 2110 s). Looser movers over 25-30. Average max separation in the swing for big leaguers: about 22 deg (35:00-36:00), "not as much as you might think"; more is not simply better.
- Flaws: pelvis lands closed with no separation (forces pelvis and torso to turn together or an arm sweep and bat pronation); or torso comes with pelvis (flies open) giving out-and-around path (33:30-35:00).
- Torso posture at landing: forward bend about 25 deg on average; flaw 32-35 deg and more; Dom 29.1 deg (36:30-38:30; slide 2230 s). Argument: landing with high-pitch posture (less forward bend) is easier because adding side bend works downward more easily than coming up.
- Head/COM: stop moving forward at foot plant (see swing).

## Swing (40:00-61:00)
- Purpose: accelerate bat efficiently to the ball. Best hitters have many "lanes" (adjust to pitch height/location) (40:30).
- Kinematic sequence: pelvis, then torso, then lead arm, then hands/bat; energy passes up the chain (17:00). Lead arm should be faster than torso. Peak rotational speeds for big-league targets (46:00): pelvis at least "mid 600s" deg/s (600 deg/s = 10.5 rad/s); torso "up to 1100 1200" deg/s (19.2-20.9 rad/s); speed gain pelvis to torso about 1.6; torso to lead arm at least about 1.2-1.3 (slide 2580 s says shoot for 1.3). Spoken as "one six one six" and "one two one three"; I read these as 1.6 and 1.2-1.3. Dom example: pelvis 761, shoulders 1250, front arm 1150 deg/s (44:30-45:30). Lead-arm and hand peak times from the slide graph (time axis -0.20 to 0 s before contact): lead-arm angular velocity peaks near -0.07 to -0.05 s (about 1200 deg/s), hand peaks near -0.05 s (about 2000+ deg/s) (read from a small graph at 2580 s; approximate, not stated in speech).
- Connection to disconnection: early in the swing phase torso, arm, hand and bat turn as one unit; then the torso slows (brakes, earlier for deeper/oppo contact, later for out-front contact) and "catapults" hands and barrel (41:30-42:30). Flaws: push pattern (hands fire early, arm faster than torso, chest press) and drag pattern (arm slower than torso, barrel drops; common in young or weak hitters). Pelvis should start first, separate from torso (47:00).
- Posture in swing: forward bend at heel strike is replaced by side bend at impact; swing = tilted rotation (48:30-49:30). Dom 29 deg forward bend at heel strike, 28 deg side bend at impact. Best hitters about -5 deg torso bend (leaned back) typical in an A-swing; best bat-to-ball hitters can instead keep forward bend to reach out front (69:30-70:30). Flaws: early extension (standing up, axis moves), and dropping under (excess side bend).
- Head: once front foot is down the move becomes rotational, head should be still and barrel works around it; continued forward head drift drags the barrel (52:00-53:30). Wrong posture at landing (too upright) forces head dive (67:30-68:30).
- Bat path: vertical attack angle of big leaguers about 8-10 deg on average, power hitters 14-15 with more swing and miss; incoming pitch plane typically 7-8 deg, rising-type fastballs 5-6 deg (54:30-55:30). Horizontal attack angle: best hitters keep direction through the incoming horizontal pitch plane through contact; weaker hitters pull the barrel around early (out-to-in at contact) (56:00). Flaws: down-and-across path. Swinging on plane widens timing margin and gives a flush collision.
- Contact location: opposite-field contact needs more in-to-out, with torso braked earlier (60:00); torso turn at contact varies by field. Elbow flexion varied by pitch location: bent lead arm for inside pitches, extended for out-front (73:00-74:30).
- Lead leg / rear leg detail in swing phase: not covered beyond: weight on front leg after heel strike.

## Disagreements with common coaching
- Hitting is largely unilateral (back leg, then front leg), not "50/50".
- Stride is for timing and adjustability, not power.
- More hip-shoulder separation is not better; ~22 deg max for big leaguers.
- Lateral COM sway back is normal and needed, not a flaw.
- Pelvis opens slightly at heel strike while torso resists; flaws are closed pelvis and torso opening with pelvis.
- No single ideal swing; the best hitters vary swing by pitch.
- Dry swings at slow speed tend to make hitters too upright.
