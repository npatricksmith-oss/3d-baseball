# A real hitter's swing on the batter's figure (2026-11-27, worker swing-flow; PLAN D45)

The owner, after watching the force-driven body's practised swing: "model the swing based off of real batters. im speaking about the overall flow of the swing not forces and minutiae yet"; "just watch a goddam real batters swing and mimic it". This note is what was done: one recorded swing of one real hitter, put on our batter's figure and drawn in the Unreal viewer, the whole motion from his stance to the end of what was recorded of his finish. No forces, no search, no score, no gate; nothing in a game changes. How to open it: [player-visuals §14.1](../../player-visuals.md#141-a-real-hitters-recorded-swing-plan-d45).

What a game could ship instead: its own capture of a hitter (a session in a mocap lab, or markerless video of a hitter the studio has signed), licensed data, or a motion the force-driven body produces that only looks like this one. Until the owner rules on Q68, this swing is a reference to look at, nothing more.

## 1. The swing chosen

**OpenBiomechanics swing 478_5** (`c3d/000137/000137_000478_69_214_R_014_1015.c3d`): a right-handed **minor-league (MiLB)** hitter, 5 ft 9 in, 214 lb, 34-in 31-oz bat; **72.5 mph** fastest bat speed (the dataset's own figure; its 95th percentile is about 73), **101.5 mph exit velocity** (the second hardest ball of the 677), **18° launch, 17° to the pull side**, pitch in zone 8 (low, middle), a 61 mph machine pitch coming down 5.4°.

Why this one, of the 98 hitters: right-handed (no mirroring needed); high level (8 MiLB and 4 independent-league hitters are the best in the set); among the fastest bats and the hardest-hit balls; an ordinary line drive up the middle-left, not a mishit; no gaps in any of its 55 markers; and the **longest recording of the finish** of the swings that qualify (500 ms after contact; most stop at 250–350 ms), with 1.6 s before contact so the stance, the leg lift and the stride are all there. He is a leg-lift hitter: the lead knee comes up and in, the foot hangs 17 cm off the ground at the top (−620 ms), the stride lands at −178 ms (10 % of his weight on it) and takes all of it at −131 ms. Not averaged with anyone: an average is nobody's swing.

Other candidates looked at: 89_2 and 89_4 (6 ft 2 in MiLB, 73 mph bat, 440 ms of finish, but met deep over the plate, 13–16 in from its back tip); 478_1 and 478_3 (same hitter, finish cut at 300 ms); 448_5 (6 ft 0 in, exactly the figure's height, but only 275 ms of finish).

## 2. How it was mapped

`analysis/research/body/real_swing.py` (its docstring has the whole mapping), from the C3D's 55 markers at 360 Hz and the dataset's own joint centres (Visual3D's, in `landmarks.csv`), its events and HitTrax's figures:

1. **Frame.** The lab's +x (toward the pitcher) is the sim's +Y, its +y (toward the right-hander's box) the sim's −X, +z up in both: a turn, no mirror.
2. **Scale.** One uniform scale about the floor, s = 1.035: the body model's leg (de Leva at 1.84 m, thigh + shank 0.905 m) over his (0.875 m), so the figure is as long in the leg as the force-driven batter; he is 1.81 m tall on the figure. A uniform scale keeps every point that was still, still: **his feet stay where they were**. The bat is not scaled: it is his 34-in bat, as the figure's is.
3. **Placement.** One translation in x and y puts the sweet spot at contact where HitTrax saw the ball met: 1.5 in toward his side of the plate's middle, 33.4 in in front of the plate's back tip (0.42 m in front of its front edge). Heights are the lab's. HitTrax's own height for the impact, 26.3 in, is 1.8 cm below the motion capture's sweet spot, scaled: the two instruments agree.
4. **The figure has his proportions, not the model's segment lengths.** The job asked for "our figure's segment lengths". The body model's (the force-driven batter's) cannot hold his swing: its upper arm is 5 cm shorter than his (scaled) and its shoulders 9 cm wider apart, and with them his hands cannot be put on his bat by up to **11 cm** after contact (the arms out of reach). Holding his own lengths fixed instead moved his lead knee up to **7 cm** off his recorded knee, because markers ride on skin: his lead thigh reads 0.44 m during the leg lift and 0.49 m after contact. So **every joint the figure has is his own joint centre at that sample**, scaled and placed, and each segment is drawn at the length those give (the viewer stretches each piece to it, as it always has): hips (Harrington 2007's regression from his four pelvis markers; the dataset's `left_hip`/`right_hip` are 0.31 m apart, pelvis landmarks and not hip centres), knees, ankles, elbows, wrists, hands. Two joints are not his own, because the viewer's pieces need them so:
   - **the lumbar joint** (he has no marker there): up his pelvis's own axis (fixed in the pelvis frame of its four markers, aimed at his shoulders' midpoint at the first sample) at the model's share of his hips-to-shoulders distance, 0.142 m;
   - **the shoulders**: his shoulders' midpoint and his width, but the line between them squared to the trunk's axis, because the chest piece can be stretched and not sheared. They move **22 mm at most** (median 4 mm), mostly up and down, at −75 ms.
5. **Turns.** Each limb's turn about its own axis comes from the joint's two markers (the knee's, the ankle's, the elbow's; the forearm toward the thumb from the wrist's two). The **feet**: heading and pitch from his heel and toe markers, no roll (a roll from the ankle's centre against the heel-to-toe line came out 40° off in the stance, and one from the ankle's two markers is the shin's sideways lean, which the flat foot does not share); the heel's sole under the heel marker by its flat height, the ball of the foot where the viewer's shoe has it for his ankle's height (an Unreal piece cannot be sheared). The **head**: its four markers' frame, set right by the headband's tilt and turn on his head, read from his standing calibration trial (17° up, 7° round; Plug-in Gait's head offset does the same). In the stance his face is level and turned 59° from the plate toward the pitcher; at −40 ms he looks 35° down at the ball.
6. **The ball.** The machine pitch at HitTrax's 61.2 mph and 5.4° down, under gravity, from the machine 40 ft out (released 414 ms before contact), arriving with its centre touching the barrel's face at the sweet spot at his contact instant. After contact the batted ball leaves at HitTrax's 101.5 mph, 18° up and 17° to the pull side, under gravity. Both for the eye only.

The viewer took four small changes for it (`client/Source/BaseballClient/BbBodySwing.*`): a file may say `kind recorded` (the screen says recording, not model; `-BbBodySwingShots=<ms>` runs to the file's last sample), `title`, `ball_after_contact shown`, and a `head` body (the head and helmet then turn on it about the neck; without one they ride on the trunk as before). The force-driven swing's files draw as before.

**Checks.** The viewer's own check (`-BbBodySwingCheck`, every one of the 756 samples drawn and read back): every joint within **3.7 mm** of the file's (the shoulders as the chest piece carries them; all others under 1 mm), every segment's turn within **0.6°** (the feet; all others under 0.1°), handedness right: PASS. Its planted faults all fail on this file as they must (exit 1): `nostretch` by 82.6 mm, `mirror` on handedness and 2.9 m, `twist` by 90.0°. The force-driven swing's file (rung 4) still passes with its old figures (shoulders 4.2 mm, feet 1.54°). `analysis/research/body/test_real_swing.py` (10 tests; run with `uv run --with numpy --with c3d python3 -m unittest analysis/research/body/test_real_swing.py`; it skips, saying why, without numpy, the C3D reader or the data, which is everywhere but the machine that fetched them) checks the file against what the exporter did not make: each knee, ankle, elbow and wrist within 10 mm of the middle of the two skin markers either side of it in the raw C3D (read in the test, with the lab-to-sim turn written out again from the dataset's README); the knob at the knob marker and the tip toward the barrel markers; the shoulders within 25 mm of the dataset's; the legs' median length the model's within 0.5 %; the sweet spot's height at contact within 4 cm of HitTrax's (it is 1.8 cm); the ball touching the barrel and coming from the pitcher; the batter in the right-hander's box facing the plate with his lead foot toward the pitcher; **every sole point within 1.5 cm in the stance and the lead heel within 3 cm from 30 ms after foot plant to +300 ms** (no skating); both hands within 7 cm of the bat's axis and on the handle at every sample. **The hips** (review P2-2) within 25 mm of a second published regression, Bell 1990, built in the test from the raw pelvis markers (12.5 mm: the exporter uses Harrington 2007), and carried by the pelvis (each moves under 10 mm in the raw markers' own frame); **the head**: the file's face line at a fixed angle (within 0.1°) to its four raw markers' front-to-back line, and the offset checked against what it was not built from: level within 5° and turned 30–90° toward the pitcher in the stance, and within 20° of the line from his head to where the ball is met from 50 ms before contact (10.4° at contact). **The feet against his raw heel and toe markers** (review P2-3): the shoe's long axis within 1° of his heel-to-toe line at every sample and the ball of the foot within 2 cm of it across the foot; the heel's sole under his raw heel marker (1 cm in the stance, 2.5 cm while the lead foot is planted); the drawn soles moving no more than his raw markers plus 5 mm. Planting any of these faults (the hips 3 cm out; the head without its offset; the soles 2 cm off; the feet turned 5°) fails these tests.

**Not checked independently:** the lumbar joint (he has no marker there; its place is the model's share of his hips-to-shoulders line); the shoulders only against the dataset's own shoulder centres (25 mm), not against raw markers; the head offset's premise that he stood looking straight ahead and level in his calibration trial (the checks above make a wrong premise visible only if it is larger than their margins); the feet's roll (set to none) and the ball of the foot (where the viewer's shoe has it, not a marker); the bat's sweet spot (the dataset's).

## 3. What it looks like, position by position

Looked at every 10 ms from −1590 to +500 ms (210 instants), each from the pitcher's side, the open side and above (630 pictures), each set beside his own stick figure drawn from his markers through the same three cameras (`real_swing.py --draw`). Not committed (the licence): the pictures are in `sim/target/codex-probes/real-swing/shots-v3/`, the stick figures in `.../real-swing/stick/`, and the 30 sheets that put them side by side, one row an instant, in `.../real-swing/full-v3/sheet_01.png` to `sheet_30.png`. Beside them: MLB slow-motion video (Mike Trout from the open side and from behind, Mookie Betts from the pitcher's side; `sim/target/codex-probes/mlb/INDEX.md` and its montages; their timings rest on a guessed slow-motion factor, ±40 %).

**The figure does what he did.** At every instant the figure's pose is his stick figure's, from all three sides; nothing was seen where they part (no mirrored side, no limb turned the wrong way, no foot sliding, no hand off the bat, no bat through the body: the barrel stays at least 21 cm from the trunk's axis and 23 cm from the head's centre). In plain baseball words:

- **Stance (−1590 to −900 ms).** Square to the plate, feet a little wider than his shoulders, knees and hips bent, chest over his thighs. Hands by the back shoulder at its height, the bat tipped back over that shoulder at about 45°. Head level, turned toward the pitcher. Small waggles of the bat and a slight rock back. Trout and Betts set up the same way: hands by the rear ear, bat tipped back; Betts much wider and lower.
- **Load and leg lift (−900 to −600 ms).** The lead heel comes up first (−880 ms), then the lead knee lifts and turns in toward the back knee, and the foot hangs about 17 cm off the dirt at the top (−620 ms). The hands go back and up a little and the bat flattens toward the catcher; his weight stays on the back leg. A leg-lift hitter, like Trout's kick, which is higher and held longer.
- **Stride and foot down (−600 to −130 ms).** The lead foot goes out toward the pitcher, landing slightly closed: its toe first touches at −178 ms and it takes all his weight at −131 ms. The hands stay back by the rear shoulder through the landing and the bat stays up. The pitch is in the air from −414 ms.
- **Hips, chest, hands (−130 to −20 ms).** After the foot is down the hips open first, then the chest. The hands drop to the back shoulder's height and then the belt. The bat tips back toward the catcher (−60 ms), lies flat behind his hands at the belt in the slot (−40 ms), and the barrel comes around under the hands (−20 ms). The rear heel comes up and the rear foot turns onto its toe. The lead leg firms up and straightens.
- **Contact (0 ms).** The pitch is low (zone 8), so the barrel is steeply down at contact, below the hands, with the ball on its face 0.42 m in front of the plate. The lead leg is straight, the back knee bent toward the plate, the rear foot on its toe, and his head down on the ball (35° down). Both arms are still bent, as the dataset's hitters are on average (note 6).
- **Extension (0 to +100 ms).** The arms straighten out toward the pitcher along the ball's line. The barrel rises past the level and the batted ball leaves at 18° and 101.5 mph. The rear foot drags about 15 cm toward the pitcher on its toe, a common back-foot drag.
- **Finish (+100 to +500 ms).** The bat goes up over the lead shoulder and wraps behind his head. The hands finish by the head with both still on the bat, his chest past facing the pitcher and the shoulders turned 180° from the stance. The lead toe swings open on its heel; at about +400 ms the lead foot comes up and steps back as he comes out of the swing. The recording ends at +500 ms with the bat across his upper back. Trout's and Betts's finishes are the same high wrap; Trout's hands end higher.

**What still looks off, or differs from a major leaguer** (none of it a fault of the drawing):

- **He meets the ball far out in front:** 0.42 m in front of the plate, where MLB's average is 0.07 m (Savant, [note 11](11-stance-depth.md)). So he stands about 0.35 m further up in the box than the MLB average, though his distance off the plate (0.90 m from its middle to his hips) is MLB's (0.92 m). A 61-mph machine pitch lets a hitter meet it out there; against a 95-mph fastball he would not.
- **The timing is a 61-mph machine pitch's.** Foot down to contact is 178 ms, inside the 150–300 ms the MLB clips suggest; the whole swing from the leg lift's top to contact is 620 ms. A game would compress it for faster pitches, which this recording cannot show.
- **The figure's body is the mannequin's**, stretched to his segment lengths: a slim 6 ft 0 in body moving like a stocky 5 ft 9 in, 214 lb hitter (scaled to 1.81 m). The hands are rounded blobs with no fingers, so the grip and the top hand's roll do not show. The neck does not turn: the head turns on its own on top of the trunk.
- **The bat speed on screen** at the contact sample reads 60.8 mph, because the impact has already slowed the bat there. His fastest is 72.5 mph by the dataset's own measure (78.9 mph by a finite difference of the dataset's sweet spot, which note 6 already found reads 5–10 % high).
- **The finish is cut at +500 ms**, the recording's end, while he is still coming out of it (the lead foot in the air).
- The rear foot's drag and the lead foot's step after +400 ms are his, not skating: the soles hold still wherever his markers do (the test, §2).

## 4. Dead ends (one line each)

- The body model's segment lengths: his hands out of reach of his bat by up to 11 cm (§2.4).
- His own segment lengths held fixed by two-segment solving between his hip and ankle, shoulder and wrist: the lead knee up to 7 cm off his.
- The lumbar joint placed to square both the hips and the shoulders to their axes: it had to move up to 10.5 cm sideways off the hips-to-shoulders line (his trunk bends 30° sideways at contact).
- The dataset's `left_hip`/`right_hip` as hip centres: 0.31 m apart, pelvis landmarks.
- The ball of the foot under his toe marker: the shoe stretched forward and down by different amounts, 10° off its turn in the viewer's check.
- The foot's roll from the ankle's centre (40° off in the stance) or from the ankle's two markers (the shin's lean, up to 55° after contact).
- The head straight from its four markers: 17° tilted up and 7° turned in the stance, the headband's own offset.
