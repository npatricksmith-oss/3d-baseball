- **How hard the shoulder resists past its range:** Engin & Chen 1986 (J Biomech Eng 108:215–221 and 222–227, "Statistical data base for the biomechanical properties of the human shoulder complex", parts I and II) fitted the restoring moment beyond the shoulder's range in men of 18–32; Peindl & Engin 1987 (J Biomech 20:119) give contours for three subjects. **AB only for all three; no N·m was read.** A perturbation study of 15 people (source not identified in the excerpt, EXC) has a stiffness of 45 N·m/rad with the muscles quiet, in the middle of the range; that is not an end-of-range resistance.

## 5. The forearm's turn, the shoulder's turn and raise, the elbow

- Forearm, men 20–44, passive: palm down 76.9° (95% interval 75.6–78.2), palm up 85.0° (83.8–86.2) (Soucie, TAB). Boone & Azen: 76 and 82 (Norkin's table, TAB). Passive stiffness in mid-range 0.29 and 0.22 N·m/rad (Formica, TAB; a poor fit, R² 0.6).
- Shoulder, the whole complex, men, mean degrees: forward raise 167, back 62, sideways raise 184, turning in 69, turning out 104 (Boone & Azen, through Norkin's table; TAB). AAOS: 180, 60, 180, 70, 90. Forward raise, men 20–44: 168.8 (167.3–170.3) (Soucie, TAB).
- Elbow, men 20–44: flexion 144.6 (143.6–145.6), past straight 0.8 (0.1–1.5) (Soucie, TAB).
- **Not found:** the shoulder's turning range in baseball position players (pitchers: Wilk, above); any passive torque near the end of the shoulder's turn.
