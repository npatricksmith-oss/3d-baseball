# Body research 15: where major leaguers' feet are, and how far a hip turns by its own muscles (2026-11-26, worker body-3d, session 11)

For [PLAN D42](../../../PLAN.md) and [body-motion §4.13](../../body-motion.md) rungs 3 and 4: the front foot's place and angle are to come from practice or MLB stance data and not from the lab study [4]; the hips are to use their active range of turn. This note reports what sources measured. It argues for no number.

**Reading status:** **FT** full text read; **TAB** a table or data file of an open page read; **AB** abstract only; **2nd** quoted by another source; **mine** computed from a source's data. ## A. MLB's stance data (Statcast)

**Source:** Baseball Savant, "Batting Stance Leaderboard", `baseballsavant.mlb.com/visuals/batting-stance` (seasons 2023–2026, with a CSV download); M. Petriello, MLB.com, 2025-03-24, "Most extreme and interesting batting stances" (**FT**).

**What is published as columns** (inches and degrees; qualified hitters; **TAB**):

| Quantity | Definition, in the page's words | 2026, all hitter-sides (n = 231): mean, SD, 10th–90th percentile (**mine**) | 2025 (n = 253) |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| depth in the box | "how far behind the front of the plate the batter stands" (his centre of mass, "the midpoint of their hips") | 27.9, 3.6, 23.9–31.7 in | 27.9, 3.8 |

- **When:** MLB.com: "each data point is the batter's center of mass, taken one second before pitch release". So the columns are the stance before the stride, not the feet at foot plant or at contact. Right- and left-handed hitters differ by under 1.2 in and 1°.
- **The stride is not published as a column.** MLB.com describes it ("distance between original front foot … and where that foot goes by the time the bat makes it to the intercept point") and prints no figure.

- **Not in the data at all:** the feet at foot plant. At release the front foot is often in the air. A front foot can turn open between foot plant and contact, so the intercept's 41–44° is an upper reading of the angle at foot plant.

- **Beside them, what the body stands on now** ([4]'s, flagged): 0.42 of height between the ankle joints (77 cm at 1.84 m), the lead foot 11 cm toward the plate (8° closed) and turned 30° toward the pitcher from square (60° closed).

## C. How far a hip turns by its own muscles

The body's hips now end at Roaas & Andersson 1982's passive means, measured prone with the hip straight (turning in 32.6 ± 8.2°, out 33.6 ± 6.8°; 105 men; **FT**, their Table 1). Session 10's review (P2-3) and §4.12 said "an active range gives about 11° more", from Boone & Azen 1979's 44.4° and 44.2°.

| Source | Subjects | How | Turning in | Turning out | Read |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Boone & Azen, JBJS 1979;61:756 | 56 men of 20–54 | active; **seated, the hip bent 90°** (so say Roaas & Andersson: "Boone & Azen measured with the subjects sitting, i.e. the hip was flexed in their study"; Simoneau 1998; a NASA report) | 44.4 ± 4.3° | 44.2 ± 4.8° | **2nd** (Roaas's Table 1; the paper itself **AB**) |
| Simoneau et al., JOSPT 1998;28:158 | 21 college men, 18–27 | active, goniometer, the same men both ways | **prone (hip straight): 32 ± 9°**; seated: 30 ± 7° | **prone: 44 ± 7°**; seated: 35 ± 8° | **FT** (OCR), Table 3 |
| Ellenbecker et al., AJSM 2007;35:1371 | 101 professional pitchers; 64 elite male tennis players | active, prone, the hip straight, pelvis strapped | 23 ± 8°; 27 ± 10° | 35 ± 9°; 37 ± 9° | **FT** |
| Roach & Miles, Phys Ther 1991;71:656 | white men of 25–39 | active, seated | 34° | 33° | **2nd**, and a garbled full text |
| Gulgin et al. 2019 | 91 adults of 18–35, both sexes | active, standing with weight on the leg | 21–23 ± 10° | 39–42 ± 10° | **FT** |
| Sauers et al., AJSM 2014;42:430 (passive, for contrast) | 49 professional position players | passive, seated | 37–38 ± 5° | 32–34 ± 5° | **FT** |
| Robb et al., AJSM 2010;38:2487 (passive) | 19 professional pitchers | passive, prone | 51 ± 9° (the back leg), 31 ± 6° | 44 ± 9°, 36 ± 6° | **FT** |

**What this says, and it is not what the review or §4.12 assumed:**

- **Boone & Azen's 44° is a seated figure, the hip bent to a right angle.** A hitter's front hip at contact is close to straight. With the hip straight, no source read gives men an active turn *in* above 32° (Simoneau's college men, 32 ± 9°), and the two athlete samples give less (23° and 27°). The front hip's limiting turn in a swing is the turn in.
- **The turn *out* with the hip straight is larger by the hip's own muscles than the body now has:** 44 ± 7° (Simoneau) against 33.6°; athletes 35–37°.
- So "the active range" does not give the front hip 11° more. On Simoneau's men (the one sample measured both seated and prone, active) it gives the turn in 32° (now 32.6°: no change) and the turn out 44° (now 33.6°: 10° more). On the 101 professional pitchers it would take 10° *away* from the turn in.
