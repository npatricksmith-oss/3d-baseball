# Sources for throwing, hands, fielding and the run environment (2026-11-14)

One bounded source pass; no model or parameter changes. High confidence means a published league measurement with a clear definition, not that its population matches this sim. Medium means a published but older, restricted, or differently defined sample. Low means a suggestive comparison only. Unsourced means no defensible numerical measurement was found in this pass. Existing sim measurements below are from the three 2026-11-13 calibration notes, not new runs. The 2024 regular season is a reproducible recent reference, not necessarily the only appropriate target.

## Ranked discrepancies and investigation priorities

1. **Run environment, high confidence in the league numbers:** 5.53 runs/team-game in the current throwing note versus 4.39 in 2024 (+1.14, 26%). In the handedness-only 300-game sample, K 15.3% versus 22.58% (−7.28 percentage points), HR/PA 5.21% versus 2.99% (+2.22 points), BB 7.0% versus 8.18% (−1.18 points). These are outcomes of several mechanisms, not evidence for any single knob. See table below.
2. **Ground-ball hits, medium confidence in comparability:** .351 in the current throwing note versus .245 for 2024 qualifying starting pitchers (+.106); the handedness-only .346 is +.101. The source describes pitcher-level averages, not necessarily a pooled league rate, and its batted-ball definitions and denominator need matching to the sim's scorer. It is a strong investigation signal, not a fitted target.
3. **Platoon K gap, low-to-medium comparison confidence:** sim 5.4 points same minus opposite versus 1.5 points in 2000–2025 non-switch hitters (+3.9). The historical sample is not a 2024 four-cell comparison and has real selection, unequal matchup weights and player skills; it does not prove a particular parameter wrong. The previous “about 3” was unsourced and should not be used as a measurement.
4. **Shortstop dives, medium confidence:** 0.98/game both teams in the handedness probe versus 0.44/game from SIS's 36 per team-season (+0.54, 2.2 times). Definitions and samples may differ. No league-wide dive total was found.
5. **Outfield transfer plus footwork, unresolved but high impact (see second pass for measured individual exchanges):** 0.85 s plus 0.2 s moving away, or 0.3 s after a dive and get-up, may double-count time. Statcast exchange explicitly spans glove contact to release, including any intervening footwork; it does not establish what proportion of this particular estimate already represents footwork. Removing all footwork changed the 300-game probe by −0.37 runs/team-game, but that experiment removes more than possible overlap. No league OF exchange distribution was found; do not subtract 0.2 s on this evidence.
6. **Positional arm means and hands:** the sim's common 36 m/s infield and 39 m/s outfield (80.5 and 87.2 mph) conceal substantial position differences. Against 2026 Statcast means, first and second are about 1.2–1.4 mph slower, shortstop 5.6 mph faster, and right field 3.5 mph faster. Measurement definitions differ. Historical outfielder left-throw shares are 29–34%, versus sim 20–25%; 2010–2014 is not a contemporary roster and games played are not unique players. See below.
7. **Stretch and hurry coefficients:** potentially consequential but numerical error is **unsourced**, not ranked by invented magnitude. Experiments below establish a speed–accuracy tradeoff, but do not quantify the effect of rushing or balance. A 0.4 m stretch costs 0.3 s in the sim; its own sensitivity experiment changed runs by roughly 0.2 per 0.1 m, but that is not a measurement of real reach. No independent hurried/off-balance speed or directional-error loss was found.

## Throwing

[Statcast's exchange definition](https://www.mlb.com/news/major-league-baseballs-statcast-glossary-of-terms-of-state-of-the-art-tracking-technology/c-118508858) is elapsed glove receipt to release, for fielders; [Sportradar](https://developer.sportradar.com/baseball/page/mlb-statcast-event-tracking-metrics) explicitly applies it to infield and outfield. Thus transfer in this sense is not simply the hand-to-hand motion. **Update:** The second pass below found measured IF and individual OF times; representative IF/OF distributions conditional on moving away or diving remain unsourced. Current `FielderAttrs::for_position` uses 0.65 s IF and 0.85 s OF; setup adds turn and footwork, with hurry possible. The overlap question requires matched video/tracking from receipt to release, separated by motion and throw urgency.

A useful cross-check, not a substitute for fielders on batted balls: [HitTrax's summary of 2017 Statcast catchers](https://cdn1.sportngin.com/attachments/document/6cfa-3026833/Catching_Module.pdf) gives average exchange 0.73 s, elite 0.64 s, poor 0.85 s and average arm 81.8 mph (36.57 m/s); medium confidence, definitions of elite/poor and sample not specified. [Savant](https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/poptime) reports 2.0 s average pop to second on steal attempts, receipt to projected receiving point, and accepts exchanges 0.4–1.3 s; high confidence for that published definition, unspecified pooled year. The sim's 0.65 s applies to its catcher on fielded balls; no comparable simulated steal pop time was measured here. Catcher stance and throw make a direct transfer comparison weak.

[Savant arm-strength leaderboard](https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/leaderboard/arm-strength), 2026 as displayed, reports league positional averages in mph: 1B 79.3, 2B 79.1, 3B 84.9, SS 86.1, LF 87.4, CF 89.6, RF 90.7 (35.45, 35.36, 37.95, 38.49, 39.07, 40.05, 40.55 m/s). High confidence in displayed values; its max-effort statistic uses the hardest portion of a player's throws, rather than all throws. The sim uses 36 m/s for all infielders, 39 m/s for all outfielders, and every player currently grade 50. **Position-specific player distributions/percentiles and pitcher arm distribution: unsourced** in this pass; means alone are not distributions. Catchers have their own leaderboard.

The sim estimates: full hurry 10% speed loss, an additional 8% off balance; direction sigma ×2 hurried or ×3 hurried and off balance; earliest release 0.6 of base-plus-turn, turn ±0.12 s, moving-away footwork 0.2 s above 2 m/s, after-dive footwork 0.3 s, safe margin 0.25 s and minimum gain 0.01 s. These and rating scales remain [E]. **Measured hurry/balance losses, turn times, decision thresholds and rate distributions: unsourced.** See the second pass for separate speed–accuracy experiments. Catcher exchanges do not validate these eleven parameters.

## Reach, dives and catch probability

[SIS shortstop study](https://www.sportsinfosolutions.com/?p=1965) reports 36 dives/team-season and outs on 22% of those dives; [SIS 2022–24 dive article](https://www.sportsinfosolutions.com/2025/04/16/stat-of-the-week-the-all-scrapes-team/) reports a second baseman's 140 dives over three seasons, 24% outs for second basemen, a leading right fielder's 30 dives and 22 catches, and 54% average RF catch rate. Medium confidence: charted plays but the standout counts are not league averages. Sim 3.8 dives/game in the handedness probe, SS 0.98 and 2B 0.90; 43% secured on the dive. An infield out after a dive is not equivalent to securing the ball. Aggregate position rates beyond SS and fielder reach distributions: **unsourced**.

[Savant's catch-rate explorer](https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/statcast_catch_probability) relates outfield distance and opportunity time to observed catches; [MLB](https://www.mlb.com/amp/news/catch-probability-updated-to-include-direction-c232532408.html) also describes direction effects. [Savant's categories](https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/catch_probability_leaderboard?min=100) are 5-star 0–25%, 4-star 26–50%, 3-star 51–75%, 2-star 76–90%, 1-star 91–95%. High confidence for definitions, but these are empirical opportunity categories, not a glove success probability conditional on reaching the ball. A numerical distance/time grid and measured additional standing reach and recovery time: **unsourced** in this pass. Sim reach 1.0 m plus 0.4 m stretch, 0.3 s recovery; do not fit it directly from star labels.

## League environment

[2024 Savant league totals](https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/league?nav=hitting&season=2024&view=statcast): 182,449 PA, 41,197 K, 14,929 BB and 5,453 HR. Dividing by PA gives K 22.58%, BB 8.18%, HR/PA 2.99%; BB includes intentional walks. [Baseball-Reference](https://www.baseball-reference.com/leagues/majors/2024-standard-batting.shtml) gives 4.39 runs/team-game. High confidence in published season totals and arithmetic; compare to handedness note's 300 games rather than treating them as infinite-precision estimates. Current throwing-note 5.53 is a later build than the other sim rates.

[2024 starters with at least 12 starts](https://www.mlbdatawarehouse.com/p/sp-luck-analysis-avg-and-slg-by-batted), published January 2025, give ground-ball .245, line-drive .624, fly-ball .251 batting averages; medium confidence because the article describes pitcher averages and its exact denominators are not fully specified. Fly-ball .251 has substantial HR power in the same table and cannot safely be treated as HR-excluded BABIP. **HR-excluded BABIP by all batted-ball types, including popups: unsourced.** Sim's .351 GB hits and .124 fly-ball hits (the latter earlier handedness build) are its own hit-per-class measures; match HR exclusion, sacrifices, errors, batted-ball classification and sample before interpreting gaps. The often-repeated .24 ground figure is directionally supported by .245, but is not established here as a precise league-wide BABIP.

### Glove to release and footwork

[Kita, Yamamoto and Maeda (2014)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ijshs/advpub/0/advpub_201316/_pdf/-char/ja) measured 14 right-handed infielders (12 college, two professional) fielding a ball rolled directly at them and throwing 20 m as quickly as possible. Mean **fastest of five trials** was 0.640 ± 0.068 s (SD), glove contact to release; mean throw speed 28.0 ± 2.4 m/s. Foot-off, hop, stride and acceleration phases averaged 0.222, 0.075, 0.197 and 0.146 s, respectively, and sum to the release interval. Medium confidence for this highly specific laboratory drill, low as a league mean. It is close to the sim's 0.65 s base (0.01 s difference), but fastest-of-five, straight-on, lower-velocity throws are not typical MLB game throws. The 0.075 s hop is **inside** 0.640 s; it is not a measured extra penalty for a ball moving away.

[MLB Statcast's 2016 second-base sample](https://www.mlb.com/news/verlander-and-buxton-lead-2016-statcast-team-c209343802) reports 1.2 s league average on plays where the batter was out and the 2B credited with an assist, mainly grounders and successful double-play pivots; Pedroia 1.02 s, fastest among 68 players with at least 25 chances, and some averages above 1.4 s. Medium confidence for this selected play set, not a blanket ground-ball or pivot mean. Compared with sim 0.65 s on batted balls and roughly 0.455 s relay transfer (0.7 × 0.65), the selected league value is 0.55 s and 0.745 s higher respectively; the aggregate does not identify the separate play-type values, nor does it justify directly replacing either parameter.

[MLB's 2017 Cleveland outfield report](https://www.mlb.com/bluejays/news/lonnie-chisenhall-talks-learning-curve-in-cf-c224663466) reports Statcast exchange averages of 1.0 s for Lonnie Chisenhall and 0.9 s for Brandon Guyer in a **limited early-season sample**; [MLB's 2024 highlight](https://www.mlb.com/amp/news/the-best-plays-of-2024-for-each-of-the-5-tools.html) gives a single throw at 0.93 s. Low confidence as population benchmarks; sample size/play selection and movement states are not supplied. The sim's base 0.85 s is 0.05–0.15 s below those two individual means; adding 0.2 s yields 1.05 s, within 0.05–0.15 s of them, but this is **not evidence** of how much is double-counted. A separate outfield crow-hop time, a league distribution, and matched running-away, dive, grounder and fly-ball exchange distributions remain **unsourced**. The controlled infield study demonstrates concurrent footwork within exchange; it does not measure outfield crow hops.

### Velocity versus accuracy

[Freeston, Adams and Rooney](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24936898/) studied 20 elite adolescent male baseball players (mean age 19.6) at 80% and 100% maximal throwing speed. Their [paper's table](https://www.academia.edu/124016146/Shoulder_Proprioception_Is_Not_Related_to_Throwing_Speed_or_Accuracy_in_Elite_Adolescent_Male_Baseball_Players) gives speeds 26.4 versus 33.0 m/s, mean total target error 46.9 versus 61.1 cm (+30%), and mean vertical error 30.6 versus 46.8 cm (+53%). Medium confidence for this experimental cohort and task, low for MLB game situations; error includes bias and is **not directional sigma**. [Freeston, Ferdinands and Rooney (2015)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26270853/) independently studied nine elite juniors throwing 10 fastballs at each of 80% and 100% of maximum at a 7 cm target 20 m away; error increased at higher speed, principally vertically. [Freeston and Rooney (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25068737/) found best accuracy at 70% speed among the tested speeds in their baseball task. Confidence medium for experimental direction, low for numerical transfer to rushed fielding.

These studies vary **ball speed intentionally**, not preparation time or balance. They do not support interpreting the sim's slower hurried throws and doubled/tripled directional sigma as an empirical causal effect. Nor does a +30% total-error ratio validate a sigma multiplier. Quantitative game throw speed/accuracy losses from haste, awkward footing or off-balance posture remain **unsourced**.
