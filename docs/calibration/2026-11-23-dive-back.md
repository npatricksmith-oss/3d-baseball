# The dive back (owner's Q39 (a)): built, measured, and what it does not fix (2026-11-23)

`work/dive-back`, main `310d6ba` → `c167aee`, then the review fixes (`e4cd152`, §6). Spec: agent-models §6.4, "Diving back". The cohorts are direct seeds 0–299 and 1000–1299 with average fielders, as in the [runner-start note](2026-11-23-runner-start-open.md). The throwaway probes `zz_dive_back.rs`, `zz_trace_play.rs` and `zz_dives_in_game.rs`, their outputs, and the Retrosheet scripts `retro_third_gb.py` and `retro_second_gb.py` are in `sim/target/codex-probes/dive/` and `sim/target/codex-probes/rs/`. They are not tracked.

## Summary

1. **Built.** A runner whose return is close dives headfirst instead of braking onto the bag. He runs in at full speed, leaves his feet 2.2 m out, glides with his hand 0.4 m ahead of him, and lies on the bag for 1.0 s. The rules see his hand's touch. A tag reaches his whole body laid out. He makes no decision until he is up. The client draws it from the sim's record, with the hand on the bag at the sim's touch.
2. **It happens about once a game.** In 600 games, 554 dives reached the bag, and the runner was out at that bag after the touch once. In the standard run, 207 of 829 dives were put out in the air (review P2-1): mostly runners held to the way back by the turn cap, beaten by more than a second. *After the review fixes* (§6) he dives on a return to a tag play only when the dive can change the race, and on any close retouch. That leaves 690 dives in the standard run, 39 of them put out in the air (25 of those are appeals).
3. **It hardly changes the outs.** Tag outs going back on grounders went from 73 to 72, appeal outs from 141 to 133, and runs did not move. *After the review fixes* they are 75 and 134 (§6). The runner-start note predicted about 90 and 60–80 fewer. **That prediction misread the umpire's margin.** `OutCall::margin_s` is the runner's *distance* from the bag at the out divided by his top speed, not the time he lost. The "0.13 s" typical tag out was a runner 1.15 m short. When the tagger got the ball, he was a median 8.7–9.9 m from the bag: about a second late, beyond any dive.
4. **The runner from third breaking on a grounder in front of him is not a fault.** Against Retrosheet 2024 he goes and scores at MLB's rate (56% scored against 56%). The excess is where trapped runners are put out: back at third in the sim, at home or in a rundown in MLB. That is the rundown (Q11), not his break. The same holds, more strongly, for the runner from second (§4).

## 1. The model and its values

agent-models §6.4, "Diving back", has the full statement. The values are:
- `DIVE_CLOSE_S` 0.5 s: he dives when his margin back, braking included, is under this.
- `DIVE_BACK_M` 2.2 m: where he leaves his feet.
- `DIVE_KEEP` 0.8 and `DIVE_PUSH_MPS` 4 m/s: the glide.
- `DIVE_REACH_M` 0.4 m and `DIVE_BODY_M` 2.0 m: the hand's lead and his length laid out.
- `DIVE_BACK_UP_S` 1.0 s: down on the bag.

The calibration targets are:
- **At full speed** the dive ties a run-through over the last 2.2 m (0.281 against 0.283 s at 8 m/s). Running through is the quickest arrival, ahead of headfirst, feet-first and run-to-a-stop (J Sport Health Sci 2016).
- **From a 3.5 m lead at rest** it takes 0.99 s; the source says "less than 1 second".
- **Against braking** it saves 0.43, 0.62 and 0.73 s from 4, 8 and 15 m at rest. The runner-start note's table had 0.45, 0.63 and 0.74.

The model's per-tick law (`dive_tick`) is checked against a closed-form calculation in the tests.

- **MLB runners do go on ground balls in front of them.** They score on half of those to the third baseman and the pitcher, and on more than half of those to first. "Ball in front, hold" is a youth coaching rule, not what the majors' data shows: with the infield back the run is usually conceded. The sim's runner breaks about as often and scores at the same rate overall, so his break is not a fault overall, and the job's fix is not made. *By fielder it is not settled* (review P3-4, below).
- **The difference is where the outs fall.** MLB's 12% are at home. That includes rundowns, which end there or at third and are scored as such. In the sim a runner who sees the throw beat him turns back once, and is tagged going back at third (5%, against MLB's 0.4%). That is the rundown stand-in (`MAX_TURNS`; Q11). The dive does not change it, because those runners are seconds late. Whether MLB's `3XH` outs include rundowns that end back near third is plausible but unchecked.
- **Open (review P3-4): the totals match, but the rows do not.** The sim scores far less on balls to the pitcher (10% against 48%) and to first (39% against 54%), and far more on balls to short (76% against 55%), so the matching 56% is two errors cancelling. MLB's cohort also includes the infield in, which the sim does not model and which moves MLB's outcomes toward outs at home. The runner's break by where the ball is hit, and the infield-in confound, stay open.

## 4. The runner from second

Of the 72 runners tagged going back on ground balls, 51 started on second, many on comebackers to the pitcher. In seed 11, play 69 the runner broke for third, the pitcher threw to third, and the runner was tagged going back to second after the throw went around. Retrosheet 2024 (`retro_second_gb.py`) takes a runner on second, first base open, 0–1 out, on an infield ground ball (645 chances per 600 games):
- **MLB:** 64% advanced, 3.1% put out at third (about 20 per 600 games), and 3 runners in 2,429 games put out going back to second (0.7 per 600).
- **The sim:** 51 per 600 put out going back to second.

The likeliest reading is the same as §3. MLB's outs on these runners are at third, where a committed runner is tagged or run down. The sim's are at second, after a turn back.

**Not measured:** the sim's outs at third for these runners, which is the comparison that settles it.

## 5. Open

- **The trapped runner (Q11, the rundown).** Most of the remaining excess of outs going back, on ground balls and on liners, is runners beaten by a second or more. The dive cannot help them. Whether they should have broken at all is the decision side: the in-flight liner read for appeals, and, for second, the read of a comebacker.
- **The runner-start note's §3 figures** (90 tag outs "within 0.3 s", 60–80 appeals rescued, about 0.25 fewer outs a game, +0.1 runs) rest on reading `margin_s` as time. Corrected here.
- **Cosmetic:** the fielder covering the bag can overlap the lying runner; nothing keeps two bodies apart (TASKS §1). The figure upright at take-off is fixed (§6, P3-6).
- **Not modelled:** a feet-first slide back; reaching for the far side of the bag; a runner who decides to dive late, at the bag, from a fresh look. He decides only at his looks, and stays committed until a look changes his goal.

## 6. The review fixes (2026-11-23, [review](../reviews/2026-11-23-rev-q39-dive-back-review.md))

**P2-1: dive only when the dive can change the race.** He dives only if his margin back by his own read lies between −`DIVE_SAVE_S` (0.4 s, the least the dive saves) and `DIVE_CLOSE_S` (0.5 s). Beaten by more, he brakes as before. This holds for the look between bases, including the runner the turn cap keeps on the way back, and for the retouch. **P3-1:** once chosen, a dive stands while he keeps going back.

Counted with the reviewer's prints and `analyse.py` (`sim/target/review-probes/q39/`) on a copy of the fixed code. The standard run, `--seed 0` and `--seed 1000`, is 600 games; the copy's report is identical to the clean run's apart from timing.

In the probe cohort (`zz_dive_back`, direct seeds), tag outs going back on grounders are 73 (main 73, first version 72). Appeal outs are 141 (main 141, first version 133), and runs are within noise. The 8 appeals the first version saved were retouches the runner believed lost by more than 0.4 s. He dove anyway and his hand got there. His retouch belief (a typical transfer and throw from the catcher) is pessimistic for those catches.

**The Coordinator's decision (2026-11-23): no lower bound on the retouch** (`b4abc03`). On an appeal the fielder needs only to touch the bag. The runner has no rundown or other way to stay alive, so his quickest arrival is his only chance, and real runners dive back on double-off attempts whether or not they think they will make it. The bound stays on a return to a tag play, where he can pull up and try to stay alive. Measured again after a touch and a clean rebuild:

|---|---:|---:|
| Dives in the standard run | 552 | 690 |
| … put out in the air | 14 | 39 (25 more appeals) |
| … play ended in the air | 2 | 8 |

| Tag outs going back on grounders, probe | 73 | 75 (noise) |

The pessimistic retouch belief is left as a lead (TASKS §1).

**P2-2: a dive ending in the air is recorded.**
- The record is made at take-off. A tag or appeal before the touch, or the play's end, closes it with no touch, his hand where it was, and him up 1.0 s later.
- The timeline has `Dive` and `DiveUp` with no `BaseTouch`.
- The FFI's `BbDive` carries the end, whether he touched, and the hand's points at take-off and at the end (P3-2). Its layout version is checked by the client (P3-5).
- The client draws the glide and the runner lying short of the bag.
