## 1. Plays 66 and 168: no double-play attempt

### 66 (B2, 1 out, runner on first, 2-2): 6-3, runner to second

Ground ball, 92 mph at −23°, spray −18°. The shortstop secures it at 2.11 s at (−8.1, 40.1) and is drifting away from second. His decision at 2.66 s (his set-up done) is from the probe's `choice_trace`:

| Option | Runner at | Ball at | Receiver at the bag | p(out) | Value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| throw to first (force on batter) | 4.64 | 3.70 | 1B already there | 1.00 | **0.90** |
| throw to second (force on runner) | 3.80 | 3.68 (direct throw 2.94, held for the receiver) | 2B **3.63** | 0.85 | 0.72 (second out p ≈ 0) |

The choice follows from his beliefs. A sure out at first is worth more than an 85% force with no double play behind it. **The belief is the fault.** `receiver_ready` (live.rs:6151) is `t + run_time(d − 0.5, TYPICAL_SPEED, 0.9)`, the run from **rest**. At 2.66 the second baseman is 3.9 m from the bag and running at 6–7 m/s; the throw needs him only within reach, not standing on the bag. His real arrival depends on how he moves; the estimate ignores that.

Counterfactual (probe copy, play 66 only): `receiver_ready` from the man's current speed toward the bag, `run_time_from(d, v0, 8, 0.9)`. The cover's movement is unchanged. The shortstop throws to second at 2.93 s. The second baseman takes it at 3.23 s and touches the bag at 3.36 s, a **force on the lead runner by 0.43 s**. There is still no double play: he takes the bag moving away from first, and the batter is at first at 4.63 s.

That is what a real shortstop does with that ball: take the lead runner.

### 168 (T6, 1 out, runner on first, 1-2): 4-6, batter safe at first

Ground ball, 71 mph at 6°, toward the second baseman. He secures it at 2.05 s at (5.9, 43.0), 7.1 m from second, with the runner from first at 3.74 s. The shortstop is covering from (−13.1, 42.1), 13.4 m away.

The holder's table on every tick (probe hook on `holder_act`):
- **2.05 s:** a *carry* to second (ball at 3.34) beats the toss to the shortstop (ball at 3.56: the shortstop is believed at the bag at **3.51**). Values 0.999 against 0.875.
- **2.05–2.50 s:** he is turning from moving away (1.5, −4.1 m/s) toward the bag. He covers only 1.6 m, and the carry's estimate slips to 3.50.
- **2.52 s:** the toss (0.957) passes the carry (0.945). He abandons the carry, brakes to a stop 5 m from the bag (`abandon_carry`, live.rs:6012), and waits for the shortstop (`asks`, live.rs:5953).
- **3.17 s:** he throws.
- **3.33 s:** the shortstop catches it on the bag, nearly stopped. Force at second, margin 0.40 s.

At 3.33 the shortstop's pivot estimate has the ball at first at 5.00 s against the batter at 4.75 s: p 0.02, so no throw.

The carry flip-flop cost nothing: the throw was waiting for the shortstop anyway. **The bottleneck is the cover.** From rest, the speed law takes the shortstop over 13.4 m in about 2.4 s, so the bag at about 2.6 s after contact; he took 3.0 s. The difference is the brake: a cover man's wanted speed is √(2·`BRAKE`·d) (live.rs:2259/2278, agent-models §5.3: "a non-chaser … wants the speed that stops him on his spot"). From 6.9 m/s at 4.75 m/s² a full stop takes 5 m and 1.45 s. Play 66's second baseman did the same (from about 3.8 m out at 2.58 s, on the bag about 3.6 s).

Counterfactual (probe copy, play 168 only): a `Role::Cover(2)` man arrives at speed v_c, wanting √(v_c² + 2·`BRAKE`·d), the form of the wall's contact-speed cap:

| v_c | Shortstop's catch at second | Batter at first | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| (now: stops) | 3.33 | 4.75 | force at second only |
| 3 m/s | 3.17 | — | force at second only |
| 4 m/s | 3.10 | 4.63 out (margin 0.12) | **double play** |
| 5 m/s | 3.05 | 4.57 out (margin 0.18) | **double play** |
| 4 m/s + `receiver_ready` fix | 3.03 | 4.57 out (margin 0.18) | **double play** |

With the `receiver_ready` fix alone the force at second comes at 3.08, but there is still no double play. Both changes are needed for 168. The `receiver_ready` fix alone settles 66.

**Verdicts.**
- 66 is a fault in the holder's belief (the choice itself is sound).
- 168 is a fault in how the covering man reaches the bag.
- Neither is the throw choice's valuation. The 2B's carry choice at 2.05 s was right by his read: he was 7 m from the bag and the shortstop was not there yet.

**Fixes.**
1. **`receiver_ready` from the receiver's visible motion** (no owner decision; a belief that disagrees with the movement law). Use his speed toward the bag (`run_time_from`, live.rs:1468), and the braking he will do if he brakes, so the estimate matches the world (`reach_estimate_tracks_the_movement_step` is the model test). Count him ready at receiving reach, not at 0.5 m. Risk: every throw's receiver timing changes, so every digest. Throws held for a late receiver go earlier and are believed better, so lead-runner forces rise. Test: the estimate against the stepped movement for covers already running.
2. **Q-A (proposed, owner): how a covering man takes a force base.**
   - *What happens now:* every man covering a base brakes to a full stop on it, 0.4–0.5 s slower over the last 5 m than running through. This is deliberate in agent-models §5.5 ("a covering man brakes onto the bag at 2.39 m/s at the touch radius"); the pivot model was built on it.
   - *What is wrong:* a real middle infielder covering second on a double-play ball reaches the bag moving and uses it (drag foot, cross or straddle). He times his arrival to the feed; he does not stop at it.
   - *Options:*
     - (a) A covering man at a base with a force pending takes it at a crossing speed `COVER_CROSS_MPS` [E, unsourced, about 4 m/s], always.
     - (b) As a chaser does: he brakes onto the bag only when early for the ball (his time to it, plus a margin, before the throw's), otherwise crosses at `COVER_CROSS_MPS`. The holder's estimate uses the same rule.
     - (c) Leave it.
   - *Recommendation:* (b). It keeps the early man's set feet and gives the late man the bag in time.
   - *Risk:* double plays. The last counts on record (ledger rows for the pivot review fixes and the infield alignment fixes, seeds 0–255) were 12.6–12.8% per opportunity against MLB 9.8%. By the fielder who started the play (pivot review fixes row):

     | First fielder | Sim | MLB |
     |---|---|---|
     | 2B | 37.4% | 44.7% |
     | SS | 54.8% | 54.1% |
     | 3B | 48.1% | 37.8% |
     | P | 43.6% | 19.6% |
     | 1B | 42.6% | 21.5% |

     2B-started plays (168's kind) are low, consistent with a slow covering shortstop. Faster covers will also raise the 3B, pitcher and first-base plays that are already high. The job should re-measure `dp_probe` by first fielder, and expect the excess on those three to need its own cause (the pitcher's share of chances is already on record).
   - A man crossing the bag moving away from first now owes the plant (agent-models §5.5: v_away/`BRAKE` − 0.42 s). That partly offsets the gain for the second baseman (66) and not for the shortstop (168), as in real double plays.

## 2. Play 71: the right fielder and the rolling ball (triple)

T3, 1 out, bases empty. A liner at 89 mph and 13°, spray 15°. First bounce 2.19 s at (20.4, 53.9). It rolls into right-centre, past the right fielder, and stops near the wall at (46.9, 106.0). The centre fielder picks it up at 7.87 s; triple.

His reads (probe hook on `read`), truth against belief, with his intercept:

| t | Read | Truth | Belief | Intercept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.05 | tracking | (6.9, 22.9) v (11.2, 31.4) | (6.8, 20.7) v (10.7, 28.4) | (31.4, 67.7) at 3.54 |
| 2.12 | tracking | (19.6, 52.3) | (18.7, 47.4) v 10% slow | (32.0, 67.8) at 3.51 |
| **2.24** | **snapshot (after the bounce)** | (20.9, 54.9, z 0.15) | **(31.1, 66.3, z −0.24)** | **(44.9, 95.3) at 6.81** |
| 3.25 | snapshot | (29.7, 72.2) | (32.6, 73.0) | (44.9, 101.7) at 7.36 |
| 4.09 | snapshot | (35.4, 83.4) | (36.1, 80.8) | (45.1, 101.8) at 7.44 |

His fixed draws for the play are err = [−3.09, −0.48, 3.41, 1.09]. That is a 3.1σ draw along his line of sight and 3.4σ in speed along it.

- **In flight** the tracking read carries `TRACK_BIAS_FRAC` (0.6) of them. It has the ball 4–5 m short along his line of sight and 10% slow, so he charged to cut it off at (32, 67.6) at 3.5 s. The ball passed (32, 77) at 3.6 s. His own doubt was 1.7 m. This is a big but **believable misjudgment**: a hard liner's depth read short by a 3σ error, which the filter is built to allow.
- **At the bounce** the tracking read ends ("a new ball (a bounce …) ends it; after that the snapshot read applies, unchanged", agent-models §5.1). The snapshot read (`predict`, live.rs:2444 → `read_prediction_k` → `noisy_ball_motion`, live.rs:1816) puts **the full draw, times `DEPTH_STRETCH` 3**, on the position along his line of sight:
  - −3.09 × 1.63 m × 3 = **−15 m**. His read jumps from 4 m off to 15 m off at the moment a real fielder gets his best cue, the bounce he just watched 26 m away.
  - The height noise (err[1]·σ/2 = −0.39 m) takes a ball rolling at 0.15 m to **z = −0.24 m**. The believed path then never meets the ground ("bounce None", z −83 m at his intercept time, path 17 s long).
  - He turned at 2.30 s toward an intercept at the wall and never came back to the ball. It passed 5.7 m to his left at 3.3 s.

Counterfactuals (probe copy, play 71 only; snapshot reads after the bounce):

| Change | Result |
|---|---|
| A true read (draws 0) | He gets to the ball's line and has it at 6.37 s at (35.3, 78.3). **Single.** |
| Height kept above the ground, draws unchanged | Triple (the 15 m depth error alone turns him) |
| Draws × 0.6 and height kept above the ground | Triple (still 9 m off) |

So the jump in error at the bounce decides this play. The underground path is a separate bug that did not change it.

**Verdict:** fault. The read after a bounce is worse than the read before it, by design; the owner's "the fielder's tracking is off" is right.

**Fixes.**
1. **No owner decision:** a read never puts a ball on or near the ground below it (clamp the believed height at the ground for a ball whose truth is on it, or take a rolling ball's height as known). A belief path must meet the ground.
2. **Q-B (proposed, owner): keep tracking a ball after it bounces.**
   - *What happens now:* each bounce, touch or wall ends the tracking read. After it a fielder's read of a rolling or bouncing ball is a fresh snapshot with his whole fixed error, three times worse along his line of sight, every replan.
   - *What is wrong:* a fielder watching a ball sees it bounce and roll. His read should go on improving as it comes, not jump.
   - *Options:*
     - (a) A bounce starts a new track seeded from the old estimate and the seen bounce point, its persistent part carried over (the §5.1 filter applies to a ball on the ground: no gravity, rolling friction as believed).
     - (b) Keep the snapshot read after a bounce but with the tracking share of the draws (`TRACK_BIAS_FRAC`) and the view counted from the bounce. Cheaper, but not enough here (still 9 m off; a triple).
     - (c) Leave it.
   - *Recommendation:* (a), an Opus job.
   - *Risk:* outfield reads of grounders and bounced liners get better, so gap balls cut off more often. Doubles and triples fall (triples were at the read-off level after the catch-throw work); runs fall.

## 3. Play 102: the second baseman hardly moved

T4, 0 out, bases empty. A grounder at 98 mph and −15°, spray 11°, up the middle on the second baseman's side of the bag. It crosses his depth (y = 42.7) at about 1.8 s at x ≈ 6, **8.8 m to his left, 1.4 s after contact**.

- **Reach.** He needs about 6.3 m beyond his reach and a dive (1.0 + 1.5 m) in the 1.15 s after his reaction. From rest the speed law gives 4.6 m. **Out of range**, as it would be for a real second baseman. A clean single.
- **Read.** His read at 0.66 s matches the truth to 0.2 m (infield σ0 0.3 m). Its intercept is at the warning track, 11.3 s. **Not chasing was right.**
- **What the owner saw.** At the defense's call (0.66 s) the cover table (`cover_table`, live.rs:4051) gave second to the **shortstop** (ball right of centre, order [SS, 2B]) and the second baseman nothing. He became `Idle` (`go_idle`, live.rs:1596), braked out of his jump and **stood from 0.81 s to 1.56 s**.
- **The job swap.** At 1.26 s the ball was down in the outfield, with the centre fielder fielding it at depth 88 m (> `RELAY_DEEP_M` = 80 m). `relay_job` (live.rs:3740) treats that as a deep ball and makes the relay for **third**. Its man is whoever starts nearer that line (live.rs:3770): the shortstop. Second then fell to the second baseman at 1.56 s, and he jogged over.

A real pair does the opposite, at once. The man on the ball's side (here the second baseman) goes out toward the ball as the cutoff to second, and the shortstop covers second. Neither stands still while the ball is loose.

**Verdict:** holding was right (out of reach, correctly read). The 0.75 s freeze and the swapped jobs are a fault in the job tables, mostly visual: bases empty, the batter stopped at first.

**Q-C (proposed, owner): the middle infielders' jobs on a ball through the infield.**
- *What happens now:* the first call deals the bases by a fixed order. A man left without a job stands where he stopped. A ground single fielded beyond 80 m counts as a deep ball, and the relay goes out for third.
- *Options:*
  - (a) At the first call, on a ball past the infield on the ground, the ball-side middle infielder takes the cutoff for second and the other covers second. The deep relay rule keeps only balls hit over or past an outfielder in the air.
  - (b) Only stop the freeze: a man with no job keeps a ready drift toward the ball's line until he is given one.
  - (c) Leave it.
- *Recommendation:* (a).
- *Risk:* small on runs (the batter-runner's read of taking second on a single); visible on every single up the middle.

## 4. Play 90: the runner gives up in a rundown (the stand-in only)

B3, 0 out, runner on second. A grounder at 105 mph, back to the pitcher (secured 0.80 s at (0.4, 18.0)).

1. The runner had broken for third at contact. He went back toward second (believing he made it, so no turn counted).
2. The pitcher held the ball 1.4 s and threw to the shortstop at second (caught 2.97 s).
3. Trapped (safe at neither base by his read), the runner turned toward the base the ball was farther from: third (live.rs:5401). That is his one turn.
4. The shortstop threw to third at 3.43 s (caught 4.42 s). On his next look the runner is trapped again and would turn back, but `MAX_TURNS` = 1 is spent (live.rs:5414). He stays committed and runs into the third baseman's tag at 6.15 s, 1.5 m short of the bag.

The batter reached first. That is the stand-in as agent-models §6.3 describes it: one turn, then committed, because fielders do not close on a trapped runner yet. The rundown is the planned job (TASKS, Q11). Nothing is proposed here.
