# The 25–50° double-offs: the read bias, the in-flight relook and the catch cut-offs

**Job.** Runners doubled off on caught 25–50° flies rose from 16.5 to 38.0 per 300 games with the catch-read fixes (`c313f6a`). The Opus re-review found that the runner's first look read these flies long even with no read error ([review P2-1](../reviews/2026-11-18-opus-catch-fixes2-review.md)), and that nothing made a runner look again while the ball was in the air (its Q8). This note covers the three steps on `work/fly-read`, from main `48f36a9`: the read bias, the in-flight relook, and the catch cut-offs.

## 1. The read bias

### Isolating it

A throwaway probe (`sim/target/codex-probes/flyread/zz_flyread_probe.rs`, from the reviewer's) takes every fair 25–50° ball that lands in the park in seeds 0–39 and, at 0.5 s after contact, flies the **true** position and velocity forward with the observer's physics. Only the spin changes between variants. The miss is along the spray line, + = read deeper, the reviewer's metric:

| Spin flown from the true state at 0.5 s | 80–100 m | 100 m and more |
|---|---:|---:|
| The true spin vector | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| The believed spin (pure backspin, median rpm by launch angle) | +2.7 m | +2.8 m |
| The true spin's backspin component only | +1.9 | +2.5 |
| The believed axis with the true \|ω\| | +0.4 | +1.2 |
| The true axis with the believed \|ω\| | +1.9 | +1.1 |
| The believed spin's lift, the true \|ω\|'s drag | +0.2 | +0.4 |

So the model and its integration step are exact, and the plate prior plays no part (the reviewer checked that too). The long read is drag. The flight model's drag coefficient grows with the whole spin (`cdspin` × rpm), and a batted ball's spin is far from pure backspin. On deep flies the median ball has 39% of its \|ω\| along its flight (gyro) and 42% as side spin. At 35°, all batted balls have a median 4,700 rpm in all, of which 2,900 is backspin. The belief held the backspin only, so every fly was flown with too little drag.

Spin also depends on exit speed, which an observer sees. At 35°, the median whole spin is 4,900 rpm at 80–90 mph and 3,700 rpm from 100 mph up (`batted_spin`, all batted balls, 96 games).

### The fix: a belief about the spin he cannot see

The belief (`believed_flight`, agent-models §5.1) now has two parts, both tabulated by launch angle × exit speed (10° × 10 mph, bilinear between bin centres):

- **Backspin:** the median of balls launched within the foul lines, `SPIN_BACK_RPM`. Foul balls are excluded because they are mishits. At 30–40°, a fifth of all batted balls end foul. Their sidespin makes them carry 5 m less than the belief, and they had pulled every table.
- **The unseen spin:** the side and gyro spin, whose axis he cannot tell. A new field `FlightEnv::unseen_spin` makes it count in the drag, √(\|ω\|² + unseen²), with no Magnus force. Its sideways push averages out over an axis he cannot see. In the world it is zero, and the world's arithmetic keeps its old branch, so no world flight changes. `SPIN_REST_RPM` holds a fitted value, not a median. For each cell it is the value at which that cell's balls carry as far as they truly do in the median. Each ball is flown from its own launch with the tables interpolated as the game looks them up (open air, no fence). The fit is repeated over all cells until it settles, because each cell's value reaches its neighbours' balls (four sweeps; the last moved a cell by at most 700 rpm). The median of each ball's own unseen spin is not enough. It varies by about ±35%, and carry is not linear in it, so flying the median read fair flies about 1.5–2 m short.

Fitted values run from about 2,000 to 6,000 rpm, 0.8–2.2× the cell's median own unseen spin (the most on 15–25° liners). They are zero at 5° and below and for pop-ups under 80 mph, where backspin alone already reads no farther than the truth, so grounders and those pop-ups keep their old belief apart from the backspin refit.

*Tried and dropped:*
- The unseen spin as a gyro vector along the launch direction adds the right drag. Once the ball turns over, though, it gives a one-signed sideways Magnus force, so a straightaway fly was believed to curve 7.7 m to left.
- Tabulating the median whole spin read 1.5–2 m short.
- Including foul balls in the fit read fair flies 2.4 m short.

### Result (zero read error, seeds 0–39, fair 25–50° balls landing in the park)

The reviewer's along-the-spray-line metric charges an observer for sideways curve, which comes from side spin he cannot see. With a belief that no longer has a one-sided bias, that charge shows up as a short read of 0.7–0.9 m, so the table gives carry both ways. Carry here is distance from home, as the depth of a catch is.

| | Before (main's belief) | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| Runner's first look, 80–100 m: median carry miss (spray line) | +3.2 m (+2.5) | −0.0 m (−0.7) |
| Runner's first look, 100 m and more | +3.3 m (+2.6) | −0.4 m (−0.9) |
| Runner's first look, 10th / 90th percentile, all | +1.0 / +6.6 | −2.5 / +3.0 |
| Reads deeper than the truth | 100% | 46% |
| Deep flies (≥ 80 m) read over the fence | 14 of 392 | 3 of 392 |
| Fielder's first look (0.35 s, true contact point), all | about the same as the runner's | −0.3 m (−1.0) |

Test `a_zero_error_first_look_reads_deep_flies_without_bias` repeats this on seeds 100–123, outside the tables' fitting sample. The deep flies have a median miss of −0.33 m, 46% read deeper, and none read over the fence. The bounds are ±1 m, at least a quarter on each side, and at most 3% over the fence.

### Fielders

Fielders share the snapshot read and the tracking filter's flight model, so the fix applies to them as well. The 2026-11-15 outfield-read note said it fixed "read every fly too deep" ([note](2026-11-15-outfield-read.md)). That held for reads 2 s before landing, but not for the first look. The probe shows the old fielder first look as long as the runner's, about +3 m. It is now −0.3 m.

Routine flies caught by outfield read grade (`fly_misses 0 100 - <grade>`, the [track-bias note's](2026-11-17-track-bias.md) band of 90–99% by ability):

| Grade | 20 | 50 | 80 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Before (track-bias note, final build) | .934 | .979 | .986 |
| After | .919 | .980 | .986 |

The spread stays inside the band, and no refit was made. The read-skill test's fly set (18–28 m runs, ≥3.5 s) caught grade 20 279 and grade 80 292 of 308 before. Its first run on the bias fix gave 288 and 287 of 304, because a poor reader gained the most from an unbiased model. The final build gives grade 20 251 and grade 80 259 of 268. These are different ball sets, drawn from games that moved.

The plate cue's spin allowance was rechecked with `plate_cue` (48 games, the true velocity flown back with the typical spin). The median miss is 0.55–0.65 m at 0.5 s, 1.8–2.2 m at 1 s and 4.3–8.3 m at 2 s, as before (`PLATE_SPIN_M` = 0.6 m at 0.5 s, 2.0 m at 1 s). Liners still up after 3–4 s now miss more: 20.5 m at 3 s and 40 m at 4 s, against 18–23 m before. That is 70 balls in 48 games, and their fitted unseen spin is the largest. Left as it is; see open items.

## 2. The in-flight relook

Agent-models §6.3, "He keeps watching while it is up". A runner who goes at his first look on a ball still in the air looks again every `RUNNER_FLY_LOOK_S` = 0.5 s [E] through the same observation as his first look, with new draws (`RunnerPerception` sub-index 2^21 + the look's number, data-formats §2.3). At 80% or more he goes back to tag up. Above 20% he pulls up into the farthest hold he can still get back from. Below that he keeps going. Fixture `a_runner_who_reads_a_home_run_pulls_up_when_the_fielder_settles_under_it` uses two worlds that differ only in where the center fielder visibly stands. With the relook switched off, the runner is still going at 57 m and 8.1 m/s when the fielder is under the ball.

A runner **holding partway** keeps looking the same way. Step 3 added this after measuring that runners on second almost never tag (below). When he becomes sure of the catch and can be back on the bag before the earliest catch he reads as plausible, he goes back to tag up. Fixture `a_runner_holding_partway_goes_back_to_tag_when_the_catch_is_sure` uses the same two worlds. With the holding relook off, the runner never reaches the bag.

## 3. The catch cut-offs

### MLB rates (Retrosheet 2024 parsed play-by-play)

The source is `2024plays.csv` from [Retrosheet's downloads](https://www.retrosheet.org/downloads/plays.html): 2,429 regular-season games, `gametype=regular`. A catch is a play whose first part is a single fielder number with a credited batting out, with fewer than two out and a runner on. A runner is doubled off when the advance field shows `1X1`, `2X2` or `3X3` with a recorded baserunning out. The script (`sim/target/codex-probes/flyread/retro_flies.py`, throwaway, outside the repository's tracked files) reproduces the runner-timing note's liner count exactly (208 runners, 25.7 per 300 games).

| Retrosheet `hittype`, fielder | Catches | Runners doubled off | Per 300 games |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Fly (F), outfielder | 5,895 | 30 (0.5% of catches) | 3.7 |
| Fly (F), infielder | 192 | 3 | 0.4 |
| Liner (L), outfielder | 1,868 | 29 (1.6%) | 3.6 |
| Liner (L), infielder | 728 | 179 (24.5%) | 22.1 |
| Pop-up (P), infielder | 2,113 | 9 | 1.1 |

| Outfield catch, 0–1 out | Chances | MLB |
|---|---:|---|
| Runner on third: scored | 1,572 | 79.6%; put out at home 2.1%; 0.257 sacrifice flies per team-game |
| Runner on second, third empty: advanced to third | 2,431 | 30.2%; doubled off 0.6% |
| Runner on first, second empty: advanced to second | 4,222 | 2.5%; doubled off 0.9% |

The sim's 25–50° band is closest to Retrosheet's fly label, so its MLB double-off count is about **4 per 300 games**. Retrosheet labels by eye and the sim by launch angle, so a few of the sim's 25–50° catches are MLB liners.

### The sim after steps 1–2

The cohorts are direct seeds 0–299 and 1000–1299, which are `runner_timing`'s and `sf_probe`'s. Outfield tag-ups come from a throwaway `zz_tag_probe`, kept in `sim/target/codex-probes/flyread/`.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Runners doubled off at 25–50°, per 300 games | 38.0 | **0** (0 of 1,479 catches) | about 4 (fly label) |
| Runners doubled off at 10–25°, per 300 games | 18.0 | 13.5 | 3.6 OF liners; 25.7 all liners |
| Runner on second, outfield catch at 25–50°: advanced to third | 0 of 85 (100 games) | 0 of 458 | 30.2% |
| Runner on first, outfield catch at 25–50°: advanced to second | 1 of 111 (100 games) | 1 of 762 | 2.5% |
| Runner on third, outfield catch: scored | 49.3% | 56.7% | 79.6% |

So the double-offs went from far above MLB to slightly below it. Runners on second and first held partway on nearly every outfield fly and never tagged. At the catch, the runner on second was off the bag on 85 of 88 25–50° catches (82 of 85 on main). The 80% return cut-off decides this: at 0.5 s his read of a routine deep fly, averaged over his ±2σ read paths, rarely reaches 80%.

### Sweeps (100 games, seeds 0–99)

The runner's return cut-off (tag) and advance cut-off (go) were swept with a temporary override:

| Tag / go | Runner on second on the bag at a 25–50° catch | Advanced to third | Doubled off at 25–50° | Doubled off at 10–25° |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.8 / 0.2 (as built) | 3 of 88 | 0 | 0 | 3 |
| 0.6 / 0.2 | 56 of 85 | 7 (8%) | 0 | 3 |
| 0.5 / 0.2 | 74 of 86 | 13 (15%) | 0 | 3 |
| 0.8 / 0.4 | 3 of 85 | 0 | 2 | 6 |
| 0.8 / 0.2 with the holding relook | 36 of 88 | 2 (2%) | 0 | 3 |
| 0.5 from second only / 0.2, with the holding relook | 82 of 86 | 16 (19%) | 0 | 3 |

- **The advance cut-off (20%) stands.** At 40% the 25–50° double-offs come back near MLB (2 in 100 games, about 6 per 300). The outfield liners also rise to 6 doubled off in 64 catches (9%), against MLB's 1.6%. The 25–50° shortfall is about 4 per 300 games, a mistake real runners make on 0.5% of outfield fly catches with runners on. It is left; see open items.
- **The return cut-off from second is lowered to 50%** (`RUNNER_TAG_FROM_SECOND` [E]). MLB runners on second tag and advance on 30.2% of outfield catches (37.3% with none out, 25.9% with one). Run values agree. From second with none out, a tag that gains third is worth about a quarter of a run, and a ball that drops costs the runner little from the bag, since he scores from second on most hits that fall deep. So tagging pays from about even odds. With one out the gain is smaller, but MLB runners still advance on a quarter of these catches, so one value serves both. The sim's tag advance reaches 19%, not 30%. The race limits it: of the runners who tag, the throw beats most. That is the same limit as the sacrifice fly from third, traced to throws that all leave at the top-10% arm speed ([tag-up note](2026-11-14-tag-ups.md)).
- **The return cut-off from first and third stays at 80%.** From first, MLB runners advance on only 2.5% of outfield catches, so a partway hold is worth more on a ball that drops. From third the runner already gets back to tag when an advance could compete (§6.3).

## Open

- **Double-offs on flies, about 4 per 300 games short of MLB.** No runner is doubled off a 25–50° catch now. MLB's 0.5% of outfield fly catches are runners who misjudge and cannot get back. With relooks every 0.5 s, the sim's runner always can on a 4–6 s fly. Raising the advance cut-off brings them back, but it also multiplies the outfield-liner double-offs (§3, sweeps). A per-catch-type cut-off or a slower relook could separate the two. Not done.
- **Tag-ups from second reach 20%, against 30%; sacrifice flies 57%, against 80%.** Both are the race: every sim throw leaves at the top-10% arm speed ([tag-up note](2026-11-14-tag-ups.md)).
- **Long-hanging liners in the plate cue.** Liners still up after 3–4 s miss by 20–40 m when flown back with the typical spin, against 18–23 m before. This is 70 balls in 48 games, the 10–25° cells where the fitted unseen spin is largest. The tracking read corrects it with looks, and the routine-fly rates did not move.
- **The tables are fitted to the median carry per cell, in open air.** A fly that ends in the stands or over the fence is in the fit, and the in-park reads come out 0.0–0.4 m short (§1). The fit's last sweep moved one cell by 700 rpm. A cell with 1–2 balls (55°, 100 mph and up) holds whatever those balls gave.
- **The run environment.** Runs fell 0.19 on seed 0 and 0.03 on seed 1000 ([ledger](../metrics-ledger.md)). Part of the fall is fewer liner hits (.683 → .670 [.686 → .683]), as fielders read liners without the long bias. Part is runners giving up advances by tagging or pulling up. The split between the two, and the difference between the cohorts, is not allocated.
