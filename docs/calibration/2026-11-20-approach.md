# The approach: fielders slow into the ball and come through it toward the throw (2026-11-20)

**Job.** The owner's Q32 and Q33 (agent-models §5.5, "The catch sets up the throw"): Q33 charged only motion away from the target, because sim fielders reached every ball at full speed and never slowed into it or rounded it, so charging the motion across the line cost about half a run. This job builds the approach (agent-models §5.2): a fielder with time in hand slows into the ball, and an outfielder with time to spare sets up behind a fly and comes through it; then it restores Q32's across term and its drift-accuracy term. Infielders are covered too (owner, 2026-11-20: their throws follow the same mechanics as outfielders'). Step 1 measures the sim as built and finds sources (§1, §3); §2 is why the first set-up behind a fly dropped catches; the spec is agent-models §5.2, §5.3 and §5.5; §5 measures what the change moves. Branch `work/approach`, from main `dce9769`.

Source tags as in the [throw-race note](2026-11-19-throw-race.md): `read` (page text read), `snippet` (search-engine summary only), `unsourced`.

## Summary

- **Sim fielders take a ground ball at a run.** 70% of infield ground-ball takes that lead to a throw, and 66% of outfield pickups, are made at 5 m/s or more, mostly across the line to the throw. An infielder's median time in hand is 0.07 s: he runs at the earliest point he can reach and arrives as the ball does (§1). Real infielders slow into the last steps and field the ball in position (§3).
- **So the across term of Q32 would cost** 0.37 s on a routine grounder, 0.73 s on the charged slow roller, 0.82 s on the backhand in the hole and 0.96 s on an outfield pickup running across (§1).
- **Outfielders who run a ball down going away had time to slow into it**: median 0.80 s in hand at 3–5 m/s away, 0.36 s at 5–7 m/s.
- **The set-up behind a fly dropped catches because its come-through was not timed to the ball** (§2): started from a fixed 2.5 m-from-rest estimate while he was still moving, at a fixed pace toward a point past the catch, the fielder reached the catch point 0.4–1.2 s early, ran on past it, and the ball fell behind him.
- **Sources** (§3): an elite infielder's backhand exchange of 0.60 s at 92% of his arm (Báez 2019, Statcast, read), which the hurry rule reproduces; coaching on slowing into the ball, rounding it or not, the slow roller on the run; no measurement of approach speeds or of an outfielder's pickup-to-release on a ball going away.

## 1. How fielders take a ball they throw (as built, main `dce9769`)

A throwaway trace (`BB_AP_TRACE`, `ap_trace.py`, never committed) logs every fielder's first throw after taking a batted or loose ball (not a thrown one): his velocity at the take against the line to that throw's target, the plant charged now (motion away only, Q33) and the plant Q32 would charge (motion away and across), the take-to-release time, whether he hurried, and the time he had in hand. **Time in hand** is how much sooner than the ball he could have been at the point where he took it: the ball's time there less his reach time from his state at his reaction gate (the movement model's estimate, `run_time_to`, running through at full speed); about zero means he got there as the ball did. The probe (`zz_ct_probe.rs`, from the catch-throw job) runs direct seeds 0–299 and 1000–1299 (both pooled). "Toward", "across" and "away" are by the larger part of his velocity along or across the line to the target; "standing" under 1 m/s. The probe's game measures reproduce the catch-throw note's final build exactly (seed 0: 4.273 runs a team-game, 1.023 triples).

What it shows:

- **Every fielder takes a ground ball at a run.** 70% of infield ground-ball takes and 66% of outfield pickups are made at 5 m/s or more, and most of that motion is across the line to the throw (infield 3,723 of 9,836 across at 5 m/s+, outfield 2,850 of 8,319). An infielder's median time in hand is 0.07 s: he runs at his earliest intercept, the earliest point of the ball's path he can reach, so he arrives as the ball does, at speed (agent-models §5.2: the intercept plan has him "arriving at speed", and only a chaser early for the ball brakes). Real infielders on a routine grounder slow into the last steps and take it in fielding position (§3).
- **That is why the across term cost so much (Q33).** Charged in full, it would add 0.37 s to the routine grounder, 0.73 s to the charged slow roller, 0.82 s to the backhand in the hole and 0.96 s to an outfield pickup made running across at 5 m/s+ (the mean plant on all outfield pickups 0.17 → 0.56 s; on infield ground balls 0.07 → 0.44 s).
- **Outfielders running a ball down going away had time to slow into it.** At 3–5 m/s away the median time in hand is 0.80 s, at 5–7 m/s 0.36 s: by the braking cost of §4 that is enough to arrive 2–4 m/s slower, which saves 0.2–0.6 s of plant. Those are the triples (catch-throw note §5).
- **Fly catches are mostly made with time in hand** (median 1.1–2.2 s in every class but the fastest), so the across term would cost little there once the set-up and arriving under control apply: 0.22 s on average as caught now, almost all on catches across or away at 5 m/s+ (557 + 298), which are the late ones (0.4 s in hand).
- **The infield cases.** The slow roller is already thrown on the run: 42% of them hurried, 0.55 s from take to release. The backhand is released in 0.64 s with no plant charged now (its motion is across, not away); with the across term in full it would owe 0.82 s or a hurried, off-balance throw, which is what Báez's elite 0.60 s at 92% of his arm looks like (§3).

## 2. The set-up behind a fly: why it dropped catches

The catch-throw job built the set-up behind a fly (`2f39851`, `4ebf724`, `050cc39`) and took it out (`66911ff`): fly-ball hits .097 → .104 and pop-up hits .004 → .016 over 300 games. Its last version was restored on this branch's base for a throwaway probe (`setup-restored-probe.diff`, with an off switch), and `bip_outcomes` found the first ball in play that differed in each of 100 games: 15 games differed: in 12 the set-up turned a fly ball out into a hit (launch 35–54°, an outfielder with a runner on), in 2 a hit into an out, and in 1 the first difference was another ball. Three were traced (`trace_play`, with a log of each set-up decision):

| Play | Fielder | Came through at (ball down by his read) | At the catch point | Where the ball came down |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| seed 53, play 23 | CF | 3.72 s (5.09 s), already moving in at 3.7 m/s | 4.45 s, 0.63 s early, moving in at 3.8 m/s | at 5.18 s, 4.4 m behind him |
| seed 48, play 86 | LF | 4.99 s (6.10 s) | 5.72 s, 0.37 s early | at 6.20 s, 2.6 m behind him |
| seed 11, play 132 | RF | 4.25 s (5.62 s) | 4.45 s, 1.2 s early | at 5.71 s, behind him |

**Why.** The old rule started him through when the time left was a run of 2.5 m from rest at the 4 m/s pace (about 1.3 s with τ = 1 s), whatever he was doing, and sent him at a point 3 m past the catch point at a flat 4 m/s:
- he was seldom standing on his spot: he was still moving in toward it (within the 1 m that counted as there), often at 3–4 m/s already, so the run took a fraction of the time it was budgeted;
- nothing timed the come-through to the ball, so arriving early he ran on past the catch point toward the base, and the ball fell behind him. Once past it his reads put the catch out of reach (an intercept 30 m away on the ground), so he was no longer under it at all;
- on the way to the spot the decision flipped between the spot and the catch point every tick (seed 11, 2.41–2.73 s), because the test for going there and the test for staying differed.

**The fix** (agent-models §5.5 and §5.3): the start is timed from his actual distance to the catch point; coming through, his pace is his distance to his latest read of the catch point over the time left (recomputed every tick, at most his top speed), so he arrives with the ball and never passes the point first; once he has chosen the spot he keeps it while the time allows (no flipping). §5 measures it.
