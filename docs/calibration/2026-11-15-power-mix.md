# Who is missing the 105+ mph contact: the batter population or the contact model (2026-11-15)

Branch `work/power-mix`, from main `f9078e2`. A measuring job: no sim behaviour changed. Follows [ev-la-bins §5](2026-11-15-ev-la-bins.md) ("batter-level mix", the next cut). Tools: example `sim/crates/bb_game/examples/ev_bat.rs` (one CSV row per fair ball: batter, his drawn BatSpeed, the sweet-spot speed at the executed contact instant, release speed, exit speed, launch angle, spray) and `analysis/statcast/power_mix.py` (all tables below; usage in its header). The example reads the game's records and adds no sim code; its digest fold is the same on a re-run (seed 0: `ee562b47…`, seed 1000: `10c56db3…`) and the fair-ball counts equal ev-la-bins' (15,869 / 15,988), so every game is bit-identical to main.

## Summary for the lead

1. **The brief's premise is out of date: the sim's batters are not all the same man.** Since D19 each batter has his own drawn `BatSpeed` (§1). The sim's population spread (SD 2.78 mph) is the real one (SD 2.5 to 2.9 mph). Only bat speed varies; every other ability is grade 50.
2. **The shortage is in the contact model, not the population.** A real league-average batter (the middle fifth by mean bat speed, 70.8–72.1 mph) puts **10.3%** of his fair balls at 105+ mph (95% interval 9.1–11.6); the sim's batters of the same bat speed (70.9–72.3 mph) put **3.8%** (3.4–4.3). The sim's whole population gives 5.5%; the whole real sample 10.5%. So the sim's middle batter is 6.5 points short of the real middle batter, while the whole-population gap is 5.0 points: the sim's own spread of batters *adds* 1.7 points (3.8 → 5.5) where the real spread adds 0.2 (10.3 → 10.5). Contact model ≈ 130% of the gap, population ≈ −30% (§3).
3. **100+ and 110+ say the same:** 100+ is 26.9% (real average batter) against 17.6% (sim average batter); 110+ is 1.3% against 0.2%.
4. **Within the contact model, about half is bat speed at contact and half is exit speed at a given bat speed.** Real average batters' competitive swings meet the ball at a mean of 72.1 mph (SD 4.1, p90 76.8) against the sim's 71.3 (SD 3.3, p90 75.6): about 0.8 mph slower and a top 1.2 mph shorter. On balls squared up to at least 0.9 of the maximum (ball speed ÷ (1.23 × bat + 0.23 × pitch)) real exit speed is **higher at every bat speed, by 0.9 to 2.7 mph** (§4). The sim's squared-up share (31–33%) matches real (32–34%): its good contact is as frequent, and about 2 mph weaker.

## 1. What the sim's batters are

- A batter's `BatSpeed` is drawn per player per game: `Population::bat_speed` (`bb_game/src/population.rs:39`), a truncated-normal grade (z truncated at ±3, line 34–36) mapped by `linear_from_grade` (`bb_agents/src/attributes.rs:5`) with μ = 32.0 m/s = 71.6 mph (`attributes.rs:22`) and σ_pop = 1.25 m/s per 10 grade points = 2.8 mph (`attributes.rs:26`; owner ruling D19, PLAN §1). `Game::new` writes it into each batter's matchup (`bb_game/src/game.rs:238`).
- Everything else about a batter is the shared default: `BatterGrades::default()` is grade 50 on every other attribute (`attributes.rs:68`), `bats` and handedness come from the roster, and the module header says so (`population.rs:1-3`).
- So the sim's exit-speed distribution is a **mixture of average-everything batters who differ only in bat speed**, with the real between-batter SD. Measured over the 600 games (10,609 batter-games): drawn BatSpeed mean 71.61, SD 2.78 mph.
- Per-swing bat speed varies around the drawn value by the effort draw plus timing and placement noise: within-batter SD of contact bat speed 3.46 mph in the sim, against 4.22 in the real competitive swings (and the swing test's target 3.2 at a fixed depth).

## 5. Conclusion for the lead

**It is the contact model.** The real league-average batter hits 10.3% of his balls at 105+ mph and the sim's average batter (matched on bat speed) 3.8%; the two *populations* differ by 5.0 points and the average batters by 6.5 (interval about 5 to 8). The sim already has the real spread of batters (SD 2.78 against 2.5–2.9 mph). Giving it more or more varied power hitters would not close the gap: its top third already supplies a larger share of its 105+ balls (73%) than the real top third does (54%), and the 105+ share of the sim's best fifth (15.1%) is already near the real best fifth's 18.5%. What is missing is at every level, most in the middle. Two parts, roughly half each: **(a)** the real batter meets the ball at a higher bat speed (+0.8 mph mean, +1.2 mph at p90, a wider within-batter spread of 4.1 against 3.3 mph); **(b)** at the same bat speed and the same squared-up ratio, real balls leave 1 to 3 mph faster (up to 4 at p90). The sim's lower exit speed at 100+ comes with lower and flatter launch angles in the average batter too.

**Uncertainty.** The 105+ shares are cluster-bootstrap intervals (§3); the real sample is twelve dates, and a fifth holds about 1,670 balls and 172 balls at 105+. The two halves of the contact model (bat speed vs. collision) are rough (±0.5 mph); I did not split the p90 gap by a controlled sim run.

**What I did not check.**
- Whether the (b) gap is the collision itself (efficiency q, the sloped barrel) or the sim's *contact location* (where on the barrel the ball meets the bat, i.e. its quality at the same squared-up ratio); the squared-up ratio does not separate them, and the sim's offset distribution was not compared with a real measure.
- Pitch speed at the plate (the squared-up ratio uses release speed on both sides; the means match, but the spread of pitch speed was not compared).
- The sim's per-pitch environment: park, altitude, temperature (exit speed is mildly sensitive to air density; the real sample spans 30 parks).
- Whether the real 7–8% of slow swings (under 60 mph) would be absent from a "competitive" real sample: dropping them raises the real average batter's 105+ share a little and widens the gap.
- Real sample size and dates: twelve dates; no new Savant data was fetched. Savant's `bat_speed` is a tracked quantity with its own measurement error, which is the same on both sides of §2 but not of §3's sim side.
- Handedness and platoon splits.
