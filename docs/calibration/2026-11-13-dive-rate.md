# Dive rate: is it believable? (owner backlog A5, 2026-11-13)

**Judgment: no. Fielders dove about ten times as often as real ones.** Fixed by changing when a fielder leaves his feet, not by a rate: 22 dives a game became 3.5.

## What real fielders do

Sports Info Solutions charts every dive:

- A team's shortstops dive 36 times in 162 games and get an out on 22% of them ([SIS, Correa](https://www.sportsinfosolutions.com/?p=1965)). That is 0.44 a game for both teams.
- Second basemen get an out on 24% of their dives; one second baseman dove 140 times in three seasons ([SIS, All-Scrapes Team](https://www.sportsinfosolutions.com/2025/04/16/stat-of-the-week-the-all-scrapes-team/)).
- The right fielder who dove most over 2022–2024 dove 30 times and caught 22; the average right fielder catches 54% of the balls he dives for (same article).

SIS's infield figures count **outs**. The probe's "caught" below means the diver has the ball in his glove on the dive; a ground ball he stops and cannot throw in time counts as caught here and as a failure there. The two percentages are not the same measure.

No league total is published. From these, a game has roughly 2 to 3 dives (an estimate, not an acceptance band) by both teams together (my estimate: about 1.5 by the four infielders, well under 0.5 by the outfielders, almost none by the battery).

## What the sim did (100 games, seeds 0–99, `examples/dive_probe.rs`)

22.1 dives a game, on 29% of balls in play, 61% caught whatever the distance or the ball's speed.

| | sim before | real (both teams) |
|---|---|---|
| SS | 4.38 | 0.44 |
| P | 3.97 | about 0 |
| three outfielders | 8.22 (6.1 of them at ground balls) | well under 0.5 |
| all | 22.1 | 2 to 3 |

Four causes, each visible in the listed dives:

1. **He dove at balls he was about to pick up.** The test for "it won't come closer" used where he stood, not where he was running to. 4.3 dives a game were at balls under 10 m/s, many by a fielder running down a roller.
2. **The dive was instant.** The glove reached 2.5 m the moment the ball came that close, so 65% of dives were at 2.25–2.5 m, and a pitcher could be laid out 2.3 m to his side 0.35 s after contact.
3. **Near misses became dives.** A fielder runs to the earliest point he can reach, so small errors leave him 1.0–1.5 m from the ball as it passes. A real fielder takes those with a stretch on his feet.
4. **Outfielders dove for everything**, though a ball past an outfielder rolls to the wall. A full-stretch dive was as likely to succeed as a short one, so nothing told him not to.

## What changed (`bb_game::live`, agent-models §5.3–§5.4)

- **A dive must gain something** (`Sim::dive_gains`, from his own read and his own running). A ball in the air: he dives unless he can catch it on his feet before it lands. A ball on the ground: only if staying on his feet would get him the ball later than the 1.5 s get-up.
- **A dive takes its lunge time.** He can be laid out only as far as the time since his read first said "only a dive gets this" allows (1.5 m in `DIVE_LUNGE_S` 0.3 s). The pitcher cannot lay out until `PITCHER_RECOVER_S` 0.7 s [E] after contact. The glove meets the ball where it passes closest.
- **A stretch on his feet** (`STRETCH` 0.4 m [E] past his 1.0 m reach). He stays up; it costs `STRETCH_COST_S` 0.3 s [E] before he can throw (the spec's off-balance footwork, §5.5), not 1.5 s.
- **The further out, the harder the catch** (`K_EXTEND` 1.5 [E] added to the glove σ multiplier at full extension). An average glove gets about 60% of balls just past reach and about 30% at full stretch.
- **An outfielder weighs it** (`OF_DIVE_MIN_P` 0.5 [E]). He knows his own hands; below an even chance he stays on his feet. Infielders, with the outfield behind them, always go. This is the "block the single, don't dive" item the task list called A18.

## Result

Numbers are for the build after the Codex review's fixes (the outfielder weighs a dive by his own read, and goes once he has decided).

Dives (300 games, seeds 1000–1299): **3.3 a game**, 41% caught. SS 1.17, 3B 0.59, 2B 0.41, 1B 0.39, P 0.09, C 0.09, outfield 0.58. Seeds 0–299: 3.7 a game, 45% caught.

The shortstop still dives about 2.5 times as often as SIS counts. Every batter in a game is right-handed today (A1), which sends him more ground balls; the rest is the near-miss effect in cause 3. Not tuned further.

Run environment (300 games each):

| | seeds 0–299 before | after | seeds 1000–1299 before | after | MLB |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| runs/team-game | 5.48 | 4.73 | 5.71 | 4.89 | about 4.5 |
| GB hit | .425 | .333 | .433 | .343 | .24 |
| FB hit | .131 | .162 | .143 | .138 | .13 |

Neither runs nor ground-ball hits was a target of this change, and no parameter was set to reach them. Ground-ball hits are still above their band (.200–.280).

### The stretch is what moves the runs, and it is provisional

Seeds 0–299, everything else as built:

| | runs/team-game | GB hit | FB hit | dives a game |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `STRETCH` 0 (no stretch) | 5.60 | .396 | .170 | 7.5 |
| `STRETCH` 0.2 m | 5.29 | .360 | .170 | 5.3 |
| `STRETCH` 0.4 m (built) | 4.73 | .333 | .162 | 3.7 |
| `STRETCH` 0.6 m | 4.41 | .313 | .147 | 2.3 |
| 0.4 m, but costing a dive's 1.5 s | 5.13 | .364 | .153 | 3.6 |

- Runs fall about 0.2 for each 0.1 m of stretch. The result is sensitive to a length nobody has measured.
- Of the 0.87 runs the stretch removes, about 0.4 comes from the quicker recovery (0.3 s against 1.5 s) and the rest from reach: the ball is taken on his feet at any catchable height, and earlier than a dive that needs lead time.
- Not validated: how often a real fielder takes a ball 1.0–1.4 m from his body on his feet, how often he holds it, and how late his throw is. Until that is checked against charted plays, treat the stretch's size and cost as placeholders and the lower run total as unconfirmed.

Fly-ball hits can rise (.131 → .162 on one seed set, flat on the other): outfielders no longer catch 60% of what they dive for.

The outfielder's even-chance rule ignores the inning, the score and who is backing him up. It will refuse the only chance at a would-be walk-off ball. A situational judgment is not built.
