## Mechanism changes made during this pass (calibration-targets §2.3 item 3)

Each change was driven by a diagnosed mechanism error, not a stat target.

1. **Release point in the batter's filter state.** The noisy release-point estimate was applied as a certain offset. Early observations then "explained" a 5 cm release error as a ~50 cm plate shift. Diagnosed by the spec's own validation case (near-zero noise ⇒ 2 mm error; normal noise ⇒ 14 cm error with 3.4 cm believed σ). The state is now (plate x/z, arrival, break deviation x/z, release x/z). After the fix, actual and believed errors agree (4.4 cm vs 3.4 cm).
2. **Break deviation states** (σ 6 cm [E]), entering the path as progress². Early flight cannot reveal late break; that is why breaking balls are hard to judge at commit.
3. **Persistent perceptual noise share** (10% of variance [E]): a per-pitch misjudgment the batter cannot average away. 50% was tried first and made fastballs look like changeups every few pitches (≈ 6.5 mph speed misjudgment), which is unrealistic.
4. **Smooth-pursuit limit** (1.2 rad/s ≈ 70°/s [E], Bahill & LaRitz): angular noise grows as (ω/ω_p)² above it. Without it the batter corrected almost every aim error in the last 100 ms.
5. **Batter model error of the pitcher's shapes** (agent-models §3.8 σ_0): spin axis 10°, rate 8%, efficiency 0.08, speed 0.6 m/s [E], drawn per PA in the cage. In-game adaptation (§3.8) will shrink it with exposure; that is the times-through-the-order mechanism.
6. **Bootstrap swing value** (agent-models §8, replaced by the learned `SwingW` table in M6): contact is half foul and half ball in play; contact probability is P(|vertical error| < 6 cm)·P(|timing error| < 25 ms) under the batter's own uncertainty plus its believed execution noise; ball-in-play value falls off 0.6 runs/m outside the heart (70% of the zone). The first version made uncertain batters passive, the opposite of real behavior.
7. **Hit-by-pitch body capsule** moved to a 0.64 m inner edge. At 0.46 m, HBP% was 11%. HBP% is now *below* band (0.2%) with no evasion modeled; agent-models §3.7 evasion plus a posed capsule set replaces this stand-in.
8. **Swing template aim offset** −0.6 cm (aim slightly under the ball's center) [E], authored against mean LA (§4.4).

## Addendum: in-game adaptation and sensitivity (later the same night)

**Adaptation (agent-models §3.8)** is implemented as `PitcherMemory`. Per pitch type it holds an exposure count n_k, with on-deck sightings weighted 0.3. The believed shape offsets move toward each noisy sighting at η/(1 + n_k), and the break-spread belief σ_∞ + (σ_0 − σ_∞)e^(−ηn) shrinks with exposure. Pitch type is identified after the fact [C].

- With σ_0 spin-axis error 10°, the times-through-the-order effect was within noise. At **15°** (now the default), contact per swing rises from **74.6% to 77.2%** between the first and third time through. With adaptation off there is no rise (74.8% → 74.3%), so the effect comes from the mechanism, not from drift. Test: `bb_game/tests/adaptation.rs` (2,000 simulated games). Fatigue does not exist yet, so this is adaptation alone, as §5.2 asks.
- B1 at the interim knobs: 12/20 in band, unchanged by the σ_0 change.

**Sensitivity (`sim-cli sensitivity`, 1,500 PA per point, common random numbers):** 9 of 10 attributes move their primary metric in the expected direction (BatSpeed → EV +2.3 mph per 10 grade points; Vision → contact +4.5 pp; Command → BB% −4.1 pp; Discipline → chase −1.2 pp; ArmSpeed → contact −1.4 pp). Flagged under §7.2 ("no dead attributes"):

- **SpinCapacity:** flat, slightly the wrong way. The batter's scouting starts from this pitcher's true shapes, so more break does not surprise it. Real batters partly expect league-average shapes; a league-prior blend in the scouting model would make extreme spin valuable.
- **SpinRecognition:** weak (−0.14 pp SwStr per 10 grade points). The spin cue's per-type signature separation (~0.5 units) is small against σ_s0.
- **QuickHands:** grades 30 and 50 give identical Contact% under common random numbers. Investigate whether commit time or swing width actually changes outcomes there.
- **BatSpeed slope** is 0.93 mph of EV per mph of bat speed, just under the §4.4 shape band (1.0–1.3).

**Main open question for the owner: the chase rate.** With persistent biases modeled as filter states, the batter's filter is now well calibrated about its own uncertainty, so it is rarely confidently wrong. Real hitters chase largely because they are confidently wrong. Two spec-consistent options:

1. Treat average hitters as somewhat overconfident: the filter underestimates its persistent biases (believed bias σ < true σ), or Discipline's `c_D` > 1 at grade 50.
2. Leave perception calibrated and let the learned `SwingW` table (M6) set the swing threshold from outcomes.

Option 1 is a modeling decision with gameplay consequences (it makes Vision and Discipline matter more), so it is left for the owner.
