# Tag-ups, outfield throws home and double-play pivots: measured values and the sim's (2026-11-14)

Research only. No sim code was changed. This note feeds the tag-up model that replaces the stale-speed
"running start" found in [the sacrifice-fly diagnosis](2026-11-14-throw-choices.md#diagnosis-why-sacrifice-flies-fell-by-three-quarters-2026-11-14-worksf-diagnosis),
and the double-play pivot work. Every number is tagged with where it came from and how far to trust it.

**How I read sources.** `read` means the page text came back and I (or a fetch agent) read the number
in it. `snippet` means the number appears only in a search-engine summary of the page and I could not
open the page: **mlb.com refuses automated fetches (HTTP 406), so every mlb.com time below is a
snippet.** `image` means the page shows it only in a chart. `unsourced` means I found nothing; the
sim's value there is a guess, and so would be any value I gave. Confidence is high / medium / low.

## Summary

- **The runner side of the sim is about right; the old 3.14 s was wrong.** A third-to-home tag-up
  from the catch should take about 3.9–4.3 s for an average runner. The sim's 4.19 s sits inside that;
  the pre-A4 3.14 s (a 25 % "running start" nobody has a reason for) is faster than a Statcast-timed
  *non-tag-up* dash from a lead (3.34 s). A fit of the exponential start to a measured 90-ft split
  suggests the sim's runner is a little **slow** (4.19 s against about 4.03 s for a standing start), not fast.
- **The ball side is plausible but its parts are unmeasured.** Measured outfield throw flight times
  fit the sim's physics. No published number exists for the outfielder's catch-to-release (the sim
  uses 0.85 s) or for the catcher's receive-and-tag; both are guesses.
- **The sim's sacrifice-fly *opportunities* look high, and its outcomes were low even before A4.**
  MLB runners sent on a fly with a runner on third and fewer than two out score 94–97 %, are sent about
  80 % of the time, and are thrown out 2–3 %. Overall that is about 75–80 % of such fly balls scoring.
  The sim was at 56 % before A4 and 16 % after, **but** the sim count includes infield pop-ups and the
  sample is not restricted to outfield catches, so the 56 % is not a like-for-like miss (see §4).
- **The sim's double-play pivot (0.455 s by the formula) is faster than any measured pivot I could
  find (0.67 s on one elite play; coaching norms 0.7–0.9 s; Statcast exchange averages around 1.0–1.2 s,
  unconfirmed)**, which is the likeliest source of its excess double plays.
- **No source gives measured first-to-second or second-to-third tag-up times, sliding-runner effects on
  a pivot, or total 6-4-3 times.** These stay unsourced.

### Verdict on the runner

- A standing-start 90 ft, average sprinter: **about 4.03 s** (Olszewski mean, medium confidence) up to
  **4.19 s** if the sim's curve is right. The first step is included in the Statcast split; the catch-to-home
  clock adds at most the runner's reaction to the glove closing, which the rolling-start technique is built to cut to about zero.
- So **a tag-up from the catch should be roughly 3.9–4.3 s for an average runner**, about 3.7–3.9 s for an
  elite one. Taylor's 3.88 s "as quickly as" and the 3.67 s best 90-ft split bracket the fast end.
- **3.14 s is not a tag-up time.** It is 0.2 s quicker than the quickest non-tag-up third-to-home dash from a lead on the page (3.34 s) and 0.5 s quicker than Taylor's best tag-up.

## 4. What the sim does today

(All in `sim/crates/bb_game/src/live.rs` at main `d1f6b46`.)

| Runner start | `run_dist(t) = vmax·(t − τ(1 − e^(−t/τ)))`, `RUNNER_TAU` = 0.85 s (`play.rs:191`); standing 27.432 m takes **4.19 s** at 8.2 m/s | see §1 |

**Opportunities.** In 100 games (seeds 0–99, `sf_probe`) the sim has 0.49 fly outs per team-game with a runner on third and fewer than two out (98 after A4, 108 before, of 200 team-games), against about **0.22 per team-game** in MLB's BP eligible set (10,699 over ten seasons of 4,860 team-games). ## 5. Where the sim's numbers are off, and which side is wrong

| Sim | Measured | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Runner 3rd to home from the catch: **4.19 s** (standing); the 3.14 s in the old rule | about 3.9–4.3 s average, 3.7–3.9 s elite (medium–low); average 90-ft split 4.03 s | **4.19 s is right to within about 0.15 s, if anything slightly slow.** **3.14 s was wrong** (too fast by a second). The old *outcomes* depended on the wrong number |
| Home-to-first 4.45 s (average) | 4.26–4.30 s (2016, medium–high) | sim **about 0.15 s slow**; the same sign as the 90-ft comparison above. Fitting τ to the Olszewski mean (vmax 8.2) gives τ ≈ 0.68 s (standing 90 ft = 4.02 s); a τ change would shift every runner in the game, so it is a separate decision |
| Ball to home in the runner's head, 3.5–4.7 s from the catch | measured flight 2.5–3.0 s for 270–300 ft at 90–100 mph, plus an exchange of unknown length (0.5–1.0 s?) plus the catch | **cannot tell which is wrong**: the sim's parts (0.75 s believed / 0.85 s actual transfer, 37 m/s believed arm) are inside any plausible range. The measured parts (flight) agree; the unmeasured part (transfer) carries the whole question |
| Runner's belief of the arm: 37 m/s | actual averages 39–40.6 m/s | the belief is weak by one standard grade; **runners are too cautious by ~0.2 s** on a typical throw |
| Pivot 0.455 s (double plays) | see §6 | sim **too fast** by 0.2–0.7 s |
| Tag time 0 s + 0.3 s hold requirement | 0.25 s quoted (low) | roughly equal if the hold counts; **unverified** whether the 0.3 s delays the out's timestamp |

So: **the runner's 4.19 s is not the problem.** What the old 3.14 s did was make the 3.8–4.6 s the runner believed the ball needs *look* beatable. At 4.19 s he needs the ball to be at least 0.35 s slower than him, i.e. roughly 4.5 s, to go. MLB runners go on about 80 % of sacrifice-eligible flies, and almost all of those that are sent are safe, so MLB runners are not racing the ball at all on most plays: they go when the fly is deep. **The most important missing piece is the depth dependence**, which a flat margin test on a 4.19 s runner cannot reproduce unless the ball side (transfer + flight) is slowed and lengthened with depth the way the real throw is (a 300 ft throw is ~3.0 s of flight and ~1.0 s of work on top).

## 6. Double-play pivots

The sim turns 52.5 % of double-play grounders against about a third in the majors; its pivot (catch to release) is the receiver's `0.7 × transfer`, **0.455 s** for a grade-50 infielder (`RELAY_TRANSFER_FRAC` × 0.65 s; the 0.43 s quoted may be a different grade or play).

## 7. The numbers a tag-up model (and a pivot model) should reproduce

Targets, in the order of confidence.

**League counts (high)**
- Sacrifice flies **1,150–1,310 a season, 0.24–0.27 per team-game, 0.6–0.7 % of PA** (2015–2025, without 2020). The sim should land near 0.25 per team-game (150 in 600 team-games). For the sim's own run environment (5.07 per team-game against 4.5) a rate up to 0.3 is not wrong.

**Outcomes of a fly ball caught with a runner on third and fewer than two out (medium)**
- Scores **75–80 %**, held **17–23 %**, thrown out at the plate **2–3.5 %** (about 3 % in total double plays). When sent, safe **94–97 %**. League send rate about 80–84 %.
- By depth: under about 225 ft (69 m) a hold; 225–275 ft (69–84 m) sent about 40 % and safe about 90 %; 250–275 ft sent about half of the time, safe 5 in 6; 275–300 ft (84–91 m) sent about 90 %, safe 19 in 20; beyond 350 ft (107 m) always.
- Center field: hold 12 % against 20 % for a corner; success when sent 1.5 points higher.
- A runner with a 3.7 s 90-ft split is sent ~70 % of the time on 225–275 ft flies and is always safe; at 4.3–4.4 s sent only ~28–30 %.

**The runner (medium–low)**
- Third to home, **from the catch**: about **3.9–4.3 s** average runner (4.03 s standing 90-ft mean from the third-base sample), **3.7–3.9 s** fast. Keep the exponential start but check τ: a fit to 4.03 s needs τ ≈ 0.68 s at vmax 8.2 m/s, and home-to-first of 4.26–4.30 s needs it too (sim 4.45 s).
- No credit for a running start unless he is physically in motion toward the next base at the catch. A rolling start with the back foot off the bag is a real technique, but its gain has no published measurement; **model it as an explicit, named [E] parameter or leave it out; do not recover it from a stale speed.**
- First to second and second to third tag-ups: no data; they follow from the same start curve over 27.4 m, but the second-to-third decision depends on the ball (a throw to third is shorter).

**The ball (medium for flight, unsourced for the rest)**
- Flight time of a throw home from the catch point: 200 ft 1.8 s, 260 ft 2.1 s (extreme arm), 280 ft 2.5–2.6 s, 300 ft 2.8 s, 325 ft 3.1 s at 92–110 mph releases. At average Savant arm strength (87.1 / 89.6 / 90.5 mph) add roughly 0.1–0.3 s: 275 ft ≈ 2.7–3.0 s. A one-hop throw is within about 50 ms of an air throw when the bounce is chosen well.
- Release speed by position from Savant 2025 means (87.1, 89.6, 90.5 mph) — the sim already matches these.
- Catch-to-release for an outfielder, throw accuracy at the plate, cut-off rate, catcher's receive/tag time: **no measurement**; keep the sim's 0.85 s and 0.7 × transfer as flagged [E] guesses and say so in the model's spec. Quoted tag time 0.25 s (low).

**Double plays (medium)**
- Turned on **40 %** of infield grounders with a runner on first and fewer than two out (shortstop 54.1 %, second base 44.7 %, third base 37.8 %, first base 21.5 %, pitcher 19.6 %, catcher 3.8 %); by exit velocity 33–38 % between 71 and 110 mph and under 10 % at ≤60 mph.
- Pivot (catch to release): **0.67 s** for the best of the day, **0.7–0.9 s** typical coaching norm; total contact to first **4.0–4.5 s** for a successful 6-4-3, with the batter arriving in 4.3 s on average; a double play is gone if the first out takes too long to leave the pivot man. No measured effect of a slide: add it as a named parameter if built, otherwise state its absence.
