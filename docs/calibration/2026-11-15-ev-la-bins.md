## Summary for the lead

1. **The deficit is almost entirely a population deficit.** Per fair ball in play the sim hits 2.61% home runs and the Savant sample 4.54% (a gap of 1.93 points). Reweighting the sim's bins to the real shares and keeping the sim's per-bin HR rates closes **106%** of the gap (reverse order, real rates on sim shares: 97%). Within a bin the sim's home-run rate is as high as real or higher; the largest rate-term cells are the sim's 105+ mph balls, which go out more often than real ones.
2. **Within the population deficit, exit speed is about twice the launch angle.** Averaging the two orders of the split: exit speed ≈ **1.36 points (67% of the population term)**, launch angle given exit speed ≈ **0.68 (33%)**. They do not separate cleanly: exit speed first gives EV 0.84 / LA 1.20; launch angle first gives LA 0.16 / EV 1.89. The balls the sim lacks are high-exit-speed balls *at* 20–40°; its hard-hit balls are hit flatter (of balls at 100+ mph, 31% are at 20–40° in Savant against 25% in the sim).
3. **The exit-speed upper tail is the shortfall:** 100+ mph is 25.2% of real fair balls against the sim's 18.8%; 105+ is 10.2% against 5.5%; 110+ is 1.9% against 0.8%. The sim has *more* balls than real at 90–100 mph (39.2% against 29.8%). The launch-angle spread is close (SD 28.0° against 28.9°); the sim's mean is lower (8.9° against 11.9°) from more balls below 0° (35.2% against 31.6%) and fewer popups at 50°+ (5.9% against 9.4%).
4. **Direction is badly off, but it is not what shows up as a rate deficit in the grid.** In the home-run bins (95+ mph, 20–40°) only 9% of sim balls go to the pull third against 38% real, and the spray SD is 13.8° against 23.7°. Re-weighting the sim's per-third HR rates to the real thirds lifts its rate in those bins from 23.6% to about 28%, roughly 0.5 points of all balls, about a quarter of the gap (rough, §4). The grid's rate term does not show this because the sim's balls carry far enough in the centre and opposite thirds to offset it.

**Mechanism to look at next:** the exit-speed upper tail, specifically 100–110 mph contact at 20–40° (the collision's output for well-struck, sloped-barrel contact, or the batter ability distribution feeding it), not the launch-angle spread. The direction gap (PLAN D20) is the second, smaller item.

## 1. The data

**Sim.** The standard run, `sim-cli games --n 300` at `--seed 0` and `--seed 1000` (game seeds `seed × 1,000,003 + i`), main `82aa0d0`. Spray is made pull-positive (right-handed: −spray; left-handed, by the swing pivot's side: +spray); the sim's ground balls come out +14.7° pull-positive against Savant's +15.7°, so the sign is right. 15,869 balls and 413 HR (seed 0), 15,988 and 419 (seed 1000); 31,857 and 832 pooled. **Ledger reproduction:** HR/PA 1.776% (23,259 PA, seed 0) and 1.774% (seed 1000), ledger 1.78% [1.77%]; fair balls at 105+ mph 5.5% pooled (ledger 5.5%). Hard-hit (95+) is 39.1% here against the ledger's 38.2%; I did not chase the 0.9 points (probably a different denominator or a strict `>`). Only a new example was added to the sim (no source file touched), so every game is bit-identical to main; `cargo test --workspace --release` passes.

**Real.** Baseball Savant pitch-level CSVs, regular season 2025, the twelve dates [collision-targets §1](2026-11-14-collision-targets.md) used (Apr 8, Apr 22, May 6, May 20, Jun 3, Jun 17, Jul 1, Jul 22, Aug 5, Aug 19, Sep 2, Sep 16), 51,723 pitches, re-fetched 2026-11-14 with the commands in that note into `sim/target/data/savant/` (not committed). Filter: `description` starts `hit_into_play`, with `launch_speed` and `launch_angle` present: **9,165 balls, 416 home runs** (`events == home_run`). Spray (pull-positive) is that note's formula on `hc_x`, `hc_y`. Public data, a generic client name. Driveline OpenBiomechanics data is not used.

**Caveats on the real sample.** Twelve dates are about a tenth of a season: a cell with n real balls has a rate SD of about √(p(1−p)/n), so cells under 100 balls are ±5 points or worse, and the 110+ row has about 175 balls in all. The real balls come from 30 parks and real weather; the sim has one park and atmosphere, so a bin's rate difference mixes carry, direction and park. Savant's `hc_x/hc_y` for a home run is not documented, so real HR spray is the least certain number here.

## 3. Decomposition

Method (redoable from the two tables). Let s and q be the sim's and Savant's share of balls in a bin, r and h their HR per ball. Totals S = Σ s·r and R = Σ q·h; the gap is R − S.
- **Order 1:** population = Σ q·r − S (real shares, sim rates); rate = R − Σ q·r.
- **Order 2:** population = R − Σ s·h (sim shares, real rates); rate = Σ s·h − S.
- **Within the population term:** keep the sim's launch-angle distribution *given* exit speed and swap in the real exit-speed marginal (the EV part); the rest is launch angle given exit speed. Then the reverse (real launch-angle marginal first). The two orders disagree because the two variables are correlated, so the table also gives their average (Shapley).

Pooled sim against Savant (points of fair balls; the gap is 1.927):

| split | population | rate within bin |
|---|---|---|
| order 1 (real shares × sim rates) | 2.042 (106%) | −0.115 (−6%) |
| order 2 (sim shares × real rates) | 1.870 (97%) | 0.058 (3%) |

| population term split | exit speed | launch angle |
|---|---|---|
| exit speed marginal first | 0.844 | 1.198 (given exit speed) |
| launch-angle marginal first | 1.887 (given launch angle) | 0.155 |
| average of the two orders | **1.365 (67%)** | **0.677 (33%)** |

By seed: seed 0 alone gives population 111% / 101% (orders 1 / 2) and EV 1.357 / LA 0.783 by the average; seed 1000 gives 100% / 93% and EV 1.355 / LA 0.558. The sim's side is stable to about ±0.1 on the LA part. The real side's noise is larger (416 home runs, about ±5%) but common to every figure.

Largest cells of the rate term (real share × real-minus-sim rate): 105–110 mph at 20–25° (−0.13 points: real 46%, sim 59%), 105–110 at 30–35° (−0.10: 81% against 98%), 100–105 at 25–30° (−0.09: 54% against 60%). All negative: the sim's hardest balls leave more often than real ones in the same bin, consistent with the sim's carry or its single park, not with a carry shortfall. The sampling noise of a 52-ball real cell is bigger than its difference.

Bins: 95+ mph and 20–40° (nearly all home runs). Thirds of pull-positive spray: pull > +15°, centre ±15°, opposite < −15°.

Of the home runs themselves: Savant 67% pull, 20% centre, 12% opposite (n 386); sim 14% / 49% / 36% (n 807). Mean pull-positive spray by launch-angle band (Savant → sim): <0° +15.7 → +14.7; 0–10° +11.8 → +2.3; 10–20° +6.8 → −0.9; 20–25° +4.3 → −4.0; 25–30° +0.5 → −5.1; 30–35° +0.1 → −6.3; 40–50° −7.0 → −8.4. The sim's ground balls are pulled like real ones; everything above them is not, and from below 0° to 30–35° the sim falls 21° where Savant falls 16° (this sample's version of the targets note's 28° against 17°; bands differ).

Rough size: apply the sim's per-third HR rates to Savant's thirds in these bins: 0.38 × 36% + 0.40 × 18% + 0.23 × 32% = 28%, against the sim's own 23.6%. The sim's bins hold 10.8% of its balls, so about +0.5 points of all balls, a quarter of the gap, if the extra pull balls kept the sim's pull-third rate. It is a within-bin check confounded by exit speed, so read it as an order of magnitude.

## 5. What I did not check

- **Foul balls** (fair balls only, as in the targets note).
- **Park and weather:** I did not rebuild the sim with real wall distances, so "rate within bin ≈ 0" could hide a carry shortfall offset by a short fence, or the reverse.
- **Batter-level mix:** whether the missing 105+ balls come from the top of the batter distribution (no hitters with real power) or from every batter. This is the obvious next cut; the data to compare against is `savant_sd.py`'s between-batter bat-speed SD.
- **Savant's `hc_x/hc_y` definition** for fielded and home-run balls (§1).
- **Real-sample size:** twelve dates; a larger pull would tighten the 105+ cells.
- **Hard-hit 38.2% against 39.1%** versus the ledger (§1).
