# The read knows the ball started at the plate (2026-11-13)

Owner item 2 of 2026-11-13 ("the read knows the ball started at the plate, and the outfield
read values are refitted"), following docs/calibration/2026-11-13-popup-read-and-calls.md §1.
Spec: agent-models §5.1 ("The plate cue"), §5.7 (calls).

## 1. The problem

The interim read (agent-models §5.1) gave a fielder a noisy estimate of where the ball is
now and how it is moving, flown forward with his own physics. Its position error grows with
his distance from the ball (σ₀ = 3.0 m at 30 m for outfielders, ×3 in depth). At an
outfielder's first look, 0.35–0.4 s after contact, the ball is about 15 m from home and he
is about 90 m from it: one σ of depth error was 26 m, and one σ to the side 9 m. A ball
15 m from the plate cannot be 26 m deeper than it is or 9 m off its line; anyone who saw it
hit knows that.

## 2. The model

While a batted ball is on its first flight, `s` seconds after contact, the fielder knows
where it was hit and when. Drag, Magnus and gravity act on the ball's velocity alone, not
on its position, so he flies his read of its velocity **back** `s` seconds with his own
physics and the typical spin for the launch angle he reads (`live::position_from_plate`;
`FlightEnv::step` with a negative step). A ball going that fast now, having left the
contact point `s` seconds ago, must be there.

He weighs that position against his direct read in 3D by how good each is
(`plate_weighing`, a Kalman update; covariances in his line-of-sight frame):

| | per lateral axis | depth | height |
|---|---|---|---|
| direct read | σ_p = σ₀ · d/30 m / √(1 + view/τ) | × 3 | × ½ |
| plate | σ_v · s (σ_v = 0.04 · speed / √(1 + view/τ)) | × 1.5 | × ½ |
| plus, isotropic | `PLATE_SPIN_M` · s^1.5 = 1.5 m at 1 s | | |

The last term is the spin he cannot see, measured, not guessed (`examples/plate_cue.rs`, 48
games, true velocity flown back):

| miss, m (median) | 0.5 s | 1 s | 2 s | 3 s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| typical spin, 25–40° | 0.60 | 2.13 | 6.47 | 10.0 |
| typical spin, 55–90° | 0.68 | 2.08 | 4.29 | 4.99 |
| true spin | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |

So the backward flight is exact and all of its error is the spin belief. A 3D miss's median
is about 1.5 σ per axis, so 1.5 m per axis at 1 s, growing as s^1.5 (0.53 m at 0.5 s, 4.2 m
at 2 s). Line drives that stay up 3–4 s miss by more (18–26 m), but by then the ball is near
the fielder and his direct read has taken over.

After a bounce, a deflection or a wall, only the direct read is used, as before.

| | 4.0 s | 4.8 | 2.1 | 0.8 | 0.4 | 5.3 | 5.3 |
| 60°+ | 0.4 s | 0.0 | 0.0 | 6.0 | 3.6 | 7.0 | 26.2 |
| | 4.0 s | 5.5 | 2.6 | 0.9 | 0.4 | 6.2 | 7.7 |

("Before" is the old table at σ₀ 3.0; the new one is at the refitted 3.5.) What an
outfielder does not know at first is how hard and at what angle the ball was hit, about 7 m
of landing point; the direct read takes over in the last seconds, and a shallow pop-up he
watches from 30–40 m is still 5–6 m unsure, as before.

The call rule (§5.7) had to follow. A fielder calls a ball only when his time to spare
covers one σ of his own read's depth error, and that σ was the error of where the ball is
*now*, which was the whole story at 26 m. With the plate cue it is under a metre early, so
every outfielder was sure of every ball at first look and called pop-ups off infielders
again (test `an_outfielder_does_not_call_a_pop_up_he_cannot_be_sure_of`). The call's
self-knowledge is now where the ball comes **down** (`landing_depth_sigma`): the error of
where it is now, combined with his velocity read's depth error carried over the time left.

## 4. Refit (calibration-targets §2.3–§2.4)

- **Outfield position read σ₀ 3.0 → 3.5 m at 30 m.** Band: fly-ball hit rate (B2 BABIP by
  type, MLB ≈ .13). Mechanism: with the plate cue the early read is far better, so flies
  were too easy (.095). The direct read still decides the last seconds, where catches are
  lost. Sweep, seed 5, 1,024 games (before the call rule's velocity term):

  | σ₀ | runs | LD | FB | PU |
  |---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | 3.0 | 5.11 | .688 | .095 | .006 |
  | 3.5 | 5.34 | .707 | .122 | .009 |

  (Seed 2 at 3.0: 5.02 runs, FB .096.) 3.5 lands FB on the reference on three seed sets
  (.122, .131, .129); not bisected further.
- `READ_VEL_FRAC` (0.04) not changed: it is fitted to ground balls (B2 GB band), and GB hit
  rates did not move (.411 → .413, .406 → .409).

## 6. Tried and not kept

- **A learned prior of how far balls go**, by launch angle (mean and SD of distance from the
  contact point s seconds after contact, `d(s)/s`: pop-ups ≈ 28 m/s, liners ≈ 41 m/s, SD
  4–7 m/s), fused with the depth read only. Every hard-hit ball was read as a typical one
  until the read firmed up: liner triples .069 → .086 and runs +0.1 to +0.2, at σ₀ 3.5. His
  own read of the ball's speed is better (≈ 2.4 m/s in depth against 4–7 m/s), so the plate
  cue uses that.
- **The same along the depth axis only**, with his speed read: depth was right, but the
  lateral read was still 9–13 m off at first look (a ball 15 m from home read 30–40°
  off its line), and pop-ups to short right dropped three times as often (22 against 7 in 128 games).

## 7. Open

- The plate cue assumes the observer saw the contact point exactly. Runners read through the
  same function, so they get the cue too; their read value (`RUNNER_READ_SIGMA0`) was not
  refitted.
- The spin floor is one isotropic σ growing with time alone; the real miss is much larger
  for line drives that stay up 3–4 s (18–26 m).
- The run environment is still high (5.2–5.5 against MLB ≈ 4.5), from ground balls (.41
  against .24) and liner/ground-ball triples (§5 table); separate work.
