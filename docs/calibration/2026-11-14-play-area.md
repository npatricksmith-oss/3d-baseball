# The limits of the play area (owner backlog A10, 2026-11-14)

**Nothing was tuned.** The park now has a backstop, foul-territory walls (the front of the stands) and dugouts (physics-spec §9.5). A ball that leaves the field is dead, and the play ends at that fact. A thrown ball out of play gets the two-base award, from the time of the pitch or of the throw (rules-spec §7.2). Before, foul ground reached the outfield fence's 330 ft in every direction: a foul pop could be chased 100 m from the plate, and no throw could ever leave the field.

The reference park's geometry comes from sources where there are any: the backstop distance from OBR 2.01, and a side clearance that makes foul territory match the major-league average area. Nothing about it was chosen for its effect on the run environment.

## The park (physics-spec §9.5)

| | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Backstop | 60 ft behind the apex, 9.0 m screen | OBR 2.01 recommends 60 ft or more; screen height [E] |
| Side walls | 50 ft off the foul lines, 4 ft high | [E]; with the taper below, this gives the MLB-average foul area |
| Taper | from 100 ft along the line to the foul pole | [E] |
| Dugouts | openings in the side wall 20–80 ft along each line | [E], unsourced |
| Foul territory inside the walls | 23,885 sq ft | MLB average ≈ 23,300 (Beyond the Box Score, 2019); Oakland ≈ 40,700 |

## Why each number moved

Deltas are main `52d6632` → `980a7dc` (seeds 0–299 [1000–1299]), the ledger's pair; the branch-point pair `a69dd50` → `25fa2dd` is in brackets where it differs. (Corrected 2026-11-14 after the Gemini review P3: this section had quoted the branch-point pair as if it were the merged one.)

- **Foul outs fell by 1.5 [1.7] a game, and foul balls out of play rose by 10 a game.** Foul pops that used to be caught 30–100 m from the plate now leave over the stands wall, into the dugouts or over their roofs, or over the backstop screen. MLB had about 1.4 foul outs a game in 2017 and 1.8 in 2003 (FiveThirtyEight, "Foul balls are the pace of play problem nobody's talking about": season totals 3,450 and 4,372 over 2,430 games). The sim's 2.2 is still high, but much closer than 3.7–3.9 was.
- **Fouls rose by 2.2 [2.3] a game:** those same pops are now fouls instead of outs.
- **PU share fell from 11% to 9% of balls in play**, and every other type's share rose a little. A caught foul pop counts as a ball in play (a pop-up out); one in the seats is a foul. PU hits rose .011 → .019 [.015 → .021], the same direction on both sets: the caught foul pops were all outs. The other types' hit rates moved within noise.
- **Reached on BIP rose by 1.5 [0.5] points.** Composition alone predicts about +1: taking 1.6 always-out balls a game out of about 55.6 balls in play gives 18.07 / 54.0 = 33.5% from 32.5%. Measured 33.9% [33.2%].
- **K% rose by 1.0 on both sets and pitches by 4–5 a game.** A pop that was the last pitch of a PA is now a strike, and the PA goes on. **PA/team-game rose by 0.2** on both sets, from the fewer outs.
- **Runs/team-game rose by 0.01 [0.09]** (on `a69dd50`: 0.15 [0.35]). The outs alone predict about +0.22: 0.8 fewer outs per team-game, at about 0.28 runs per out avoided in this run environment. The thrown-ball awards add perhaps +0.03 more. The four pairs average +0.15, inside the noise of that (a 300-game mean has σ ≈ 0.13 runs/team-game, and the two builds diverge after their first changed play, so each pair is two independent samples).
- **HR/PA rose by 0.04 [0.12] points** (on `a69dd50`: 0.14 [0.43]). The extra PAs explain it: 0.8 caught foul pops a team-game are 2.1% of PAs, which now go on to other outcomes at about 5.1% home runs, +0.11 point. The merged pair's mean, +0.08, matches; the branch-point pair's larger rise is inside the noise of one pair (σ ≈ 0.2 points). (The first version of this note called the rise unexplained; the Gemini review P3 did the arithmetic on the merged pair, and it holds.)
- **Throws out of play: 0.11–0.18 a game**, all of them new. No MLB rate was to hand. Errors run about 0.5 per team-game and throwing errors about half of those, and only some throwing errors leave the field. This rate is plausible but unchecked.

The run environment is further from MLB's ≈ 4.5 than before. Per the brief, nothing was tuned to pull it back.

## After the Gemini review fixes (main `0a17409` → `27c2494`)

The [review](../reviews/2026-11-14-gemini-play-area-review.md) found four P1s, five P2s and a P3; its Resolution lists each. What changed in play: a ball or body centred on fair ground never meets the taper (P1-1); fielders see the walls, so no intercept past a wall beyond reach over it, and they brake for the nearest wall at 6 m/s² (P2-3); a throw over a wall is dead as it crosses (P2-2); a dugout has a 4-ft roof, over which a ball comes down in the seats instead of the dugout (P2-1; out of play either way); a ball that starts beyond a wall is out at once (P2-4). The rules fixes (P1-2 walk-off on an award, P1-3 the legal base at a throw, P1-4 a caught fly carried out) touch plays that are rare or that the sim cannot produce yet. ## Not done

- No protective netting above the side walls: a ball that would strike it goes into the seats. With netting to the far end of the dugouts, fewer foul pops would reach the dugouts.
- The outfield fence still meets the ball at its center, not its surface (3.7 cm off). A rolling ball still stops dead at any wall, as it did at the fence before.
- There is no glove-deflection fact yet. The engine has the "fair fly deflected out over foul ground" award (OBR 5.05(a)(8)), but the sim cannot produce that play.
- The client: `bb_ffi` does not decode the `BallDead` marker yet (data-formats §5.4). The client needs its time and exit point (in `BbPlayInfo`) before it can cut back to the field.
