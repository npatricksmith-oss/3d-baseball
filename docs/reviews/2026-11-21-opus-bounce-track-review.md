# Review: tracking across the bounces (`work/bounce-track`, 2026-11-21)

Reviewer: Claude Opus (independent; wrote none of the change). Range `519842c..fe9d5a0`. Files: `sim/crates/bb_game/src/live.rs`, `sim/crates/bb_physics/src/ground.rs`, `docs/agent-models.md` §5.1, `docs/calibration/2026-11-21-bounce-track.md`, `docs/metrics-ledger.md`, `observations.md`, `TASKS.md`. Probes under `sim/target/codex-probes/review-bounce/` (probe copies of main and of the branch, built into `sim/target/review-probes`).

**Summary.** No P1. The world's roll is bit-for-bit unchanged (200 game digests), the change is deterministic, first-flight looks keep their keys, nothing the fielder reads is world state beyond what he sees, and the standard run matches the ledger row exactly. The filter's propagation is right where I checked it against Monte Carlo. The main problem is the gate: it fires about 15 times as often as the note and spec say, nearly always on a track whose position is still good, and the snapshot read it falls back to is sometimes tens of metres worse (P2-1). The rest is P3: an unsourced spin σ that is too small for some balls, a rolling-estimate stop that cannot recover, honesty figures that drift on other seeds, a cause line that leaves out the ground clamp, and stale comments.

## Findings

### P1

None. Checked: world roll refactor (bit-identical), invariants 2, 3 and 5, test suite, standard run (Verification).

### P2

**P2-1. The gate ends good tracks about 1.4 times a game, not "9 in 10 games, all rolling", and the snapshot read it falls back to can be far worse than the track it drops.** `live.rs:1275` (gate), `live.rs:3330` (track dropped), `docs/agent-models.md:434`, note §2 and §3 item 3.
- *How I know.* Probe copy of the branch with one `eprintln` at the gate (env-gated; digests identical to the branch on all 200 games). Game seeds 0–199: **272 gate ends in 111 plays** (1.36 a game): 239 rolling, **33 on hops**. In games 0–9 it is 9, all rolling, as the note says; that sample was too small to stand for the rate or for "none on hops". At the moment of the gate the track's error along his line of sight is small (median 0.68 m, p90 3.95 m); what is off is his velocity (estimate/true speed median 0.87; on hops 0.6–0.8, the first look 0.01 s after the bounce). The snapshot read that replaces it has median 0.06 m but p90 9.0 m and up to 93 m along the line of sight. **27 of the 272 replace a read within 2 m with one more than 5 m off** (20 backups, 4 idle, 3 chasers). Examples: seed 4 play 19, the left fielder backing up a ball rolling to the wall: track 1.2 m off when gated at 4.36 s; the snapshot read then puts the ball, at rest against the wall, 19 m along his line of sight beyond it for the rest of the play. Seed 177 play 64 (44 mph foul-side roller): all four fielders gated at the same instant on the first hop, their bounce law having it 25% slow. I found no play whose result the gate decides (the chasers involved were near the ball, or the ball was leaving play), so this is not P1.
- *Why it happens.* (a) The velocity looks are very sharp (σ = `READ_VEL_FRAC`·`LOOK_SCALE` = 0.8% of the speed across per look, floor 0.024 m/s), so P_vv after a few looks is tiny and any modelling mismatch in the roll (the wall stop's timing, the surface the world kept, a stop the estimate made early, P3-2) is many σ. (b) On hops, the first bounce's believed spin can be thousands of rpm off (P3-1). "χ² passes 50 once in 10¹⁰ looks of an honest track" is right for χ²₃ (8·10⁻¹¹), but the tracks are not honest in the tails (P3-3), so the rate says little.
- *Would a real fielder do this?* No: a man watching the ball who sees it go faster than he thought keeps it in sight; he does not suddenly misplace it by 10–90 m.
- *Suggested fix.* On a gate failure keep the track and reset what has gone wrong rather than dropping to the snapshot read: keep r̂ and its covariance, re-seed v̂ from this look (velocity covariance from the look's own σ, plus a floor), â back to its prior, and update with the position look. Or widen P by the innovation (a fading-memory step) instead of ending the track. Then correct agent-models §5.1 *The gate* and note §2/§3 with the rate over a large sample, and the share on hops.

### P3

**P3-1. `BOUNCE_SPIN_RPM` = 1,000 rpm is unsourced and too small for some balls.** `live.rs:641`. The first bounce applies the typical-spin table; for a mis-hit or a low liner the true spin can be the other sign (topspin against a believed backspin), and the bounce then keeps far more speed than he believes (seed 177 play 64: believed 12.2 m/s out, true 16.5 m/s; the 33 hop gates have believed/true speed 0.6–0.8 on the first look). A source is at hand: the spread of the contact model's spin about the table at each launch angle and speed (`examples/batted_spin.rs`, the tool that made the table). Use that spread (by bin) as the σ, and check the hop gate rate after it.

**P3-2. A rolling estimate that believes the ball has stopped cannot learn that it has not.** `live.rs:1177` (`go = h.min(sp/decel)`), `live.rs:1165–1225`. When the estimate's speed reaches zero within a step, the step's Jacobian in velocity is zero, so P_vv and its cross terms collapse to 0 (Monte Carlo below: case 1, filter P_vv 0 against an MC σ of 0.46 m/s along the roll). After that a velocity look cannot move v̂ (zero gain), only `ROLL_DRIFT`'s tiny q·h³/3 re-opens it, and â has no effect at v = 0 (`open` returns early). If the ball is in fact still rolling, the innovation grows until the gate ends the track: 16 of the 272 gate ends have the estimate stopped and the ball rolling (up to 11.7 m/s; seed 61 play 176, left fielder chasing, his estimate stopped by its own wall stop while the ball still rolled to the wall). Suggested: keep a velocity variance floor while the ball is seen rolling (it is seen moving), e.g. the look's own σ at the floor speed, and let `at_rest` alone set it to zero.

**P3-3. The honesty figures do not hold as tightly on other seeds.** Note §5. The worker's probe `zz_bounce_honesty`, run on game seeds 300–359 (the note used 0–59): outfielders tracked from the flight, RMS error / RMS σ / mean e²/σ²: −0.05 s 1.42 / 1.09 / 1.39; +0.05 s 1.32 / 1.00 / 1.43; +0.5 s 1.38 / 1.02 / 1.43; +1 s 1.42 / 1.01 / 1.51; **+2 s 1.34 / 0.84 / 1.56** (note: 1.14 / 0.84 / 1.37). Infielders 0.94–1.11, as the note says. So the outfield read after a bounce is about 20–25% over-confident in σ (the in-flight filter's own 1.39 is the starting point), not "0.9–1.4", and its error does not fall over 2 s on these seeds. And 943 of 2,232 outfielders (42%) have a larger error just after the bounce than just before; the RMS improves, individual reads often do not. Not wrong enough to block; state the range from more than one seed set, and say the post-bounce outfield read inherits the in-flight over-confidence.

**P3-4. The roll's process noise puts the learned-acceleration drift on both level axes, but the roll uses only its component along the roll.** `live.rs:1216–1229` against `roll_slowing` (`live.rs:1347`). The across-roll component of â never moves the ball, yet Q adds q·h⁵/20 and q·h³/3 to across-roll position and velocity (about 0.27 m of position σ over 2 s). Conservative and small; the Monte Carlo agrees with the filter to within about 10% on open ground. Either project Q onto the roll direction or state in §5.1 that the across-roll part is a deliberate allowance.

**P3-5. The ledger row's cause leaves out the ground clamp.** `docs/metrics-ledger.md` (newest §3 row), note §6. The first play to differ in game seeds 200, 201, 203 and 205 is in each case an infield ground ball that bounced before any fielder reacted (no track; snapshot reads), changed only by `on_or_above_ground`: the infielder's throw left 0.05–0.07 s *later* (seed 200 play 4, 201 play 11, 205 play 0) or the catch moved 0.4 m (203 play 9). The note says such plays move "0.0–0.03 s sooner". GB hits do not move, so the cause line is not wrong about the totals; add that the clamp alone changes snapshot-read plays, by a few hundredths of a second either way.

**P3-6. Stale comments and doc lines.**
- `live.rs:8063`: "keeps looking at a ball on its first flight": now across the bounces until a wall, a touch or the gate.
- `live.rs:937`: `Phase::Hop` "In the air after a bounce, a wall or a glove": a wall or a glove ends the track; only a bounce leads to a hop.
- `live.rs:653`: `ROLL_DRIFT` has no doc comment of its own and sits after `LOOK_VEL_FLOOR_MPS`, away from the `ROLL_SLOW_PRIOR` comment that describes it.
- `docs/agent-models.md:397` (*Typical spin*): "After a bounce, a deflection, or a carom off the wall: 600 rpm topspin" now holds only for the snapshot read; a tracked fielder uses his bounce law's spin.
- `docs/agent-models.md:421` (*Looks*): "until someone holds the ball" overstates it: looks stop at a wall carom, a touch or a gate end (the next bullets say so).
- `docs/agent-models.md:434` and note §3 item 3: the gate's rate and "with the spin carried, none did" (P2-1).

**Pre-existing, not this change (for TASKS, not for this branch).** The snapshot read of a ball at rest against the wall can put it well beyond the wall (seed 4 play 19: 19 m); the world stops a ball rolling into the padding at 15 m/s dead at the wall by an even slowing from the roll start (`roll_from`), with no carom.

## Answers to the questions

1. **World unchanged.** Yes. `roll_from` is the old block verbatim (same expressions, same order; only the unused `v_wall` dropped). Main `519842c` built in a probe copy, and the same copy with the branch's `ground.rs` swapped in (the binary changed): game digests of seeds 0–199 identical (sha256 of the list `0d3dc98b…a6615a5` both). The note's remark is right and pre-existing: on main `batted_path_dt` already rolled with `surf.mu_r` of the roll's first surface for the whole roll, while physics-spec §9.2 says to switch at the dirt–grass boundary; ledger §5 now records it.
2. **Invariants 2 and 3.** Hold. No crate dependency changed (`bb_agents` still has no `bb_world`; the crate-graph test passes). What the belief takes from the world, all in `bb_game`: the *times* of bounces and wall caroms (`ball_events`, never their positions), whether the ball rolls after a bounce (`rolling_after`), whether it is at rest (`bv == 0`), whether it is rolling now (`seen_rolling` for the snapshot and the runner's look), and whether it is held. These are the seen phase events the spec lists. Everything else in the update is his estimate: the surface and its roughness under his estimate, his law applied to his estimate, `roll_from` on his estimate. The true velocity enters only the world's draw of the look (as before), and the velocity floor there depends on his track's phase, which follows the ball's. No `Executed*` value is read. One caveat for the spec: the phase events are perfect (exact touch time, exact moment of rest); acceptable, but say so.
3. **Determinism.** No new `Purpose`; no `HashMap`, `mul_add` or bare transcendental in the added code (the only `powi` is in a test); sums run in fixed order. An assertion in a probe copy that every first-flight look's sub-index `kp` equals the track's own look count (main's key) held on every look of game seeds 0–199. Branch digests of seeds 0–199 run twice (8 threads, different scheduling): identical (`ca16ed34…aceede5`), and all 200 differ from main.
4. **The filter.** Monte Carlo against the filter's propagation (probe test in the branch copy): the bounce (velocity covariance through `ground::bounce` with tilt σ = roughness and 1,000 rpm level spin σ), five cases on grass, 40,000 draws: every element within about 10% (e.g. row y: filter 0.467 2.941 −0.400, MC 0.430 2.768 −0.379), means within 0.5 m/s. The roll over 1–2 s with â drifting: within about 5% on open ground; wrong where the estimate stops (P3-2). Honesty: P3-3. Too good anywhere? Not in position. The velocity read (0.8% of the speed per look, at any distance) is pre-existing from the first flight; carried onto a rolling ball 80–100 m away it is sharper than a person can judge a roll's speed and is what makes the gate fire (P2-1).
5. **Parameters.** All six are named constants with reasons. `BOUNCE_Z_M` (numerical), `ROLL_SLOW_PRIOR` ((0.28 − 0.22)·g = 0.59 m/s²), `ROLL_DRIFT` ((σ/2)²/2 s = 0.045) and `TRACK_GATE` (χ²₃ tail) are derived, not fitted. `LOOK_VEL_FLOOR_MPS` is unsourced but was added to stop a divergence, not to hit a figure. `BOUNCE_SPIN_RPM` is unsourced and "checked by the honesty measurement" (P3-1). None of them does a calibration job: none was moved against a run total.
6. **Tests.** All three are independent of the implementation. `no_read_puts_the_ball_below_the_ground` is a physical invariant; with `on_or_above_ground` disabled in a probe copy it fails (a believed point at z −1.69 m, rolling ball from 10 m, draws (−4, −4, −4, +4)). `a_seen_bounce_continues_the_track` fails on main only trivially (main has no track after the bounce, so its count assertion fails); its comparisons against the truth are sound but loose (e²/σ² within ×2, snapshot 13 m against 1.1 m). The relaxed "does not grow, in units of a look's σ at that range" for standing fielders is right: from one place the persistent part (a fraction of the range along the line of sight) is only weakly separable from the ball as the range changes, and the measured RMS does not shrink for standing outfielders (P3-3's +2 s row). It still catches a diverging filter, so it is worth keeping. The play-71 fixture would fail on main (phase after the bounce, and a 15 m read); its 2.5 m pass threshold is close to the 1.9–2.1 m measured, so a small unrelated change could trip it.
7. **Measurements.** Matched exactly (Verification). On game seeds 300–449 (my own set) the same shape: triples −20% (ground-ball triples 116 → 70), singles +50, doubles −2, runs −0.22 a team-game (Verification). Traced: seed 200 play 20 (99 mph at 9°, down the left-field line to the wall) the left fielder picks it up 0.25 s sooner, same spot, throw 0.25 s sooner; the note's seed 4 play 1 and seed 1 play 44 reproduce as it describes. (On seed 4 play 1 a throw 0.03 s sooner turns a triple into a single: the batter's decision is that sensitive, which is worth knowing but is not this change's doing.) The +125 singles against −97 triples and −4 doubles leave 24 more hits in about 4,650: within the noise of a run where every digest changed. P3-5 is the one movement the row does not name.
8. **Real player.** The note's account of play 71 holds: in the game, the right fielder's track error is +4.4 m at the bounce (2.20 s), +2.5 m at 2.4 s, 0.5 m at 3.2 s, 0.1 m at 3.3 s; the ball passes 2.1 m from him at 3.4 s at about 16 m/s, he turns for the wall at 3.2 s (target (46.5, 106.3)), and the centre fielder secures it at 8.64 s: triple. The missing outfield dive at a ball rolling by is a fair open item. New behaviour that looks wrong: the gate (P2-1). I found no track kept through a bad hop he should have been fooled by: a tilted bounce shows up at his next look 0.1 s later, as it would for a real fielder.
9. **Docs.** §5.1 *Across the bounces* describes the code, apart from the gate's rate and P3-6's lines. Observations item 71's note is fair and plain; "[~]" is right since it is still a triple.
