The authorized workspace tests passed after relocating Zig's caches to `/tmp` (the initial attempts failed because its default cache was read-only). Two runs of the authorized 500-PA command produced identical displayed rates: O-Swing 10.1%, foul/contact 22.0%, called-K share 55.6%, pitches/PA 3.2, Zone 56.8%, K 16.2%, and 52 checks. This reproduces the principal calibration gaps, but is not a bitwise replay test or an experiment establishing how much each finding contributes. P1 = high priority; P2 = medium; P3 = low. Scenarios below are code-derived unless explicitly identified as observed runs.

1. **[P1] The liveness guard invents a ball in play.** `sim/crates/bb_game/src/pa.rs:561`–`:563`, also `:751`. After more than 40 pitches, a PA becomes `PaResult::InPlay` regardless of the last physical result. **Scenario:** a batter reaches two strikes and keeps fouling pitches; pitch 41 is another foul, yet the PA ends as a ball in play, with no corresponding fair contact. This is deterministic, but still violates D1's requirement that outcomes come from physical facts; a deterministic fallback is not an exception. It also corrupts BIP and pitches/PA. **Suggested fix:** surface an explicit simulation/liveness error with the pitch history, or continue the physical PA. Never substitute a baseball result. Allocate subsequent pitch RNG keys without relying on a maximum legal PA length.

2. **[P1] Observation uncertainty leaks the exact future arrival time.** `sim/crates/bb_game/src/pa.rs:350`–`:359`. The noisy `tau` sample is accompanied by `sigma_tau = weber * K_perception * true_tau` whenever true_tau exceeds 20 ms. An agent that knows its own effective Weber fraction can recover the noiseless future crossing time from the uncertainty field alone. This differs from legitimately using truth to synthesize a noisy observation: the accompanying metadata preserves the exact answer. **Scenario:** at t = 0.20 s, with true arrival 0.40 s and effective Weber fraction 0.25, `sigma_tau` is exactly 0.05 s regardless of the random error in `obs.tau`; `t + sigma_tau / 0.25` recovers 0.40 s. The current EKF does not explicitly perform this division, but it receives and uses this truth-dependent covariance. **Suggested fix:** derive reported uncertainty from perceptual information available to the batter, or implement the multiplicative-noise likelihood separately for each hypothesized arrival time. Do not expose the true future crossing through an observation's error bar.

3. **[P1] Adaptation receives the true pitch identity instead of learning from the batter's observations.** `sim/crates/bb_game/src/pa.rs:715`–`:721`, `:735`, `:765`; `sim/crates/bb_agents/src/batter.rs:539`. `learn` indexes memory using `rec.ty`, the pitcher's actual selected type, then generates a new shape cue independent of the samples the batter saw. On-deck learning likewise uses the selected type directly. **Scenario:** a slider is perceived as a fastball throughout the pitch. The batter's slider memory nevertheless receives exactly one exposure and a correction toward that slider's true nominal shape; its fastball memory receives none. Even changing the observed spin cue cannot change which memory learns. The spec's §3.8 learning is driven by observed spin cues, with observation-only agent inputs (§1.1). **Suggested fix:** pass a typed post-pitch/on-deck observation and the batter's inferred type responsibilities into learning. Keep beliefs in estimated physical units, and derive innovations from those observations rather than an oracle type index and an error coordinate centered on truth. If a public post-pitch type reveal is intended, specify that information channel explicitly and still use observed shape cues.

4. **[P1] A check-swing decision deletes the bat and automatically becomes a take.** `sim/crates/bb_game/src/pa.rs:412`–`:422`, `:471`–`:478`. Braking sets `swung = false`, leaves `SwingResult::NoSwing`, and bypasses both bat contact and the geometric swing judgment. There is no 40 ms braking motion or maximum-yaw measurement. **Scenario:** the batter begins a swing, changes its mind just before the abort deadline, and would carry the bat forward during braking. The implementation cannot record contact, a foul, or a judged swing from that motion; an in-zone pitch becomes a called strike, including strike three. This directly affects the low chase and high called-K metrics: the run recorded 52 checks, all handled as takes. Whether each would physically count as a swing remains unmeasured. **Suggested fix:** turn the decision into a braking motor command, integrate the remaining bat motion, resolve contact, and have the rules judge its maximum yaw. The decision to stop must not itself decide the pitch result (agent-models §3.6; rules-spec §4.5).

5. **[P1] The EKF treats correlated perceptual errors as independent evidence.** `sim/crates/bb_game/src/pa.rs:333`–`:360`; `sim/crates/bb_agents/src/batter.rs:280`–`:286`, `:298`–`:317`. Ten percent of angular/TTA error variance persists across the whole pitch, but every filter update uses the full marginal R as though its error were independent of previous samples. Both the posterior covariance and accumulated type likelihood therefore overstate the new information. Adding release and break states does not explicitly model these persistent angular/Weber biases. **Scenario:** for a scalar constant observation with this noise, averaging 20 samples has variance `sigma² * (0.1 + 0.9/20) = 0.145 sigma²`; the independent model reports `0.05 sigma²`, almost three times smaller. The changing flight model makes the exact factor different, but not the independence assumption correct. **Suggested fix:** augment the state with persistent angular and multiplicative-TTA bias variables, use the independent 90% share as measurement noise, and include their uncertainty in predictive likelihoods. Alternatively implement a correlated-observation update. Validate coverage and type-weight calibration at commit and cutoff, not just mean convergence. This is a plausible source of excessively crisp zone judgments; its net effect on chase needs an ablation.

6. **[P1] Mid-swing adjustment rebuilds the whole swing retrospectively instead of respecting motion and phase limits.** `sim/crates/bb_game/src/pa.rs:403`–`:439`; `sim/crates/bb_agents/src/swing.rs:149`–`:155`. Only after collecting the late observations does the code construct an analytic swing whose start is already in the past. Clamping endpoint x/z displacement by `0.6 * elapsed` does not bound changes in the actual placement parameters, pivot/axis motion, or phase rate. Contact depth changes without a clamp (`dp.y`), and the timing allowance includes decision latency rather than the period in which phase can actually change. The observation loop also admits the first 10 ms sample beyond `t_cut`, and the cutoff remains tied to the original arrival even when the final contact time moves earlier. **Scenario:** commit at 0.20 s for contact at 0.40 s; collect observations through approximately 0.34 s, then construct a new swing starting around 0.25 s. Moving contact to 0.38 s leaves only 40 ms after the last observation, despite the specified 60 ms cutoff, and rewrites the already-executed part of the motion. **Suggested fix:** instantiate the intended/executed motion at commit, honor motor latency, and apply causal parameter/phase updates at each eligible tick with the specified bounds. Retain execution error without revealing it to the decision code. Reject samples after the applicable cutoff. Over-effective late correction is a credible contributor to too few timing fouls and cannot be repaired reliably by increasing noise alone.

7. **[P2] The release-estimate covariance disagrees with the generated observation noise.** `sim/crates/bb_game/src/pa.rs:324`; `sim/crates/bb_agents/src/batter.rs:205`–`:221`. Release position error scales with `K_perception`, but the bank always assigns it a 5 cm standard deviation. **Scenario:** at the interim setting 2.5, actual release-estimate sigma is 12.5 cm while the filter believes 5 cm: its prior variance is too small by 6.25×. Early angular residuals are consequently assigned to plate position, time, or break more readily than warranted—the same class of misattribution the interim report says the new release states were intended to fix. **Suggested fix:** include the release observation's covariance in `ReleaseObs` and initialize the release states from it, including the applicable global/composure factors. Check posterior coverage at the calibrated knobs as well as at 1.0.

8. **[P2] Failure of a belief-side aim lookup directly manufactures a world-side miss.** `sim/crates/bb_game/src/pa.rs:424`–`:443`; `sim/crates/bb_agents/src/batter.rs:368`–`:385`. If either initial or late `point_at_depth` returns `None`, no bat is executed; the orchestrator constructs `SwingResult::Miss` with infinite gap. **Scenario:** the initial target is valid, but a later dominant hypothesis requests a depth beyond the finite nominal trajectory (which ends at y = −0.5 m). The physically ongoing swing could still contact the actual pitch; instead the lookup failure guarantees a whiff. A change in an agent's internal trajectory horizon should not decide whether bat and ball touch. **Suggested fix:** retain and execute the last valid motor command when prediction/refinement fails, or report a planning error if no command exists. Obtain misses and closest approach only from the physical detector.

9. **[P2] End-cap contacts use a side-surface normal, sometimes zero.** `sim/crates/bb_physics/src/contact.rs:43`–`:47`, `:127`–`:130`. The clamped-axis gap includes axial separation at an endpoint, but the normal calculation discards that component with `reject(bat.axis)`. **Scenario:** a bat points along +X and a ball approaches its tip along −X. At the endpoint sphere's contact distance, `r - p` is entirely axial; rejection yields zero, the barrel slope is zero, and `n` is zero. The impulse solver declines the contact as separating, leaving the ball unchanged, yet the world records a bat contact. Oblique cap contacts get the wrong impulse direction and spin. **Suggested fix:** use explicit endpoint geometry and its corresponding unit normal, separately from the tapered side surface. Reconcile the bat module's “flat end” description with the detector's sphere-like endpoint gap. Test axial and oblique contacts at both ends, including outgoing velocity and impulse count. These defects can affect end-of-bat fouls, but their frequency was not measured here.

10. **[P2] Plane-tilt execution noise rotates around the wrong axis.** `sim/crates/bb_agents/src/swing.rs:210`–`:215`; `sim/crates/bb_agents/src/motor.rs:93`–`:95`. Rotating the attack direction around `axis_c × Z`, then projecting perpendicular to the bat, can remove the intended perturbation. **Scenario:** with `axis_c = +X` and attack direction `+Y`, the rotation axis is `−Y`; any `plane_tilt` rotates +Y about itself, changing nothing. With initial attack angle alpha, this geometry gives `atan(tan(alpha) * cos(plane_tilt))`, an even, second-order response rather than a signed first-order tilt. **Suggested fix:** rebuild the attack direction using `attack_angle + plane_tilt`, then project appropriately, or rotate around the bat axis using the correct handedness convention. Verify positive and negative errors for both batting sides. The current implementation makes one component of the swing-noise knob largely ineffective; increasing the knob mainly increases the other errors.

11. **[P2] A velocity cone is treated as a caught foul tip without a catcher.** `sim/crates/bb_game/src/pa.rs:456`–`:462`, `:547`–`:550`. Outgoing speed and direction classify `FoulTip`, and that call increments strikes even at two strikes. The spec requires a sharp/direct flight to a glove that actually secures the ball; no such event exists here. **Scenario:** contact sends the ball backward within 25° of the incoming direction at more than half its incoming speed, but it passes outside the catcher's reach or would be dropped. It still ends a two-strike PA as a strikeout rather than a continuing foul. **Suggested fix:** require geometric catcher capture and its timestamp before producing a foul tip. Until that subsystem exists, label this as an explicit cage assumption or leave backward contact as a foul; do not silently equate direction with secure capture. This changes two-strike PA length and K accounting. Both foul types already count in the report's foul numerator, so this is not by itself an explanation of the entire low foul/contact share.

12. **[P2] Swing value ignores the batter's actual believed execution precision and horizontal aim error.** `sim/crates/bb_agents/src/batter.rs:466`–`:467`, `:490`–`:499`; `sim/crates/bb_agents/src/motor.rs:78`–`:79`. Every batter assumes 1.5 cm vertical and 8 ms timing noise, regardless of BatControl, Timing, or the swing factor. Contact probability also ignores `c.mean.x - aim.mean.x`, reach limits, and the contact station. This is a material simplification of W's dependence on the full offset and the batter's execution distribution (§3.5), not merely an unknown learned value. **Scenario:** a grade-20 Timing batter at K_swing = 1.5 has approximately 17.6 ms timing sigma but evaluates swings with 8 ms; a grade-80 batter assumes the same 8 ms despite executing at about 4.6 ms. Two hypotheses with identical height/time but widely separated horizontal locations receive the same contact-window probability even when aiming at one would put the other beyond the usable barrel. **Suggested fix:** supply believed execution parameters derived from attributes and known factors, and use a geometric/table-based W sensitive to horizontal displacement, reach, station, and timing. These are beliefs, so doing this does not require exposing the executed noise sample. Recheck two-strike decisions across archetypes after correcting the mismatch.

13. **[P2] Strike probability drops x–z covariance without documenting that approximation.** `sim/crates/bb_agents/src/batter.rs:403`–`:408`, `:485`–`:488`. The filter propagates a full plate covariance, but `p_strike` multiplies marginal rectangle probabilities as if x and z were independent. The comment acknowledges square corners, not the separate independence assumption. **Scenario:** near an upper/outside corner, strongly positively correlated x/z errors can place most mass on the inside-inside/outside-outside diagonal. If each marginal is 0.5, joint strike probability can approach 0.5, while the product reports 0.25; negative correlation can instead make it approach zero. These differences change take and check decisions. **Suggested fix:** integrate the bivariate location Gaussian over the circle–rectangle strike region, using the full covariance, or document and validate a bounded approximation. A geometry simplification is permitted as a belief, but silently discarding covariance undermines the claim that the bank's uncertainty is used in EV_take.

14. **[P2] Adaptation of one pitch type shrinks uncertainty for every type.** `sim/crates/bb_game/src/pa.rs:730`–`:733`; `sim/crates/bb_agents/src/batter.rs:205`–`:219`, `:548`–`:550`. The caller computes break sigma using the most-seen type and supplies this single value to all bank hypotheses. The code comments admit the substitution, but the spec still requires per-type exposure. **Scenario:** after seeing ten fastballs and no curves, every curve hypothesis is initialized with the fastball's reduced spread. With eta = 0.15, its sigma falls to approximately 0.612 of the original despite zero curve exposure. **Suggested fix:** pass a per-type prior covariance/spread into bank initialization and use each type's own memory. Keep any deliberate cross-type learning as an explicitly specified model rather than implicitly making all types as familiar as the most-seen one.

15. **[P2] The PA batter has no sequence prior or velocity anchoring.** `sim/crates/bb_game/src/pa.rs:325`–`:329`, `:529`–`:536`; `sim/crates/bb_agents/src/batter.rs:211`. Each pitch initializes priors from count usage alone and starts arrival means at the scouting model's time. The preceding observed pitch and speed do not enter. The newly added exposure memory does not supply these mechanisms either. **Scenario:** a changeup immediately after a fastball is perceived with the same initial speed model as a changeup at that count with any other history. The specified 0.2 velocity anchoring and 0.85 repeat-type prior factor never occur. **Suggested fix:** keep observation-derived within-PA history, apply §3.1's sequence and anchoring rules to believed type models/priors, and add the corresponding tunneling/interception behavioral checks from §11. Their absence removes a specified mechanism for deception and is a credible contributor to low chase; increasing observation noise is not a substitute for it.

16. **[P2] Configuring the collision sweet spot does not configure the swing's sweet spot.** `sim/crates/bb_physics/src/bat.rs:141`–`:143`, `:165`–`:166`; `sim/crates/bb_agents/src/swing.rs:185`; `sim/crates/bb_physics/src/contact.rs:186`–`:193`. `BatBallParams.ss_from_tip` controls COR and reported sweet-spot velocity, but swing planning uses a separate hardcoded 0.15 m station. **Scenario:** set `ss_from_tip` to 0.20 m for a different bat: the inverse still aims the station 0.15 m from the tip at the ball, while resolution and bat-speed telemetry refer to a point 5 cm nearer the knob. Noise-free intent, collision tuning, and reported BatSpeed now describe different points. **Suggested fix:** put the chosen station in the shared bat definition and pass it into the inverse and telemetry. Keep a separate fixed Statcast measurement station only if the distinction is explicit. Defaults happen to agree, so this does not explain the present default-matchup calibration gaps.

17. **[P2] The fair/foul test ignores the ball's radius.** `sim/crates/bb_game/src/pa.rs:459`–`:460`. Fairness is tested using the center's `abs(x) <= y`, although rules-spec §5.2 includes a ball overlapping a foul line. **Scenario:** beyond first base, a ball first touches the ground with center `(x, y) = (20.03, 20.00)` m. Its center is only `0.03 / sqrt(2)`, approximately 2.1 cm, outside the line, so a regulation ball overlaps fair territory. The code calls it foul. **Suggested fix:** use signed distance to each foul line with the ball radius and the appropriate physical adjudication event. Before first/third base, preserve pending status until the specified touch, settling, or base-passage event rather than treating every first landing as final. The radius error biases toward extra fouls, so correcting it alone will not explain or solve the reported foul deficit.

18. **[P3] Contact feeds stale spin into the otherwise correctly framed impulse inputs.** `sim/crates/bb_physics/src/contact.rs:123`–`:126`. Spin is taken from the preceding trajectory knot and called piecewise constant, although flight integrates continuous spin decay. Physics-spec §5.3 requires rebuilding the event state with a partial step. **Scenario:** contact one millisecond into a step starting at 250 rad/s, around 40 m/s airspeed and tau100 = 22 s, should have about 249.9898 rad/s spin; the solver receives 250. Moving contact across the next knot introduces a small discontinuity in the friction input. **Suggested fix:** reconstruct the full ball state at contact with partial-step integration (using the environment), retaining the interpolant for root location. The size is too small to credibly explain the large calibration gaps.

19. **[P3] The intended execution-isolation guarantee is not enforced by visibility.** `sim/crates/bb_agents/src/motor.rs:41`–`:45`, `:86`–`:88`, `:91`. Executed values and all their fields are public within the agent crate and available to decision modules; the spec says decision code cannot receive/name them and requires a compile-fail check (§§1.1, 11). Current inspected decision code does not read them, but the stated compile-time protection is absent. **Scenario:** a future decision routine imports `crate::motor::ExecutedSwing` and reads `placement.t_shift` to cancel its exact timing error; these declarations allow it. **Suggested fix:** separate the execution implementation into an orchestrator-facing module/crate boundary that decision code cannot access, and add a meaningful negative compilation test. Preserve the current direction of data flow. Do not describe the crate dependency check against bb_world as also proving execution-error isolation.

20. **[P3] M3's composure and adjustability attributes are replaced by fixed average behavior.** `sim/crates/bb_game/src/pa.rs:320`–`:321`, `:427`, `:437`; `sim/crates/bb_agents/src/motor.rs:34`–`:35`, `:78`–`:79`. The PA path has no composure/leverage input to its noise factors, and adjustment speed is the literal 0.6 m/s rather than the batter's Adjustability. The generic motor constructors can accept a combined scale, but this caller supplies only global knobs. **Scenario:** two otherwise identical batters with different composure in a leverage-three situation, or different adjustability after the same late read, behave identically. These mechanisms are specified for M3, not declared deviations in the implementation/spec. **Suggested fix:** expose the physical attributes and perceived-leverage input, centralize the named factors, and feed them into observation/execution noise and causal swing adjustment. If intentionally deferred, update the M3 scope and spec explicitly. Average, neutral-leverage calibration alone cannot validate them.

Additional audit limits: no direct OS RNG, unordered-map iteration, wall-clock dependency, native transcendental calls, or FMA use was found in the requested files. The EKF's `P H^T`, `H P H^T`, gain, and `P - K H P` products, and the seven-state plate projection, are algebraically consistent for symmetric P and independent measurement noise; findings 5 and 7 concern the modeled uncertainty, not a discovered transpose error. The side-surface contact point, relative velocity, inverse-inertia orientation, normal-speed COR input, and launch/spray signs match the specified conventions. Ordinary left-handed planning reflects x correctly and transforms angular velocity as an axial vector; no general handedness sign reversal was identified. `cage.rs:55`–`:64` intentionally reads a true trajectory for the explicitly documented M2 perfect-perception lab; the PA path does not call that helper. The zero-lag swing template and the chosen interpretation of the taper COR station are acknowledged in code, so they were not reported as undisclosed changes. These checks do not establish that the simplified swing template reproduces the required foul distribution.
