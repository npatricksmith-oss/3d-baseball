## Summary

The core architecture — a pure forward-integrated articulated body in `bb_physics::body`, proximal muscle-group drives with activation and Hill force–velocity dynamics, delayed postural feedback during stance and stride, learned drive programs built once per player and blended forward across aims, and execution noise drawn up front in `bb_agents::motor` — keeps the owner's D31 rule in games and is the right foundation. However, a bug in `breakdowns.py` invalidates the spec's headline numbers for lunging and the heel-plant wait (including Owner Question 3 in §14), `ratings.py` re-times effort swings backward against D31 while hiding an incompatible variance budget in §6.1, §9's client re-integration breaks binding Decision D9, and the 12-DOF core and batter control loop have structural gaps. **Verdict: sound with named fixes** — fix P1-1 through P1-4 in the spec, probes, and §14 questions before asking the owner to rule or starting phase 1, and fold P2-1 through P2-4 into phase 1's gate.

## Findings

### P1-1. `breakdowns.py:shifted()` shifts `t_on` without shifting `t_off`, falsifying the lunging and heel-plant-wait breakdowns and corrupting §14 Question 3

- **Where:** `analysis/research/body/breakdowns.py:68-72`, `analysis/research/body/breakdowns.py:89-95`, `analysis/research/body/breakdowns.py:104-108`; `docs/body-motion.md` §1 (lines 29–33), §5.3 (line 290), §6.1 (line 367), §6.2 (lines 379–405), §14 ("What the hips do when the front foot is late", lines 635–639).
- **What is wrong:** `shifted(plan, names, dt)` adds `dt` to `p.ch[n][1]` (`t_on`) and leaves `p.ch[n][2]` (`t_off`) unchanged (`off_legs = -52 ms`, `off_core = +35 ms`). Delaying a channel by `+77` or `+100 ms` therefore chops `77` or `100 ms` off the leg drive's active duration (from `176 ms` down to `99` or `76 ms`) and switches the core drive off before contact, while shifting a channel `-40 ms` earlier *adds* `40 ms` of active leg drive (`216 ms`). By contrast, `analysis/research/body/ratings.py:38-40` shifts `t_off` whenever `p.ch[n][2] < 0.9`. In addition, §1 and §6.2 attribute lunging's collapse to the rear foot unloading and shrinking the ground yaw couple (`cap 212 N·m against 251`), when the friction cap never actually engaged on a single step.
- **Evidence:** Instrumenting `chain.py` (`sim/target/review-probes/gemini-carbon/verify_all.py`, §3) shows `capped_steps = 0` in the average swing (`max_raw_legs = 203.6 N·m`, `cap ∈ [157.5, 315.0] N·m`), in lunging 60 ms, and in lunging 100 ms (`max_raw_legs = 167.0 N·m` buggy, `209.5 N·m` fixed; at `s = 0.524 m` over a `0.773 m` stride, the lever rule still leaves 32% BW on the rear foot). Shifting `t_off` along with `t_on` changes five breakdown cases completely:

  | Case | Spec / buggy (`t_off` fixed) | Fixed (`t_off` shifted with `t_on`) |
  | :-- | :-- | :-- |
  | `late foot +120, hips wait 37` | `+38.4 ms`, `u = 0.599 m`, `27.9 m/s`, pelvis `50°` | `+36.8 ms`, `u = 0.627 m`, **`30.2 m/s`**, pelvis **`63°`** |
  | `late foot +160, hips wait 77` | `+84.7 ms`, `u = 0.511 m`, **`22.0 m/s`**, pelvis **`22°`** | `+76.1 ms`, `u = 0.621 m`, **`29.7 m/s`**, pelvis **`62°`** |
  | `hips early 40 ms` | `-7.0 ms`, `u = 0.643 m`, **`31.8 m/s`** (`+1.1`), pelvis `101°` | `-5.8 ms`, `u = 0.625 m`, **`30.1 m/s`** (`-0.6`), pelvis `90°` |
  | `lunging: held 60 ms` | `+63.2 ms`, `u = 0.560 m`, `25.1 m/s`, pelvis `36°` | `+58.7 ms`, `u = 0.623 m`, **`29.9 m/s`**, pelvis **`62°`** |
  | `lunging: held 100 ms` | `+114.9 ms`, `u = 0.435 m`, **`17.6 m/s`**, pelvis **`-1°`** | `+97.9 ms`, `u = 0.617 m`, **`29.3 m/s`**, pelvis **`61°`** |
  | `late swing 15 ms` | `+15.0 ms`, `u = 0.623 m`, `29.8 m/s` (`-0.9`) | `+14.7 ms`, `u = 0.632 m`, **`30.6 m/s`** (`-0.1`) |

  Because of this bug, §14 Question 3 tells the owner that waiting for the heel plant leaves the swing *"late but intact (85 ms late, 22 m/s at 160 ms)"* — 3.2 m/s slower than firing with no foot down (`25.2 m/s`). With `t_off` shifted, waiting gives `29.7 m/s` and `62°` pelvis turn (genuinely late and intact), while lunging does not lose rotational power in the planar model because the planar chain lacks front-knee collapse and vertical centre-of-mass drop.
- **What would fix it:** Fix `shifted()` in `analysis/research/body/breakdowns.py` (`if p.ch[n][2] < 0.9: p.ch[n][2] += dt`), regenerate `analysis/research/body/breakdowns.json`, update §1, §5.3, §6.1, §6.2, and §14 Question 3 before presenting §14 to the owner, and move lunging to §6.2's list of breakdowns that require the 3D body.

### P1-2. `ratings.py` re-times effort swings earlier against D31, hiding an incompatible variance budget in §6.1 between within-batter bat speed (`1.9 m/s`) and contact depth (`0.27 m`)

- **Where:** `analysis/research/body/ratings.py:78-88`, `analysis/research/body/ratings.py:134-140`; `docs/body-motion.md` §5.5 (line 342), §6.1 (lines 349–370), §7.2 (lines 422–432).
- **What is wrong:** §5.5 and §6.1 state the D31 rule plainly: *"An effort error drawn at the start is part of the program and cannot move anything earlier"* and *"A slower or weaker swing starts when it was planned and arrives late."* Yet `ratings.py` Section B ("the same hitter, easier or harder (nothing re-learned, re-timed only)") calls `timed()`, which shifts `t_0`, `t_fd`, and all channel `t_on`/`t_off` *earlier* by up to `17 ms` so that `×0.8..×1.2` drive scalings still reach `A_c = -8°` at `t = 0`, and §7.2 cites that shifted run as matching MLB's within-batter speed-to-length relation *at fixed contact depth*. When the start time is held fixed as D31 requires, involuntary drive scaling cannot satisfy §6.1's two within-batter calibration targets simultaneously. In addition, §6.1's table sets `"strength on the day"` size from `"fatigue, composure"` alone, which leaves zero trial-to-trial motor amplitude noise for a fresh, calm player.
- **Evidence:** Running `chain.py` with `shift = 0` (`verify_all.py`, §5) shows that because `v_bat ∝ √T` while `T_swing ∝ 1/√T`:
  - `×0.955` drive (`-4.5%` torque) lowers bat speed by only `-0.38 m/s` (`30.66 -> 30.28 m/s`) and arrives `+3.3 ms` late (`-13.2 cm = -5.2 in` deeper on a `40 m/s` pitch).
  - `×0.800` drive (`-20%` torque) is needed just to drop bat speed by `-2.05 m/s` (`28.61 m/s`, ~1 SD of §6.1's `1.9 m/s` target), and arrives **`+16.8 ms` late** — shifting contact depth by **`-0.67 m` (`-26.4 in`)**, **2.5× the entire `0.27 m` (`6.7 ms`) within-batter contact-depth σ** before any perception, pitch-speed, foot-timing, or onset-jitter error enters.
  MLB's `1.9 m/s` (`4.3 mph`) within-batter SD (`docs/research/swing/10-swing-length-by-bat-speed.md` §2) spans all counts, locations, and contact depths (`sim/crates/bb_agents/src/motor.rs:100-102` notes it drops to `3.2 mph = 1.4 m/s` near mean depth over the middle of the plate) and includes *intentional* approach changes (such as two-strike shortened swings, which the batter plans and times his commit for), whereas involuntary motor-unit recruitment noise on one maximal contraction (`docs/research/body/6-movement-variability.md` §1) is small (`~3–5%` CV, giving `~0.4 m/s` and `~3 ms`).
- **What would fix it:** Remove `timed()` from within-batter effort probes; give motor drive noise a small baseline physiological CV (`[E]`, ~3–5%) widened by fatigue and composure; and distinguish *planned* effort/approach programs (which set their own commit clock legitimately) from *unplanned* execution noise rather than back-solving unplanned noise to match `1.9 m/s` and `0.27 m` at once.

### P1-3. Re-integrating `ExecutedDrive` in the client (§9) violates binding Decision D9 (`PLAN.md` §1) and omits §5.3's proprioceptive noise from the replay contract (Invariant 5)

- **Where:** `docs/body-motion.md` §5.3 (line 292), §9 (lines 545–549), §10 (lines 556, 561, 566); `PLAN.md` §1 row D9.
- **What is wrong:** `PLAN.md` D9 is Decided (owner Q30(b), 2026-11-18): *"Replays store recorded timelines, not seeds ... A saved play stores the build's commit and the recorded play itself, so the client shows it exactly as seen on any later build — Re-simulation drifts as code changes."* Reopening a decided row in `PLAN.md` §1 requires the owner (AGENTS.md Invariant 8), yet §9 recommends storing only `ExecutedDrive` (~100 bytes of onsets, rises, offs, strengths) in `PlayTimeline` and re-integrating `bb_physics::body` in the client through `bb_ffi`, without putting that choice in §14. Furthermore, §5.3 specifies that postural feedback over the 1.5 s delivery reads *"his true body state with his own sensing noise, an input of `bb_agents::motor`"*, which is missing from §9's 100-byte drive tuple — so client re-integration would not even reproduce the *same* build's delivery bit for bit (Invariant 5).
- **Evidence:** §9 justifies re-integration by estimating *"16 segments' positions and rotations at 240 Hz is about 200 kB a pitch, 60 MB a game."* That overstates the cost in two ways: background/headless games (`sim-cli games`, parallel seasons) need not serialize visual body frames at all, and an articulated body in a watched or saved `PlayTimeline` needs only its generalized coordinates `q(t)` (root position + joint angles: ~18 floats for the 12-DOF core, ~41 for the 35-DOF body) quantized to `f16`/`f32` at `60–120 Hz` over `~0.8 s` (`~3–6 kB` per pitch, `~1–2 MB` per watched game), reconstructed by pure forward kinematics with zero drift across later physics changes.
- **What would fix it:** Store sampled generalized coordinates `q(t)` in watched/saved `PlayTimeline`s to keep D9 intact, or add an explicit question to §14 asking the owner to reopen D9 for body poses (and include any proprioceptive noise stream in `ExecutedDrive` so same-build replay is exact).

### P1-4. Three-way conflict in the batter's control loop between eye-point motion (§4.1, §6.2), the decision clock (§5.4), and mid-swing `Adjustability` (§5.5)

- **Where:** `docs/body-motion.md` §4.1 (line 116), §5.4 (lines 303–320), §5.5 (lines 338–343), §6.2 (line 403), §10 (lines 560–566); `analysis/research/body/fit_params.json:3-8`.
- **What is wrong:**
  1. *Execution error reaching perception vs §10 & Invariant 3:* §4.1 (line 116) and §6.2 (line 403) state that pulling off (an execution onset error on `core` drawn in `bb_agents::motor`) moves the head and *"that is how pulling off reaches perception."* Yet §10 draws `ExecutedDrive` as a single output after agent decisions and integrates the batter's body in `bb_world` in one pass, and Invariant 3 forbids decision code from reading `Executed*`. Pulling off cannot alter pre-commit observations unless `bb_game` co-steps `bb_world`'s pre-commit body alongside `bb_observe` every `10 ms` tick (computing noisy sightlines from the true eye point while `bb_agents`' filter continues to assume its planned eye point).
  2. *Shoulder onset precedes commit by 61 ms:* §5.4 puts `legs` and `core` on the pre-commit clock (D22) and rules that `shoulder` and `wrist` *"may start only after `t_commit + T_latency`"*, identifying that with [4]'s `-170 ms` first move. In `fit_params.json`, however, `on_sh = -230.8 ms` — earlier than `on_legs = -227.6 ms` and `61 ms` before `-170 ms`. On a `400 ms` MLB fastball, `T_swing = 231 ms` plus `T_latency = 50 ms` forces `t_commit` to `-281 ms` (`119 ms` after release, ~15 ft of ball flight). Delaying `shoulder` to `-171 ms` (`arms committed 60 ms later` in `breakdowns.py`) spins the trunk open to `128°` (vs `63°`) and pulls the bat `+9.2°` because the fitted pre-commit `core` relies on `-tau["shoulder"]` reaction torque from `-231 ms` onward.
  3. *`Adjustability` has zero unstarted channels left to adjust:* §5.5 restricts mid-motion adjustment (`Adjustability`) to *"the onsets that have not happened, the strengths of channels not yet on, a switch-off."* Because `legs`, `core`, and `shoulder` are all already on at or before commit (`-238` to `-170 ms`) and `elbows`/`wrists` carry no active drive (§5.1), no active upper-body channel remains unstarted after `t_commit + T_latency`, making `Adjustability` dead except for `t_off`.
- **What would fix it:** (a) Constrain `on_sh >= -170 ms` (after `t_commit + T_latency`) when fitting the average and learned swings so pre-commit `legs`/`core` balance without early shoulder reaction torque; (b) allow `Adjustability` to modulate future excitation `u(t)` for `t >= t_now + T_latency` on *already-active* channels (filtered through `TA_UP`/`TA_DN`, which is strictly causal and touches nothing already integrated); and (c) specify in §10 whether `bb_game` co-integrates body and perception in `10 ms` ticks or keeps perception on the planned eye path in phase 3.

### P2-2. Integrator checks (`checks.py`) are blind to skew-symmetric Coriolis/gyroscopic bugs and omit angular momentum; the wrist stop has a `-71 N·m` sticky damper

- **Where:** `analysis/research/body/checks.py:26-54`, `analysis/research/body/chain.py:187-190`; `docs/body-motion.md` §8.1 (lines 504–511).
- **What is wrong:** In any multibody system `M(q)q̈ + C(q,q̇)q̇ = Q`, a skew-symmetric error `ΔC = -ΔCᵀ` (such as a wrong sign or factor in 3D gyroscopic `ω × Iω` or Coriolis terms) does identically zero mechanical power (`q̇ᵀ ΔC q̇ ≡ 0`). Neither work–KE balance (`check 1`) nor zero-drive KE conservation (`check 2`) can ever detect it, and linear momentum (`check 3`) cannot detect rotational coupling errors. In addition, `chain.py:189` applies `2.0e4 * (lag - b.lag_stop) + 40.0 * (qd[3] - qd[4])` without the one-sided velocity guard used on `SEP_STOP` (`line 185`), pulling the bat backward as it begins uncocking.
- **Evidence:** Mutation testing in `verify_all.py` (§2–§3) confirms:
  - Injecting a bogus skew-symmetric Coriolis term `h[3] += 0.5*qd[2]*qd[4]; h[4] -= 0.5*qd[2]*qd[3]` passes all three checks in `checks.py` (`c1_work = 4.74e-8`, `c2_freeKE = 1.62e-10`, `c3_dpy = 2.74e-10 N·s`), while shifting vertical angular momentum `L_z` by `0.259 N·m·s`.
  - Deleting the reaction torque `-tau["core"]` on the pelvis (with matching `power` and `|sep| < 45°`) passes `c1_work = 7.29e-7` and `c3_dpy = 1.14e-8 N·s` because `Mm[0][1] = 0`, while violating `L_z` by `53.6 N·m·s`.
  - During the average swing, `chain.py:189`'s wrist-stop torque drops to **`-71.4 N·m`** (`verify_all.py` §3) as `qd[4] > qd[3]` while `lag > 133°`.
- **What would fix it:** Require in §8.1 for phase 1: (1) 3-axis linear *and* 3-axis angular momentum conservation when external contacts are off; (2) a randomized finite-difference Euler–Lagrange identity test (`max |d/dt(∂L/∂q̇) − ∂L/∂q − Q| < 10⁻⁴`, verified to `2.3e-4` on `chain.py`); and (3) clamping unilateral contact/stop forces to `≥ 0` so damping never becomes adhesive on release.

### P2-3. The 12-DOF 3D core (§8.2) lacks forearm rotation (kinematically rank-deficient), splits phase 1 from phase 2, and understates game and roster-generation costs

- **Where:** `docs/body-motion.md` §5.5 (lines 326–337), §8.2 (lines 515–543), §13 (lines 612–615), §14 Question 1 (lines 620–626); `analysis/research/body/bench_chain.rs`.
- **What is wrong:**
  1. *Kinematic rank deficiency of the 12-DOF core:* §8.2 (lines 534–539) lists `pelvis yaw and tilt (2)`, `trunk rotation, side bend and flexion (3)`, `lead arm's shoulder (2) and elbow (1)`, `wrists' cock (1)`, `bat's roll (1)`, and `forward travel (1)` (11 DOFs, dropping forearm rotation, rear arm, and pelvis vertical rise/front-knee extension). Rolling an axisymmetric bat about its shaft leaves its axis `â` unchanged, so only **4 arm+wrist DOFs** (`2 shoulder + 1 elbow + 1 cock`) remain relative to the trunk to place **5 bat-pose coordinates** (3D hand position `(x, y, z)` plus 2D barrel direction `(azimuth, elevation)`). Without forearm pronation/supination (`§4.1` line 95), the elbow hinge locks the wrist's radial–ulnar cock plane so the hands cannot "turn the barrel" onto different pitch planes; without pelvis `z`/knee extension, the front-leg block and collapsed front leg cannot occur.
  2. *Phase 1 builds 35 DOFs while games need the core:* §13 Phase 1 builds and fits the 35-DOF model of §4.1, whereas §14 Question 1 recommends running the ~12-DOF core in games and §13 Phase 2 assigns a `2–3 session` Sonnet port to match the oracle bit for bit (*"if the core of §8.2 has to be derived from the full body first"*). Deriving and fitting a reduced 3D multibody model is an Opus physics/oracle task, not a porting detail.
  3. *Understated per-game and roster-time compute:* `bench_chain.rs` (`1.68 µs/step`) uses hardware `f64::sin_cos()` and `exp()` rather than `bb_math::m` (`libm`), with no closed-loop or contact complementarity solve. More importantly, §8.2's `250 steps/pitch` (`0.6 s/game`) relies on tabulating the pitcher's delivery *"up to the arm's acceleration"* (`0.2 s` left), which **eliminates per-pitch execution errors in the pitcher's stride, front-leg block ("collapsed front leg", §11), and trunk ("flying open", §11)**, ignores continuous per-pitch fatigue (`agent-models §2.5`), and omits D22 hip turns/brakes on the ~53% of pitches that are takes. Integrating both players from stride touch-down (`~0.8–1.0 s` combined, `500–800` steps with `libm`) puts even a 12–14-DOF core closer to **`1.5–4 s/game`**. Finally, §8.2 omits **roster-generation cost**: §5.5 builds `~25–75` learned swings per batter by search; at even `300` evaluations × `1.5 ms` per swing, one 26-player game roster takes `~12–35 s` (`hours` across `sim-cli games --n 300` unless cached or pre-fitted).
- **What would fix it:** Replace `bat's roll` with forearm pronation/supination and add pelvis `z`/knee extension in the core (~13–14 DOFs); build and gate *that* core in phase 1's Python oracle (leaving the 35-DOF model optional or kinematic-IK); integrate per pitch from stride touch-down and update §8.2 / §14 Question 1's time budget (`~1.5–4 s/game`); and specify an offline pre-fitted response surface (shipped as data, refined in ≤5 Newton steps at roster creation) so `Game::new` stays fast.

### P2-4. Planar reductions distort 3D expectations on contact speed (`-3.6 m/s`), the `2×` coil spread (§7.2, §14 Question 5), and millimeter vertical plane control (§5.3)

- **Where:** `docs/body-motion.md` §4.3 (line 171), §5.3 (lines 284–290), §6.1 (line 369), §7.2 (lines 436–450), §14 Question 5 (lines 648–652); `analysis/research/body/chain.py`, `analysis/research/body/inverse.py`, `analysis/research/body/ratings.py`.
- **What is wrong & Evidence:**
  1. *Flat-bat speed boost:* `fit.py` fits `chain.py`'s horizontal `qd[4]` directly to [4]'s `bat_az_vel` (`2,435°/s`) with `E = 0°` (`cos E = 1`), yielding `30.6 m/s`. In 3D ([4] has `E = -30°` at contact, `cos(-30°) = 0.866`), `inverse.py` on the same [4] curves gives **`27.0 m/s`** at contact (`inverse.out:47`) — a `3.6 m/s` (`8 mph`) gap that phase 1 must recover through 3D elbow extension, hand speed, and elevation rate while also keeping bat lag at `65°` instead of `50°`.
  2. *`2× coil` is a 2D fixed-geometry artifact, making §14 Question 5 premature:* `ratings.py` Section A held `Height = 1.84 m`, `L_arm = 0.30·H`, `lag_stop = 133°`, and `T_first = 239 ms` constant while rotating `pelvis, trunk, arms, bat` together in 2D (`±26°` for `±15%` strength, `±15°/SD` vs `±7°/SD` in [6]). That suppressed three real 3D contributors to `swing_length`: steeper vertical barrel wrap (`+44° -> -30°`), independent hand/scapular load (blocked in `chain.py` by the fixed `0.30·H` arm and `133°` wrist stop), and stature/limb-length correlation with bat speed across MLB hitters. Asking the owner in §14 Question 5 option (a) to approve `2×` real torso coil *"until phase 1 finds the other part"* reverses the phase order: phase 1 comes *before* phase 3/4's roster code, and D27 forbids baking in a `2×` posture exaggeration.
  3. *"The swing needs no feedback" ignores 3D gravity and millimeter barrel sensitivity:* In 2D, out-of-plane error does not exist. In 3D (`docs/physics-spec.md` §8.3), `2 cm` vertically separates a line drive from a pop-up (`0.83°/mm`). While visual feedback (>150 ms) cannot act mid-swing, zero-stiffness open-loop torques under gravity and 3D centrifugal couples will droop or wobble the swing plane unless muscles carry intrinsic joint impedance (stiffness/damping around the learned trajectory) through the swing as well.
- **What would fix it:** Defer §14 Question 5 until phase 1 measures how much 3D hand load, vertical bat wrap, and stature explain MLB's `0.05–0.09 ft/mph` slope; retain intrinsic muscle stiffness/damping (zero-delay co-contraction impedance, distinct from delayed reflex) during the 3D swing; and add vertical contact-offset σ and MLB deep-contact bat speed (PLAN D30) to phase 1/3's checks.

### P2-5. Pitcher release precision (`0.57°` MLB command vs `3°/ms` curvature at `dt = 1 ms`) and multi-pitch finger spin (§11) lack a workable mechanism

- **Where:** `docs/body-motion.md` §8.1 (line 502), §11 (lines 581–593).
- **What is wrong & Evidence:** §11 notes that hand path speed over radius turns the ball's velocity vector at **`~3°/ms` (`52 mrad/ms`)** and cites Hore & Watts's `1 ms` finger-release timing precision. In `docs/calibration-targets.md` §4.2, however, grade-50 MLB `Command` is `σ_cmd = 10 mrad = 0.57°` (`~0.29°` at grade 80). At `3°/ms`, `1 ms` of release jitter produces `~3°` (`~0.85 m = 33 in` at home plate — 5× wilder than MLB), requiring `~0.19 ms` (`95 µs` at grade 80) effective timing precision unless the arm's kinematics flatten the hand's velocity tangent near release ("launch window" elbow-extension / wrist-flexion compensation, reducing `|dv̂/dt|` to `~0.3–0.6°/ms`). Numerically, locating release by *linear* interpolation inside a `1 ms` RK4 step (§8.1 line 502) during `117 rad/s` (`6.7°/ms`) shoulder internal rotation introduces `O(dt²)` direction errors comparable to elite command. Furthermore, §11's two sentences on the ball *"rolling off the finger pads, whose last contact sets the spin axis"* specify no equations capable of producing distinct spin rates, efficiencies, and axes across all eight arsenal pitches (`FF, SI, FC, SL, ST, CU, CH, FS`). Also, blending a `5 × 5` target grid (§5.5) on a nonlinear 1.5 s delivery risks deterministic between-node aiming biases as large as `Command` itself.
- **What would fix it:** Specify cubic Hermite interpolation or `0.1 ms` sub-stepping around ball release; require phase 5's delivery fit to verify low path curvature (`|dv̂/dt|`) across the release window; model the finger-ball interaction as an explicit grip-parameterized impulse/roll-off law driven by hand kinematics, back-force threshold, and slip noise (rather than rigid-finger contact); and verify grid-blend aiming linearity to `≪ 2 mrad`.

### P3-1. `Coordination` unit and skill folding (§7.4), `Mass`-only torque scaling (§7.1), and un-relearned timings in `ratings.py` Section C

- **Where:** `docs/body-motion.md` §7.1 (line 416), §7.2 (lines 452–462), §7.4 (line 484); `analysis/research/body/ratings.py:37`, `analysis/research/body/ratings.py:143-154`.
- **What is wrong & Evidence:**
  1. §7.4 defines `Coordination` as *"σ of every onset, ms"*, whereas §6.1 and §11 require `~1 ms` at the fingers, `a few ms` at distal joints, and `10–30 ms` at the stride/hips; `Coordination` must be a dimensionless multiplier on per-channel baseline `σ_0,k`.   3.
