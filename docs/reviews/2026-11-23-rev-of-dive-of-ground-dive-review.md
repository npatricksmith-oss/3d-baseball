# Review: the outfielder's dive at a ground ball (rev-of-dive, 2026-11-23)

Reviewer: `rev-of-dive` (Claude Opus, high effort, Code Mode), who wrote none of this change. Range `main...work/of-ground-dive` (`f6eba9b..1ed61ab`: `5c2c556`, `14d1030`, `1ed61ab`). Spec: agent-models §5.3 ("An outfielder has nobody behind him"); note: `docs/calibration/2026-11-23-of-ground-dive.md`.

**Verdict.** The change is correct, keeps invariants 2 and 3, and on balance makes the game better. In a paired old-rule/new-rule probe over 600 games it makes the first changed play better 107 times and worse 19 times: 118 fewer bases for the batter and 24 fewer runs. The note's arithmetic and the ledger's seed-0 figures reproduce exactly, and the mutation claim holds. One design gap is worth a follow-up (P2-1): `dive_gains` still charges a dive only the 1.5 s get-up. A knock-down costs about 2.7 s before he has the ball again, and on the ground the new gate passes every dive an average outfielder can reach. Nothing here blocks the merge.

## Findings

### P2-1. A knock-down is charged as a clean catch: the dive goes for balls he would have had on his feet sooner (`dive_gains`, `live.rs` 7168–7178; agent-models §5.3)

`dive_gains(i, DIVE_GETUP_S)` lets him leave his feet when his standing take is more than 1.5 s away. That is the cost of a dive that catches the ball. Under the old pocket gate nearly every outfield dive was one he expected to catch, so the cost was right. Now on the ground a dive is worth it for the rim, and a rimmed ball costs more. His teammates do not field the deflection: he does, himself, after his get-up. In the probe the diver fielded the rimmed ball again in 90 of 96 cases (83 of 85 for seed 1000), a **median 2.68 s [2.72 s] after the dive**. Meanwhile the gate itself never binds on the ground in games (P3-1), so `dive_gains`, working on his own read, is the only check left.

The paired probe (below) shows the cost where you would expect it. Each row is a first changed play with a new outfield ground dive. "Standing take" is when, in the old-rule game, the same outfielder secured the ball on his feet, counted from the moment he dives in the new game:

| outcome of the dive | standing take < 1.5 s | 1.5–2.7 s | ≥ 2.7 s | another fielder took it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| caught (85) | 7: 0 better, 0 worse | 11: 3 / 0 | 39: 26 / 0 | 28: 21 / 0 |
| rimmed (103) | 13: **1 / 6** | 22: 3 / 2 (17 same) | 43: 34 / 1 | 25: 19 / 1 |
| missed (36) | 4: 0 / **3** | 8: 0 / **4** | 11: 0 / 2 | 13: 0 / 0 |

("better / worse": batter's bases plus twice the runs, minus twice the outs on the play. Seeds 0–299 and 1000–1299.)

- When he would have had the ball on his feet within about 2.7 s, a knock-down gains nothing (4 better and 8 worse of 35) and a miss costs extra bases (7 worse of 12). Examples: seed 193 play 141, a double on his feet (taken 0.52 s later) becomes a triple after a rim; seed 1031 play 293, a single taken standing 0.07 s later becomes a double; seed 52 play 2, a double taken 0.24 s later becomes a triple after a miss.
- 24 of the 224 dives (11%) were at balls the world says he would have taken on his feet within the 1.5 s that `dive_gains` itself requires. His read told him otherwise. That is a believable misjudgment, and the old pocket gate used to filter most of those reads out.
- When the standing take was 2.7 s or more away, or someone else would have fielded it, the dive is almost all gain (100 better, 2 worse of 159).

Would a real outfielder do it? Laying out to stop a ball in the gap that he cannot otherwise reach before the wall: yes. Laying out to knock down a ball he could have cut off a couple of seconds later on his feet: rarely. So the spec's new line "Missing it costs him what staying up would have: the ball is past him either way" is not true as written: he lies down 1.5 s, and 18 of the 23 [14 of 23] missed balls were then fielded by another outfielder. **Suggested** (owner's call, since it touches a decided rule): on the ground, weigh the dive against the standing take with the knock-down's own cost, the get-up plus his walk to a ball leaving the glove at the rim's speed. That can be derived from `DIVE_GETUP_S` and the deflection law, with no new knob. Re-measure the table above after the change. Not a blocker: the net effect is clearly positive.

### P3-1. On the ground the gate is dead in games, and no test pins it (`live.rs` 7003–7004; tests)

Every game fielder has `hands` 0.018 (`game.rs` 250, `FielderAttrs::average`). At the worst point in the envelope (2.5 m, any ball under about 300 m/s) the rim chance is at least 0.74, above 0.5. So in games, at a ground ball, `OF_DIVE_MIN_P` never keeps an outfielder up: on the ground outfielders now behave like infielders ("always go"). The spec's "only poor hands … keep him up" applies only to custom fielders. Mutation check: making the gate always pass on the ground (copy, mode 2) **left all 11 related tests passing**. `an_outfielder_stays_up_for_a_grounder_he_can_take_on_his_feet_or_cannot_reach` checks geometry and timing (the stretch, the 2.5 m envelope, the 0.24 s lunge), which `a_ball_just_past_reach_is_taken_with_a_stretch_not_a_dive` and `a_dive_needs_its_lunge_time` already cover with sure hands. Suggest one test that pins the ground side of the gate: a fielder with `hands` 0.03 at a grounder passing about 2.4 m away stays up. In `an_outfielder_lays_out_for_a_grounder_passing_within_his_dive`, "kept it by him" means the ball is held or slower than 8 m/s 0.8 s in. That is effectively "touched it" (a rim leaves the glove at no more than 0.45 of the ball's speed), not "near him". It is acceptable, but the test does not check where the ball is.

### P3-2. Two figures in the spec and note are off

- agent-models §5.3 (new paragraph): "only poor hands (σ_hands above about **0.03 m** at full stretch) keep him up". At full stretch the multiplier is 3.8 (a slow ball) to 4.05 (40 m/s), so the threshold is σ_hands = 0.1019/mult = **0.025–0.027 m**. The note's "about 0.025 m" is right; the spec should say the same.
- Note §2 and spec: a rimmed 16 m/s ball "leaves the glove at 5–7 m/s" / "about a third of the ball's speed". Measured on outfield ground-ball rims: **median 3.8 m/s** out of a median 15.9 m/s in (about a quarter), at most 8.6 [9.1] m/s. The law is `0.3·v + 0.15·|v|·push`, and the push often opposes the ball (next item). The conclusion still holds: the ball stays near him (median 3.5 m [3.1 m] from where he left his feet, 90th percentile 7.8 m [4.6 m], 1 of 181 reached the wall).

### P3-3 (pre-existing, not introduced here). The rim's push is in fixed world axes, not a collision

`live.rs` 7146–7147: `push = (ex, −|ez|, ez).normalized()`, so a deflection always gains a component toward home (−Y), and its sideways direction comes from the glove error, whatever the ball's direction or the glove's normal. §5.4 says the deflection is "solved by the physics collision model with glove e_n ≈ 0.15 (physics spec §9.4)". It is not. For an outfield grounder rolling away from home, the push always slows it, which favours the defence. This change now relies on that law for about half its dives (96 of 209 ground dives were rims), so it matters more than before. Leave it to its own task.

### P3-4. The deep-call test lost its centre-field takeover case (`throw_choices.rs`, `14d1030`)

Dropping seed 118 play 162 was right. Put back into the test on the branch (in a copy), it fails at its premise, "seed 118 play 162: no relay man out with the ball down": the right fielder now knocks the grounder down. The branch does not touch the jobs code, so nothing is broken now. But the specific rule "the deep call does not flip back as the centre fielder takes over from the right fielder" has no test left, and the remaining two plays are balls to left. This is a regression-guard gap, so P3, not P2. Candidates from the branch's own games (runner on first; the ball down early through the right side, passing within about 4 m of the right fielder, fielded by the centre fielder 117–120 m out with no dive), found by a probe but **not checked against the test's premises**: seed 36 play 158 (two out, as 118-162 was), seed 198 play 108 (two out), seed 204 play 174 and seed 29 play 51 (one out). Freeze one from the merged `main`.

## Answers to the job's questions

1. **Invariants 2 and 3.** "On the ground" is `Sim::ball_still_up()` (`live.rs` 5283): whether the current free ball's first bounce has already happened (`path.bounces.first().0 > self.t`). That is a fact of the past that anyone can see, and the same one `dive_gains`, `expire_peels` and others already use. A future bounce time is never compared with anything but now, so it reveals only "not down yet". σ_g uses his belief's speed and distance (`fd.belief.at(self.t)`, 7000–7002). The envelope (`how`, world `b`) is physical feasibility and was already there. No `Executed*` is read: `GlovePlacement` is drawn after the decision, at 7033. A ball that has bounced and is up on a hop at 1 m is "on the ground", so the rim applies (and the dive needs z ≤ 1.2 m anyway). That is what a real outfielder weighs: a hop he knocks down is stopped as surely as a roller. A rimmed ball off a glove is "in the air" again for the few tenths of a second before its first bounce. That is harmless.
2. **The rim's value.** The law is above (P3-3). Measured on the branch's outfield ground-ball dives: seeds 0 [1000] gave 209 [186] dives, 0.70 [0.62] a game; caught 90 [78] (43% [42%]), rimmed 96 [85], missed 23 [23]. Rimmed balls stay near him, as the note says (P3-2), and he fields them again himself in 2.7 s. **The batter's bases after a rim: out 1 [0], single 19 [27], double 64 [52], triple 11 [6], home run 1 [0]**, against 4/57/26/3/0 [1/42/31/4/0] after a catch and 0/1/1/20/1 [0/6/1/16/0] after a miss. So a knock-down mostly turns a triple into a double. The gate is not too generous in the "squirts to the wall" sense. It is generous in the timing sense (P2-1).
3. **As baseball.** Dives of 3.0 a game and outfield ground-ball dives of 0.7 a game are reproduced. I have no sourced MLB rate for outfield layouts at ground balls, and the note gives none (its "MLB about 2–3" is for all dives, without a source). The case the job asked about (a standing take later than the get-up but well before the wall) exists, and `dive_gains` does not rule it out: P2-1. A knock-down becomes a double two times in three, partly because the get-up is 1.5 s (`DIVE_GETUP_S`, pre-existing). Real outfielders are usually up faster than that; not for this branch.
4. **Tests.** The grounder test and the play-71 fixture are independent: geometry from the §5.3/§5.4 radii and the play's own contact. The moved in-air tests check their own premise (the liner is still up as it passes; the fixture asserts its first bounce comes after). The mutation claim holds: with the old rule (pocket on the ground too) the grounder test fails ("the center fielder stayed up") and so does the play-71 fixture ("the right fielder's dives: []"). With rim everywhere, `an_outfielder_does_not_dive_for_a_ball_he_will_probably_miss` and `an_outfielder_weighs_a_dive_by_his_own_read_of_the_ball` fail, so the in-air side is pinned. The ground side's threshold is not (P3-1). What the play-71 fixture asserts (one dive, before 3.5 s, from beyond his stretch and within his dive) is the decision this change makes. The outcome is the glove draw's, so printing it rather than asserting it is right.
5. **Seed 118 play 162.** Dropping it was right; there is a coverage gap: P3-4, with candidates.
6. **The numbers.** Reproduced: P3-2 and the Verification section. The "at least 0.74 anywhere in the envelope" claim holds for balls up to 40 m/s (the fastest ground ball an outfielder dove at in 600 games was 24.2 m/s, largest σ_g 0.070 m).
7. **Docs.** agent-models §5.3 matches the code, apart from the 0.03 m figure (P3-2) and the "costs him what staying up would have" sentence (P2-1). §5.4's "bobbles that stay close can be re-fielded" is borne out. Its "solved by the physics collision model" was already untrue (P3-3). TASKS §2 and observations item 71 are accurate: play 71 is still a triple on its glove draw, and triples fall by about a seventh. The a4 README marks 118-162 unused.
