# Independent review of the body-motion design

**Verdict — sound to build phase 1 on with named fixes.** Keep the forward, force-driven body.  
The committed planar results reproduce. They establish a useful example of a driven swing and leave 3D control, contact and individual variation open.  
Before using the probes as phase 1 gates, specify the causal clock, replace the wrist stop and test actual contact, correct the inverse-dynamics comparison, and require independent checks of the hands and ground.  
Keep the full body as the checking model. Decide on ratings, production cost and any reduced body after measuring what that model can do. Preserve recorded replay under D9.

## P1 findings

### P1-1. The decision clock and heel trigger do not yet describe one causal motion

**Where** [Design §§2, 5.4–5.5, 6.1, 8.2 and 10](../body-motion.md#54-the-batters-timing-when-his-body-is-real) and [breakdowns.py](../../analysis/research/body/breakdowns.py#L68).

The fit starts core drive at −238 ms, shoulder drive at −231 ms and leg yaw drive at −228 ms. In the design, the arms wait for the decision plus latency, and the walk-through's heel-plant option triggers the hips around −170 ms, after they have already begun under D22. The fit can initialize a body from a measured state for a laboratory comparison. It cannot give the in-game clock without reconciling those events. The probe's wait is a preset toe-down time plus 30 ms. With the foot 160 ms late, leg drive begins at −150 ms and the model's own 50% load event comes at −41 ms. The shifted core starts earlier still. The wait also moves the lead brake and shoulder onsets while keeping the leg and core off times fixed. Its +85 ms result describes that particular controller, with a 5.5 m/s loss even relative to the already weakened fixed-clock swing. “Late but intact” misstates the result.

Specify a pre-commit hip drive that starts on every pitch, a separately gated increase in drive, and the arms' commitment. Choose a sensed trigger with a definition, latency and fallback. Fortenbaugh defines foot down and weight commitment by force thresholds. Neither identifies heel contact. Once the player starts an action, changing his estimate of the pitch, learning about a late foot or executing with less strength can change only future effort. Cache only through the first possible divergence in input, perception, fatigue or contact. Grouping by pitch speed is permissible using the batter's current expectation. Choosing or reloading an earlier stride from the true speed after release would violate D31 and invariant 2. If two pitches share every available cue to a given time, their nominal plans and executed motions must also share the prefix for the same motor draws. Test that property through a take, a changed read, a late plant and a weak swing. Estimate the commit deadline from the nominal time to the reachable hitting region **given the prefix already in progress**. The +6, +21 and +49 ms meetings from delaying the arms 30, 60 and 90 ms rule out treating arms-to-contact time as a constant under those changes.

### P1-2. The wrist result depends on a device that can decide whether the barrel reaches the point

**Where** [Design §§4.2–4.3 and 5.1](../body-motion.md#43-how-well-the-reduced-model-reproduces-the-measured-swing), [chain.py](../../analysis/research/body/chain.py#L181), [fit.py](../../analysis/research/body/fit.py#L151), and [checks.py](../../analysis/research/body/checks.py#L62).

The fitted wrist onset is +74 ms, beyond the +30 ms fit horizon. Setting its strength and rise to zero leaves the cost exactly 0.5202764915747476. “The wrists switched themselves off” assigns physical meaning to a channel that the fit cannot see. The 133° figure is the maximum angle between the bat and the clavicle-to-wrist vector in Fortenbaugh's 3D data. Assigning that value to a passive planar hinge stop is a modelling choice with no anatomical bound behind it. The stop reaches 257.5 N·m and applies torque of either sign during engagement. A true motion limit can react at a joint, but the penalty model also permits a pulling reaction on rebound when its damping term dominates. A single hinge conflates two wrist joints with the couple the arms can deliver through two grips.

The 20 N·m capped-stop check preserves the **azimuth crossing** speed and changes lag at that crossing from 50° to 63°. With the stop disabled, my first fixed-point crossing was 8.7 ms on the probe clock at 0.905 m from the hands, beyond the 0.784 m bat end. The −8° crossing still occurred. The claim that the swing does not depend on the stop is too broad. In phase 1 use separately bounded wrist drives and hand constraints, with a sourced or explicitly assumed anatomical range and a passive limit that cannot pull. Test with and without the limit, record its work and reaction torque, and use actual finite bat contact for the comparison. Delp's roughly 10 N·m per wrist is a static deviation measurement and a useful bound to explore. It neither bounds the couple through two hands nor establishes the wrists' dynamic capacity. [Fortenbaugh, Appendix C and Table 1](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/991031448086302976), [Delp et al., abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8884484/)

Adding 3D lag to bat azimuth cannot reconstruct the projected arm azimuth. Separate angle and rate extractions also need a consistency check before differentiating. These approximations matter more than the duplicate translation in the main result. Removing the duplicate in memory changed required angular impulse from 35.3 to 35.4 N·m·s, versus 12.9 from reported shear in the adopted frame. The additional export flip accounts for the probe's positive mapping of **plotted** GRFy. Use that transformation explicitly and check the exported centre-of-pressure signs before using them. ### P1-4. Reintegrating an old drive program on a new build breaches D9

**Where** [Design §§8.1, 9–10](../body-motion.md#9-what-the-timeline-carries-and-what-the-client-draws-141-item-7) and [PLAN D9 and §4.2](../../PLAN.md#42-determinism).

Bit-for-bit replay from inputs is a same-build guarantee. Model revisions, constraint ordering, activation and event changes alter old motion. Even with the same build, the proposed packet omits controller history, sensed inputs or their executed responses, and the collision impulse if the follow-through responds to the ball. A drawn second hand cannot be guaranteed to stay on the bat when the reduced physics omits that arm. Save the actual trajectories needed for playback and budget their sampling and compression against measured pose error. A complete, versioned executed-drive and event stream can additionally support same-build diagnosis. Specify whether the body receives the existing bat/ball collision's reaction impulse and how play resumes after it. A physical response may affect only the continuation of the motion.

This fixture can remain as a geometric sensitivity check. Use the full bat, moving ball and collision for the breakdown goldens, and report misses, contact location, collision speed and resulting launch. A further-round bat alone gives neither the spray angle nor a rollover. At the cited distances and constant speeds, the changeup is about 90 ms later, about 25 ms short of the lunge's fixed-point delay. This comparison cannot establish on-time contact. The bar should include one perturbation at a time and comparisons with onset-only and whole-window shifts. See [owner walkthrough](../research/body/0-owner-walkthrough.md).

### P2-2. The average fit identifies a motion in one model, with weak evidence for its drive parameters

**Where** [Design §§4.3 and 5.1–5.2](../body-motion.md#43-how-well-the-reduced-model-reproduces-the-measured-swing) and [fit.py](../../analysis/research/body/fit.py#L125).

The loss adds several weighted squared residuals at each sampled time and divides by the number of times. The reported 0.52 is neither an estimate of fit uncertainty nor a per-observable SD criterion. Most observables were used in the fit. Pelvis and trunk finish 13° and 12° short, lag is 15° short, and bat peak is late. The motion starts at a measured mean state with inferred arm azimuth and shared initial bat and arm rates. Fortenbaugh §3.5 describes averaging successful middle fastballs within hitter and then across hitters relative to ball contact. The five event lines label phases and help locate the digitised time axis. The source does not support alignment on all five events. Differentiating a filtered, averaged trajectory gives the torque of that approximation. It does not estimate mean muscle drive, the reliability of a late peak, or a universal resolution of 40 ms.

The refit moved the lead force scale 960 to 1,125 N and advanced its onset 18 ms while reducing the objective by about 1%. Active duration, amplitude, geometry, the grip reaction, friction and the force–velocity curve can substitute for one another. The core switch-off is after contact, and the fitted shoulder is effectively left on through the modelled swing. Treat post-contact switch-off as a new braking choice with tests. At the sample of the reported 219 N·m core peak the relative rate is 2.70 rad/s, and the peak is after the azimuth crossing. Before the crossing the maximum was about 207 N·m. Both exceed the 173 N·m mass-scaled golfer mean at 0.52 rad/s, even before correcting for speed. The 352 N·m isometric coefficient is roughly twice that scaled mean. The comparison is a plausibility flag, with different tasks, axes, posture and active versus passive/net torque. Golf model generators and a measured shoulder abduction torque cannot validate yaw drive. The high-speed part of each lumped force–velocity law is extrapolated. In phase 1 use explicit priors and sensitivities on strength, force–length, speed and activation, multiple fit starts, and hold out kinematics or pitch conditions. Report each residual and where parameters saturate. ### P2-3. Planted feet and a one-axis balance probe do not establish that a 3D swing runs open loop

**Where** [Design §§4.1–4.2, 5.3 and 8.1](../body-motion.md#53-what-keeps-a-driven-body-on-its-path), [chain.py](../../analysis/research/body/chain.py#L149) and [balance.py](../../analysis/research/body/balance.py#L25).

The swing cannot fall in the planar model. Its centre moves along one axis, feet are fixed, and the programmed split gives exactly one body weight in total vertical force. Fortenbaugh's lead foot alone reaches about 1.3 body weights. The rear foot retains a free moment even when the lead foot has no load. Fore-aft traction, lateral traction and twist draw separately on the same contact, so they can collectively exceed a physical sole's wrench bound. The cap is a causal surrogate. In 3D resolve the contact wrench together, with nonpenetration, friction, centre of pressure, roll or pivot, and loss of support. Use the elbow excursions, both hands' loop and measurement frame to test height and tilt. Support loss, trunk lean and errors in one arm could matter at 250 ms even when a new voluntary command cannot arrive in time.

The balance probe demonstrates disturbance rejection by a bounded one-axis controller with an assumed trajectory and gains. For the 1.5 s case it allows 0.30 m fore-aft centre of pressure on either side before the last 0.28 s, despite calling the pitcher's leg lift single support. It has no lifted leg, lateral balance or landing impulse. “Fall” means 15° lean. Loram and Lakie's 0.91 ± 0.23 is quiet-standing intrinsic ankle stiffness, attributed in that experiment mainly to foot, tendon and aponeurosis. It is not a measured swing gain. Test feedforward first, but perturb stance, foot friction, ground contact and all three rotational axes across a plausible range, with bounded stiffness and delayed proprioception available. A causal posture reference that responds through bounded forces is a legitimate control model. Hard pose tracking, even during “quiet” phases, would replace the dynamics. [Loram and Lakie, primary paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12482906/)

### P2-4. The integrator checks have real power and limited coverage

**Where** [Design §8.1](../body-motion.md#81-the-integrator), [chain.py](../../analysis/research/body/chain.py#L207) and [checks.py](../../analysis/research/body/checks.py#L25).

The kinetic energy reconstructed from point velocities and the forward momentum check are real consistency checks for the reduced equations. In an in-memory variant removing the velocity-dependent acceleration terms, work mismatch rose from 0.00245 J to 746 J and internal forward momentum drift from about 7 × 10⁻¹⁰ to −69.6 N·s. A wrong core force sign applied consistently to equations and work changed trunk rate from +0.15 to −12.18 rad/s by −170 ms while work still balanced within 1.5 mJ over that interval. The checks can catch a missing inertial term or a sign error in mapping otherwise correct force to acceleration. They cannot verify masses, torque directions shared by force and power, or the external ground model. Free motion should assert the stops are inactive, and the tests should assert finite results and residual tolerances over the whole interval.

For 3D specify how the integrator handles constraint drift, unilateral contact and impacts. Held activation, interpolation over a discontinuity and projection can all set the observed convergence rate. The 2 ms result is ≈0.02 ms from the finest, but the 1 ms result is further away, and the event is a yaw threshold. Check analytic free-body and constrained cases, internal action–reaction and angular momentum, work from measured external forces, joint-limit energy, and actual event-time convergence. Use a deterministic solve and define tie order, any projection or substepping, and what happens on failure.

My unshifted 0.70 and 1.30 strength trials crossed the azimuth at +28.9 and −16.9 ms with small but nonzero fixed-point differences. Neither is an in-game contact test.

### P2-6. The error model is causal in form, but its scales are underdetermined

**Where** [Design §§5.3, 6.1, 7.4 and 10](../body-motion.md#61-where-each-error-enters), [research note 6](../research/body/6-movement-variability.md) and [PLAN §13](../../PLAN.md#13-calibration-and-verification).

Onset jitter, shared and channel-specific effort, stride-drive variation and grip variation can enter at physical or control inputs. Calibrating a few population noise scales to measured output distributions is allowed by §13. Solving a particular pitch's inputs against its resulting contact or release violates D31. Calibrating across a league also cannot rescue broken Tier A mechanics. In the probe the toe-down time is a direct schedule input. In the full body perturb the stride drive or starting posture and let touch-down follow from contact. For slip vary grip/contact parameters. Preserve positive windows, reachability and covariance among onsets and offsets. A swing with jitter alone must still have a plausible physical cause.

Within-player depth and speed include pitch location, approach, counts, deliberate effort and selection on swings that can be tracked. The conversion of 0.27 m to 6.7 ms attributes all depth variation to timing. Pitcher release ellipses include delivery variation at multiple links, and their 3–6 cm conversion assumes a projected bivariate normal data ellipse. Jegede's 7 ms is a measured spread expressed as a release window in a throwing experiment, with a separate definition from an allowable timing window or an onset SD. Nakashima's 9.36 ± 6.25 ms is a sweet-spot tolerance across combinations of separately measured bat and pitch paths. Hore and Watts' precision is as low as 1 ms with ball back-force, not a generic jitter coefficient. Two marginal batting SDs cannot identify six onset SDs, their correlations, perception noise and effort. Start with a few identifiable common factors, sensitivity at fixed pitch and aim, and held-out covariances, matched tasks and miss rates. Label the inferred channel scales [E] and give ranges. ### P2-8. Pitch release needs a model of the ball while held

**Where** [Design §§4.1, 6.1, 11 and 13](../body-motion.md#11-the-pitcher-at-design-depth).

A series-elastic shoulder is a reasonable candidate. Give the element deformation, stiffness, damping and a joint/actuator geometry, and compare energy storage and recoil as well as speed. ASMI's internal-rotation torques around 90–100 N·m are inverse-dynamics values near maximal external rotation. The 90–120 range includes horizontal adduction. The 115 rad/s is a peak velocity elsewhere in the delivery, so those numbers cannot establish a simultaneous torque demand or rule out active contribution throughout acceleration. Attias's one starter and the common group timing fractions are useful references. They do not establish that all repertoire, strength or arm-slot changes can be captured by one delivery shape.

Finger opening can initiate a change in grip. Detachment should follow when the accelerating ball can no longer be held, with forces, normal compliance, slip and finger motion carrying it through separation. The last contact location alone cannot determine spin. Integrate the torques from the contact forces on the ball through the grip and roll, and pass the ball's own position, velocity and angular velocity into flight. Hand state is one input. Specify how a learned grip and aim are chosen and what happens when release is premature or fails. Use local path curvature and full flight to measure directions for each hand, grip and arm slot. “Up and arm-side” and its opposite, a backup slider, a collapsed block and fatigue reducing speed are tests of mechanisms with possible exceptions. Hard-coding those directions would decide outcomes. The 3°/ms radius estimate would imply nearly a metre at the plate per millisecond if applied literally, so it is a sensitivity hypothesis [E]. Kinoshita's forces **peak** 4–7 ms before separation, with earlier peaks too. The paper does not report they **end** then. Make release accuracy a convergence test against finer event location. ## Remaining answers

- **D31 and invariants.** Fixed onset, rise and switch-off commands, physical limits, physically bounded delayed feedback, and later braking are causal. Blending adjacent *unstarted* commands is an honest approximation if they share a reachable prefix, respect ordering and latency, and are allowed to miss. Mid-motion changes on a scheduled off time and check-swing braking can affect only future force. A stopping outcome still comes from contact and the rules. A one-time search on synthetic practice tasks can be consistent with D31 if Nathan approves the interpretation. Use nominal inputs, reachable regions and realistic timing and effort ranges, let some aims fail, and freeze the library before gameplay or motor draws. An optimizer selecting every player's same contact instant and even the same travel time as a fallback would preserve the old restriction in the player library. Search against recorded motions to identify model parameters is a separate lab operation. The probe's exact per-trial time alignment is diagnostic and should stay outside normal execution.
- **Agent boundary and deterministic replay.** §§5.5, 6 and 10 put execution draws in motor, and there is no outcome roll in these probes or proposed core. Document where the true body becomes a delayed/noisy proprioceptive sample. Decision code may receive an observation with a timestamp through the established boundary. It cannot receive true pose, exact future events or an executed drive. The body integrator takes its already specified motor noise and feedback inputs without drawing RNG. Give new keyed purposes and stable subindices to actuator, feedback and grip draws and fix their order, so a branch does not shift unrelated draws. Running pitcher motion first for offline orchestration is safe only when the batter's consumer receives cues in simulated time. §8's fixed order and libm are a good start.
