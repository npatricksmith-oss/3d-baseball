## Summary of Findings

- **P1-1 (Sacrifice-Fly Gap at 225–300 ft — Decision Rule & Ball Time)**: Runners on third hold on almost every fly ball caught between `225` and `300 ft` (`0%` at `225–250 ft`, `6.7%` at `250–275 ft`, `26.5%` at `275–300 ft` vs. MLB `~40%` at `225–275 ft` and `~90%` at `275–300 ft`) because **both** sides of the inequality in `runner_decide` (`sim/crates/bb_game/src/live.rs:2603`) are skewed against advancing at medium depth:
  1. **The decision rule (`t_ball - (self.t + t_run) + noise > 0.35` with $\sigma = 0.15\text{ s}$)** requires a $+2.33\sigma$ margin ($\hat{P}(\text{safe}) = \Phi(0.35 / 0.15) = \mathbf{99.0\%}$ at the median draw), ignoring the run-expectancy rule in `docs/agent-models.md` §6.3 (`lines 586–592`), where the breakeven safe rate for scoring from third with one out (second out made on the catch) is **$33.1\%$** ($32.5\%$ in Olszewski 2015; $73.1\%$ with zero outs), and ignoring the strong right skew of long-throw arrival/tag times when throws miss the plate laterally.
  2. **The ball's side is `0.30–0.40 s` too fast at `250–280 ft` (`76–85 m`)**, which is why lowering the `0.35 s` margin alone in the current world would produce unrealistic outs at the plate: every outfield throw leaves at `100%` of the Statcast **top-10% (90th-percentile)** max-effort arm speed (`THROW_EFFORT = 1.0`, `87.4–90.7 mph`) on an un-bounced high trajectory (`solve_throw` up to `35°`) rather than typical throw effort (`~0.94–0.96`) on a lower one-hop line (`+0.15–0.20 s` bounce loss); outfield catch-to-release uses a flat `0.85 s` transfer with zero crow-hop/momentum-reversal cost when catching below `FACE_MIN_MPS = 3.0 m/s`; `read_throw_time` (`live.rs:155–158`) caps believed flight time with a halfway relay (`0.525 s` transfer) that reaches home in `3.01 s` from `280 ft`; and in the world (`live.rs:2443–2445, 2887`), throws at `85 m` ($\sigma_{\text{lat}} = 85\sin 1.2^\circ = 1.78\text{ m}$) that miss the plate by up to `1.35 m` (`4.4 ft`) pay **zero** off-line tag penalty (`on_bag = true`) while `tag_reach_m = 1.0 m` lets the catcher tag the runner `1.0 m` (`0.12 s`) *before* he reaches the plate whenever `ball_at + 0.25 s` precedes `runner_at - 0.12 s`.
- **P1-2 (Base-Holding Read — `still_since` Not Reset When a Stopped or Retreating Runner Breaks Forward)**: In `move_runners` (`sim/crates/bb_game/src/live.rs:2630–2635`), `still_since` is set the first tick a runner's signed speed is $\le \text{SEEN\_MOVING\_MPS}$ (`0.5 m/s`) and is only cleared once `r.direction * r.v > 0.5 m/s`. When a runner ahead has been tagging up on a base, waiting at a base, or retreating to a base (`direction = -1.0`), his `still_since` is already $\gg \text{HOLD\_READ\_S}$ (`0.2 s`) old at the instant he decides to advance (`goal = b + 1`). Because accelerating from `v = 0` to `> 0.5 m/s` takes `3` ticks (`0.05 s`) and trailing runners (`k = 0, 1`) are stepped before lead runners (`k = 2, 3`), `HOLD_READ_S = 0.2 s` provides **zero** grace period when a tagging, waiting, or retouching runner breaks for the next base: any trailing runner heading for `b` during those 3 ticks sees `self.t - t0 >= 0.2 s` and is falsely turned back (`live.rs:2705–2707`).
- **P2-1 (Invariant 2 / Future Individual Arms — `ball_time_to` Hardcodes `FielderAttrs::average(*by)`)**: In `ball_time_to` (`sim/crates/bb_game/src/live.rs:2491–2494`), the runner reads `let mean = FielderAttrs::average(*by);` and uses `mean.arm + ARM_READ_SIGMA_MPS * err`. While this uses only public positional knowledge now, it completely ignores `self.f[*by].a.arm`. When players receive individual arm grades (`FielderAttrs::with_throwing`), runners will be completely blind to whether an outfielder has an 80-grade or 20-grade arm, contradicting the comment at `live.rs:160–162` (*"so he knows a strong arm from a weak one only roughly"*).

## Detailed Answers to Review Questions

### 1. Invariant 2 (Agents Never See World State)

- **What the runner reads in `ball_time_to` (`sim/crates/bb_game/src/live.rs:2485–2495`)**:
  - When `Ball::Held { by }`, the runner uses:
    1. Who holds the ball (`*by`, position index `0..9`) and where that fielder is (`self.ball_at(self.t)`), plus when the catch was secured (`self.secured_at`).
    2. That position's grade-50 `transfer` (`0.85 s` outfield, `0.65 s` infield) and grade-50 `arm` (`arm_strength_50_mps(*by)`), via `let mean = FielderAttrs::average(*by);`.
    3. A per-runner, per-fielder, per-play normal draw `err` (`ARM_READ_SIGMA_MPS = 1.1 m/s`, keyed by `(seed, play, r.id, Purpose::RunnerPerception, (1 << 20) + *by as u32)`).
  - Every input (`*by`, fielder position, catch time, and positional scouting averages) is something a runner or his base coach can see or know before the pitch. (Note that `self.ball_at(self.t)` for a held ball reads the holder's exact coordinates without distance noise, unlike `runner_predict` on a free ball, though at tag-up distances a stationary fielder's position is visually clear.)
- **When players get individual arms, will this read the true arm? (`P2-1`)**:
  - **No — it will ignore the individual arm completely.** Because `live.rs:2491` calls `FielderAttrs::average(*by)` rather than reading a noisy/shrunk version of `self.f[*by].a.arm`, a runner facing an 80-grade RF (`43.86 m/s`) or a 20-grade RF (`37.26 m/s`) configured via `FielderAttrs::with_throwing` gets the exact same `mean.arm = 40.55 m/s` (`90.7 mph`) plus the same random draw `err`.
  - That contradicts the comment at `live.rs:160–162` (*"A runner's read of a fielder's arm is his position's grade-50 arm ... off by this σ ... so he knows a strong arm from a weak one only roughly"*): adding noise to a positional constant does not distinguish a strong arm from a weak one at that position. When individual player ratings are active, the runner's scouting read should be centered on (or Bayes-shrunk toward `mean.arm` from) `self.f[*by].a.arm` with error `ARM_READ_SIGMA_MPS * err` (e.g., `0.5 * (mean.arm + self.f[*by].a.arm) + ARM_READ_SIGMA_MPS * err` or `self.f[*by].a.arm + ARM_READ_SIGMA_MPS * err`) so that he knows a strong arm from a weak one roughly without seeing exact attribute truth.
- **Does `base_held_ahead` or any remaining path read private goals or world truth? (`P1-2`)**:
  - `base_held_ahead` (`live.rs:2564–2569`) no longer reads `o.goal`; it checks `o.still_since.is_some_and(|t0| self.t - t0 >= HOLD_READ_S)` where `still_since` tracks when `o.direction * o.v <= SEEN_MOVING_MPS` (`0.5 m/s`).
  - The only remaining reads of `r.goal` outside `r`'s own movement are in `live.rs:2941` (`ScorerCarryFact.batter_goal`) and `live.rs:3056` (`ScorerThrowFact.batter_goal`), both of which are world-side scorer evidence never consulted by any agent, plus `choice_trace::RunnerView.intended_goal` (`live.rs:2840`, opt-in diagnostic trace).
  - **However (`P1-2`)**, `still_since` has a logic bug whenever a runner ahead was standing on or retreating to base `b` before deciding to advance:
    - In `move_runners` (`live.rs:2630–2635`), `still_since` is set as soon as `r.direction * r.v <= 0.5 m/s` and is **not** reset when `runner_decide` sets `r.goal = b + 1` (after a catch on a tag-up at `live.rs:2673`, a ball dropping in at `live.rs:2681`, a base clearing ahead at `live.rs:2698`, or a retouch at `live.rs:2765`).
    - When that runner starts from rest (`v = 0`) at `b`, his speed on the first 3 ticks (`DT = 1/60 s`, $\tau = 0.68\text{ s}$) is $0.20, 0.40\text{ m/s} \le 0.5\text{ m/s}$. Because `still_since` still holds the timestamp from when he first stopped or started retreating (often $1\text{–}3\text{ s}$ earlier), `self.t - t0 >= HOLD_READ_S` (`0.2 s`) is **already true** during those 3 ticks!
    - Thus `HOLD_READ_S = 0.2 s` fails to do what its comment (`live.rs:620–622`) claims (*"A runner who has just decided to go is still under `SEEN_MOVING_MPS` for about three ticks"*), and because trailing runners (`k = 0, 1`) are stepped before lead runners (`k = 2, 3`) in `move_runners`, a trailing runner advancing toward `b` can be falsely sent back to `b - 1` on the very tick the runner at `b` breaks for `b + 1`.

### 2. Physics (`RUNNER_TAU = 0.68 s`, `vmax = 8.2 m/s`, Batter Box Exit, and `tag_apply_s = 0.25 s`)

- **Checking the `RUNNER_TAU = 0.68 s` fit and `vmax = 8.2 m/s` against Statcast (`P2-3`)**:
  - Under the exponential speed law $v(t) = v_{\max}(1 - e^{-t/\tau})$, distance is $d(t) = v_{\max}(t - \tau(1 - e^{-t/\tau}))$.
  - For $d = 27.432\text{ m}$ (`90 ft`), $v_{\max} = 8.2\text{ m/s}$, and $\tau = 0.68\text{ s}$:
    - Continuous `run_time(27.432, 8.2, 0.68)` gives $t = 27.432 / 8.2 + 0.68(1 - e^{-4.0235/0.68}) = 3.3454 + 0.6782 = \mathbf{4.0235\text{ s}}$.
    - The 60 Hz semi-implicit Euler loop in `move_runners` (`live.rs:2728–2730`) reaches `27.432 m` in `241` ticks = **`4.0167 s`**.
  - **Is `vmax = 8.2 m/s` right against Statcast's `27.0 ft/s` average, and is the `4.03 s` target right for an average runner?**
    1. `27.0 ft/s` is $27.0 \times 0.3048 = \mathbf{8.2296\text{ m/s}}$ (`8.2 m/s` is `26.90 ft/s`, `0.36%` low). Moreover, Statcast Sprint Speed is the runner's **best 1-second window**, where $\bar{v}_{[3.02, 4.02]} = 8.15\text{ m/s}$ (`26.74 ft/s`) under the exponential curve, so an asymptotic $v_{\max}$ of `8.25–8.28 m/s` corresponds to a `27.0 ft/s` best-second window.
    2. More importantly, as recorded in `docs/calibration/2026-11-14-tag-ups.md` §1 (`lines 42–48`), the Statcast 2017–2020 sacrifice-fly sample ($n = 848$, mean `4.03 s`) has a mean runner Sprint Speed of **`27.62 ft/s` (`8.42 m/s`)** due to selection (faster runners reach third and attempt close tag-ups), with a regression slope of `-0.115 s` per `ft/s`. For a league-average **`27.0 ft/s`** runner (`0.62 ft/s` slower than `27.62 ft/s`), that same Statcast regression predicts:
       $$t_{90,\text{ avg}} = 4.03\text{ s} + 0.62 \times 0.115\text{ s} = \mathbf{4.10\text{ s}}$$
       Fitting `4.02 s` at `8.2 m/s` (`26.90 ft/s`) makes an average runner `~0.08 s` too fast on a standing 90-ft run and requires a steep initial acceleration of $a_0 = v_{\max}/\tau = 12.06\text{ m/s}^2 = 1.23g$ (covering `0–30 ft` in `1.75 s` vs. Savant's `~1.83 s` 30-ft split). With $v_{\max} = 8.25\text{ m/s}$ and $\tau = 0.76\text{ s}$, a `27.0 ft/s` runner runs `4.08 s` while a `27.62 ft/s` (`8.42 m/s`) runner runs `4.01 s`, matching both the sample mean and the league-average split.
- **Should the batter's home-to-first time include getting out of the box after a swing (LHB vs. RHB)? (`P2-3`)**:
  - **Yes.** Currently, `live.rs` gives every batter `BATTER_START = 0.25 s` (`live.rs:79`; note `play.rs:181` still defines `BATTER_START = 0.30`) and starts `r.s` at `0.0` (`27.432 m` to first base) regardless of handedness (`live.rs:3197`).
  - Meanwhile, `Sim::runner_pos` (`live.rs:3092–3110`) visually offsets the batter by `batter_box * (1.0 - r.s / BOX_EXIT_M)` over the first `4.0 m`, claiming (`live.rs:3095–3096`) that *"The distance he is charged for is still the base-path distance, which is within a few centimetres of this route's length."*
  - Geometrically, that claim is false: first base is along $\hat{u}_{1B} = (1/\sqrt{2}, 1/\sqrt{2})$. An LHB at `x ≈ +0.7 m` starts $+0.7/\sqrt{2} = +0.50\text{ m}$ (`1.6 ft`) closer to first base (`26.94 m` Euclidean distance to first), whereas an RHB at `x ≈ -0.7 m` starts $-0.50\text{ m}$ (`1.6 ft`) farther (`27.93 m` to first) — a **`1.00 m` (`3.3 ft`, `~0.12 s`)** physical path difference, on top of the swing follow-through momentum carrying an LHB toward first and an RHB toward third. Charging both `27.432 m` from `s = 0` erases the LHB advantage (`4.26 s` LHB vs. `4.30 s` RHB in Statcast, or `~0.10–0.12 s` at equal Sprint Speed) and makes an RHB's 3D position cover `4.52 m` while `r.s` advances `4.0 m`.
- **Is one `tag_apply_s = 0.25 s` right for a catcher at the plate, an infielder tagging a runner sliding into his bag, and a fielder chasing/carrying? Does it delay force outs? (`P2-2`)**:
  - **Force outs**: It does **not** delay force outs at a bag (`Input::BaseTouched { holding_ball: true }` at `live.rs:2896–2908` has no `tag_apply_s` check, and `holder_options` at `holder.rs:188, 192` sets `tag = 0.0` when `force` is true). However, if a fielder tags a forced runner on the basepath via `Input::TagContact` (`live.rs:2887`), that tag still waits for `self.secured_at + 0.25 s`.
  - **Comparing the three tag contexts**:
    1. *Catcher receiving a throw at the plate*: `0.25 s` matches the MLB.com/Statcast benchmark for an **on-line** throw, but in `live.rs:2443–2445`, the catcher stays on the plate (`on_bag = true`) and gets the same `0.25 s` tag even when the throw misses the plate by up to `1.35 m` (`4.4 ft`), where a real catcher must lunge and sweep back (`0.4–0.7+ s`).
    2. *Infielder tagging a runner sliding into a bag*: When the throw arrives more than `0.25 s` before the runner (`secured_at + 0.25 <= t_runner_at_bag - 0.12`), the fielder already has the ball in his glove ready at the bag; in `live.rs:2887`, `TagContact` then fires as soon as the runner enters `tag_reach_m = 1.0 m` (`0.122 s` *before* reaching the bag!). Yet both `holder_options` (`holder.rs:192–193`) and `runner_decide` (`live.rs:2594–2595`) add `+ 0.25 s` to `ball_at` and compare against `runner_at` at `s = b * BASE_L` (`0.0 m`), overstating the time needed when the throw beats the runner by `> 0.13 s`.
    3. *Fielder carrying the ball to a bag or chasing a runner*: In the world (`live.rs:2887`), `tag_apply_s` is measured from **`self.secured_at`**, which has already elapsed while the fielder was running with the ball! Yet `holder_options` (`holder.rs:188–189`) adds `+ fd.a.tag_apply_s` (`+0.25 s`) **on top of** `run_time_to(fd, target, 0.6)`, penalizing every unforced carry option by `0.25 s` (`> 2\sigma` in `p_out`) for a receive-and-sweep motion the carrier does not perform.

### 3. The Remaining `225–300 ft` Sacrifice-Fly Gap (`P1-1`)

At `275–280 ft` (`83.8–85.3 m`), MLB runners on third tag up and go `~70–90%` of the time (`~40%` at `225–275 ft`), whereas in `sf_probe` (`300` games) they go `0%` at `225–250 ft`, `6.7%` at `250–275 ft`, and `26.5%` at `275–300 ft`. Examining each mechanism in the prompt shows that the gap comes from **two interacting causes**:

1. **The Decision Rule (`+0.35 s` flat margin = `99.0%` required safe probability vs. `agent-models §6.3`)**:
   - In `runner_decide` (`live.rs:2598, 2603`), the runner goes iff `t_ball - (self.t + t_run) + 0.15 * draw > 0.35`.
   - At `draw = 0`, requiring a `+0.35 s` margin with $\sigma = 0.15\text{ s}$ means the runner requires $\hat{P}(\text{safe}) = \Phi(0.35 / 0.15) = \Phi(+2.333) = \mathbf{99.0\%}$! Even when the runner beats the tag by `+0.20 s` (`90.9%` safe), he holds **84% of the time**; when the race is 50/50 (`margin = 0.00 s`), he goes **1.0% of the time**.
   - By contrast, `docs/agent-models.md` §6.3 (`lines 586–592`) specifies a **run-expectancy decision rule**:
     $$\hat{P}(\text{safe}) \cdot \Delta\text{RE}_{\text{safe}} + (1 - \hat{P}(\text{safe})) \cdot \Delta\text{RE}_{\text{out}} > \Delta\text{RE}_{\text{hold}}$$
     As documented in `docs/calibration/2026-11-14-tag-ups.md` §4 (`lines 159–179`), with 1 out before the catch (2 outs after the catch), scoring is worth $+0.737\text{ RE}$ while being thrown out costs $-0.365\text{ RE}$, giving a breakeven safe probability of **$p_{\text{BE}} = 33.1\%$** ($32.5\%$ in Olszewski 2015; $73.1\%$ with 0 outs before the catch).
   - Furthermore, real third-base coaches know that throwing out a runner from `250–280 ft` requires an **on-line throw**: if the throw is off-line, hops poorly, or is cut off, the runner is safe. Because the runner's `ball_time_to` computes the arrival time of a *perfect, on-line* throw (`t_ball_ideal`), a coach sending a runner whenever `t_ball_ideal - t_run >= 0.0 s` (even odds against a *perfect* throw) achieves an `80–90%` actual safe rate once off-line throws and cutoffs are accounted for.

2. **Why the Ball's Side in the Sim Is `0.30–0.40 s` Too Fast (and Too Accurate) at `250–280 ft`**:
   - If the decision margin were lowered from `+0.35 s` to `0.00–0.10 s` *without* changing the ball's side, runners at `250–280 ft` would be thrown out far too often in the sim (`t_ball = 3.92–4.05 s` vs. `t_run = 4.02 s` at `275–280 ft`). Four physical effects make the sim's outfield throws `0.30–0.40 s` too fast/deadly at `75–85 m`:
     - **Every throw uses `100%` of the Statcast top-10% (90th-percentile) max-effort velocity on the fly (`THROW_EFFORT = 1.0`, `live.rs:103`)**: `ARM_MEAN_MPH` (`87.4–90.7 mph` in the outfield) is the mean of each fielder's **top 10%** hardest throws, yet `live.rs` launches every set throw at `1.0 * arm` (`40.05–40.55 m/s`) on a pure fly (`solve_throw` up to `35°`, `2.82–2.89 s` at `275–280 ft`), with no one-hop bounce penalty (`+0.15–0.25 s` on a lower line-drive throw) and no difference between routine and crow-hop effort (`~0.94–0.96` of top-10% speed).
     - **Outfield catch-to-release has no crow-hop cost (`0.85 s` flat transfer, or hurried relay)**: When an outfielder catches a fly moving slower than `FACE_MIN_MPS = 3.0 m/s` (`live.rs:2204, 2211`), `turn_extra_s = 0.0` and `footwork_s = 0.0`, so he releases a 90 mph throw in `0.85 s` flat (vs. `1.05–1.20 s` for a real outfield crow-hop and long-arm windup). ### 4. Replayability (Invariant 3)

- **New RNG stream (`sim/crates/bb_game/src/live.rs:166, 2492`)**:
  - `ball_time_to` draws the holder's arm-read error from `KeyedRng::new(RngKey { game_seed: self.inp.seed, play_index: self.inp.play, agent_id: self.r[k].id, purpose: Purpose::RunnerPerception, sub_index: ARM_READ_SUB + *by as u32 })` where `ARM_READ_SUB = 1 << 20`.
  - Because `KeyedRng` is stateless and counter-based, using `sub_index = 2^20 + by` (`1,048,576..1,048,585`) is completely isolated from existing `RunnerPerception` sub-indices (`1` for the initial jump read at contact, `2n` for decision `n`'s timing noise, `2n + 1` for decision `n`'s free-ball read) and yields the exact same draw if `ball_time_to` is queried multiple times for the same `(runner, holder)` during a play.
  - `docs/data-formats.md` §2.3 (`line 66`) should be updated to list `sub_index = 2^20 + pos` under `RunnerPerception` (`P3-1`).
- **Float comparisons (`P3-1`)**:
  - `self.t - t0 >= HOLD_READ_S` (`0.2 s = 12 * DT`, `live.rs:2567`), `now < leave` (`live.rs:2720`), and `self.t >= self.secured_at + fd.a.tag_apply_s` (`0.25 s = 15 * DT`, `live.rs:2887`) are deterministic and bit-for-bit replayable from the seed, but because `self.t` is accumulated by adding `DT = 1.0 / 60.0` (a repeating binary fraction), `12 * DT` or `15 * DT` can fall `~1e-16` below `0.2` or `0.25` depending on `t0` / `secured_at`, delaying the trigger by one tick (`1/60 s`) unless `- 1e-9` is used.
