## Findings

### P2-1. A throw that goes by the double cut's first baseman leaves him standing, first open, for the whole throw

`sim/crates/bb_game/src/live.rs:5968` (`update_relay`): a throw to anyone else ends a cutoff man's job with `go_idle`, which stops him where he is (`stop_point`), and nothing gives him a job again until the throw is caught: `hear` re-works covers only while the ball is loose or held (`live.rs:4741`), and `deep_jobs` returns while a throw is in the air. On main this happened to the single's cutoff man, but then the second baseman had first (`home_order`). On the double cut nobody has first (as ruled), so for the length of the outfielder's throw the first baseman stands in the infield and first is empty.

*How checked.* On the worker's game traces (`sim/target/codex-probes/dcut/tr2-base.log`, `tr2-v2.log`, seeds 0–119), counting from the first row after the first baseman was out on the cutoff home at which he has no job and nobody has first, to the next row at which either changes (my script `idle2.py`, in the scratchpad; its rows are the trace's job changes, so a spell still open at the end of the play is not counted):

| | Main `ba40063` | `816a15e` |
|---|---|---|
| Spells, all during a throw | 0 | 241 (616.6 s) |
| of them with the batter-runner held at first | 0 | 91 (209.8 s) |
| longest | — | 3.1 s (46/114); with the batter at first 2.95 s (64/196) |

Seed 17 play 103 (the note's §3): the centre fielder throws to second at 6.05 s; the first baseman stands with no job until the second baseman has the ball at 8.13 s, then runs back, and the second baseman's throw to first behind the batter-runner, who had rounded the bag, waits for him: 9.41 s against 8.60 s on main (out on both). Seed 64 play 196: a ball first down 96 m out, fielded 120 m out, the batter-runner at first; the throw goes to second at 9.48 s and the first baseman has no job until 12.43 s.

A real first baseman whose cutoff is passed goes back to his bag at once when the batter-runner is at or near first (or trails the runner toward second). The note's §6 already lists "after a throw goes by the first baseman he stops where he is"; this measures it.

*Suggested fix.* When a throw goes by a cutoff man (the release is public), he takes his own job at once (`take_own_job` after `go_idle` in the `Ball::Thrown` arm, at least for the first baseman), so that the table puts him back on first. Measure it on the trace (this table) and the standard run; it may move single-cutoff plays too.

### P3-1. The route takes any man with any cutoff job, now the nearest of them; the spec says the man lined up for that base

`sim/crates/bb_game/src/live/holder.rs:45-54` (`throw_race`). `lined` is every man known to have a `Relay(_)` job (any base) more than 10 m away; the route goes to the one nearest the holder of those closer to the base than he is. agent-models §5.5 ("The route") defines a man lined up in a throw as one with the relay job *for that base*, between the holder and the base and within `CUT_LINE_M` = 5 m of its line, and the new sentence builds on that. `man_in_throw` (`live.rs:8325`, the cut height) uses that definition; the route does not. Before, the route also ignored the base, but it took the lowest-numbered man, so the change does widen it: a shortstop out on the cutoff to third who is nearer the holder now wins over the first baseman lined up home, and a first baseman still running to the line (97/119: 16 m off it at the throw) is taken as the relay man's man from where he is.

*How checked.* In the traces two men out on cutoffs for different bases at once before the first throw: 11 plays (bases 2 and 3) on both builds, none at the last row before the throw. Throws through any cutoff or relay man: none in 120 games on either build (the note's §3, and the `C` lines). A probe with the old rule (`ZZ_OLDROUTE` in my copy) fails only the constructed unit test; the fixtures and the seeds 0–7 test pass. So no game is affected today.

*Suggested fix.* Filter `lined` by `Role::Relay(base)` and the `CUT_LINE_M` line test, sharing `man_in_throw`'s predicate, so the route and the cut height agree on who is in the throw.

### P3-2. The relay man's throw through the first baseman is checked only at the route, not at the choice

`sim/crates/bb_game/src/live.rs:11384-11395` (`the_double_cut_lines_up_the_first_baseman_behind_the_relay_man`). The test's last step asserts `throw_race(relay, 4).relay == Some(2)`: the candidate route. Nothing asserts what the relay man's direct throw home is cut at (it is `through_spot`, 14 m: `man_in_throw` calls `deep_ball` on the relay man's position, false for an infielder 55 m out, so the cut is at the first baseman's spot, as the spec says; I read this, I did not run it), nor that `holder_act` ever takes the throw through him, nor what the first baseman does with it. In games it never happens (no relay throws in 120 games). The test is sound for what it checks (the alignment against the ruling, the spot against 45 ft from the owner's write-up, the route), and fails on `ba40063` and with the old route rule, as claimed.

*Suggested fix.* Add to the constructed test `cut_seen(Some(relay), pos, 4) == Cut::Through` and a case where the relay man's throw through wins (a weak-armed relay man far out), checking `holder_act` releases to the first baseman and the first baseman then throws home.

### P3-3. A relay throw that lands short of the relay man ends the first baseman's job

`sim/crates/bb_game/src/live.rs:5171-5199` (`double_cut_job`). A throw that reaches the ground becomes a new loose ball (`update_thrown`, `set_free`: new `ball_gen`, `ball_since`). While it is loose, `deep_cut_point` returns `None` once the relay man is the known lead (an infielder); once he holds it, the `Held` arm needs his `Relay(4)` shown at or after `ball_since`, which it was not. Either way `double_cut_job` is `None`, and `update_relay` sends the first baseman to first just as the relay man picks up a short hop, whereas a real one stays in line for the relay. Read from the code; not seen in games (no relay throws).

*Suggested fix.* Keep the first baseman's job while the relay man is going for or holds a ball the outfielder threw to him (the `relay: true` throw is public), e.g. key the `Held` arm on the throw having been to him rather than on `ball_since`.

### P3-4. Text the change made inexact

- `docs/agent-models.md:655` (§5.7, "And back"): "a first baseman who was out on the cutoff this play and has been replaced (a deep ball's relay man has taken over) ... takes first back". With the double cut a deep ball with a play at home no longer replaces him; the case left is a deep ball whose relay goes to third. Say so.
- `sim/crates/bb_game/src/live.rs:5091-5093` (`relay_base` doc) and agent-models §5.7 *When*: "the same on a deep ball with the first baseman known out on the double cut". `held_home` (`live.rs:5101`) holds for either corner man known out on the cutoff home, now on a deep ball too, so a third baseman still known out on a single's cutoff home when the ball turns deep also holds it (for up to `CALL_HEARD_S`, until he is known back). Probably harmless (the double cut then follows); say "a corner man known out on the cutoff home", or restrict the code to the first baseman on a deep ball.
- agent-models §5.7 *What each man knows*: "re-works his own cover or backup job when the convention ... changes (`deep_jobs`)". `deep_jobs` re-works the cover only; a backup is re-worked only through `take_own_job` when he loses a cover. Say "cover job".

## The questions

1. **Invariant 2.** Every input to `deep_cut_point` / `deep_cut_men` / `deep_cut_as_known_by` / `deep_jobs` / `latch_deep` is public: the ball's state (held by whom, thrown, loose) and position, the batted ball's first touch (`first_down_pt`, a fact), the known lead (`lead`, from shown chases 0.3 s late), the holder's or lead's position (`fielding_point`), the pre-pitch spots (`deep_relay_man`), the runners' positions and speeds (`relay_base`), the jobs shown 0.3 s late (`seen` = `known_job` for anyone but the viewer himself), and per viewer what he knows of who is chasing (`chasing_as_known_by`). `double_cut_job` is the first baseman's own job; its `Held` arm reads the relay man's *shown* job at the catch and its `Thrown` arm the throw's public receiver and the relay man's position. "At once" is defensible for each: the convention names the men from what all see (a ball down past the outfielders), so no one needs to hear a call to know it, as on a ball through the infield (`home_cutoff_by_convention`); the relay man's own gates cannot keep him in on a ball down past the outfielders (his intercept is beyond `INFIELD_BREAK_R`; he only stays if he chases, which the convention excludes per viewer). One latent point, not a finding: `relay_base(true, fielder)` uses the *outfielder's* `throw_choice` thresholds (`moving_mps`, `visible_target`) for everyone's table; all players share the default today (`play.rs:106`), and `relay_job` already did this. If those become per-player, the convention should use the viewer's. The harness check: `isolation_no_private_state_reaches_a_teammate_in_real_games` (seeds 0–1, in the workspace run) and `isolation_many_seeds` with `ISO_SEEDS=2..10` (see Verification).
2. **The ruling and the spec.** First baseman in any field (`double_cut_job` does not look at the field; the unit test covers centre, left-centre, the right-field line); third baseman on third (`corner_back` in `cover_after_chasers`, the convention in `cover_table`; the seeds 0–7 test); other middle infielder on second; not on a catch (`first_down_pt` is `None` once a glove secured the ball first); not with nobody on (`relay_base` gives 3 or 2). The first baseman's spot is `through_spot` = min(`CUTOFF_M`, half) short of the plate on the line from the relay man's spot, as §5.7 says; `CUTOFF_M` = 14 m is the existing named parameter (45 ft = 13.7 m). No new literal beyond the mask flag `1 << 15`.
3. **The route.** P3-1 and P3-2. It cannot pick a man behind the holder (he must be closer to the base), it can pick one off the line or lined up for another base. The relay man's choice with the first baseman behind him is tested only by the constructed test.
4. **`latch_deep`.** Set when a ball first down on the dirt is judged deep while loose with an outfielder the known lead, or in an outfielder's hands; read only while the ball is loose or in an outfielder's hands; cleared by any new loose ball (`ball_gen` moves on a bobble, a short hop, a throw that lands), and gone with the play (`Sim` is per play). It cannot keep a ball deep that is then cut off shallow: it is set only once `deep_ball` has seen the fielding point beyond `RELAY_DEEP_M` and the ball past the outfielder; a ground ball rolling out only gets deeper, and a carom off the wall stays in the same loose ball. Without it, 118/162 fails (my mutation run).
5. **First left open.** As ruled, the alignment leaves first; the cost is in P2-1 (the first baseman standing through the throw), not in the alignment. Two figures for the owner's question (note §6): of the 279 double cuts in 120 games, **97 (35%) are on balls the batter-runner ends at first**, so the note's reason for leaving first ("the batter-runner is past it or going for second") does not hold on a third of them. Many look like a baserunning gap that is not this change's: seed 64 play 196, one out, a ball first down 96 m out and fielded 120 m out, and the batter stops at first on both builds. Worth a line in TASKS for the batter-runner, not a finding here.
6. **Tests.** Independent of the implementation: the ruling's alignment, 45 ft from the owner's write-up, the line geometry, "first baseman out before the call never leaves", "third and second covered at every tick". Confirmed by running (Verification): on `ba40063` each fixture play fails separately, the seeds 0–7 test fails (premise: no double cuts), the unit test fails; on the head with the convention switched off all three fixtures and the seeds 0–7 test fail (seed 2 play 77, as the note says); without the latch only 118/162 fails; with the old route only the unit test fails. The edited pickup test (`the_relay_man_keeps_the_job_through_the_outfielders_pickup`) is loosened only for a first baseman already out at the pickup; it passes on both builds.
7. **Measurements.** The note's §5 table matches `std-{main,head,final}-{0,1000}.txt` line for line; the final binary (`563bfa9`) gives the same figures as `f7baee3`; `git diff 816a15e HEAD -- sim` is empty.
