## Findings

### P2-1. The contact read can send a forced runner on an ungrounded liner

At `sim/crates/bb_game/src/live.rs:5627`, the first 0–1-out decision uses exact position and vertical velocity from `ball_at` to decide whether the runner gets a noisy catch read. A descending ball below the literal 1.5 m threshold bypasses that read. A rebound can also clear the test even after a recorded ground touch. The gate predates the moving start. It now decides whether a runner with forward momentum should check or accelerate, and it ignores the available ground-touch cue.

In seed 5, play 183, runner 259 was on first with one out. At 0.500 s the ball was still on its first flight, 1.186 m high and descending at 2.736 m/s. The trace switches from `Read` to `Run` toward second at 3.00 m/s, as the force in `runner_decide` requires once this branch is entered. The first baseman catches the liner at 0.633 s. By then the runner is moving at 3.85 m/s and needs until 1.450 s to stop. This proves the misread and extra momentum. The trace alone cannot establish that an earlier check would have saved him. I also reproduced the opposite classification in seed 0, play 144, runner 3. The ball bounced at 0.027 s and was rising at the 0.500 s read. Its post-bounce state passes the air gate, but the movement step still released the runner at the scheduled read.

### P3-1. The third-base blend changes lateral velocity discontinuously

At `sim/crates/bb_game/src/live.rs:270`, the linear offset changes slope where the third-base route enters and leaves each blend. The runner’s scalar speed follows the acceleration and braking model, but the position used for tags and playback changes direction without a turn. Seed 0, play 33, runner 260 crossed 10 m past third between 0.717 and 0.733 s at about 6.5 m/s. Applying the route formula to those trace samples gives a jump from zero lateral speed to about 1.95 m/s at the boundary. The scalar speed also understates the speed along each diagonal blend by about 4.4%.

The lead itself is on the correct side of the line. The offset is perpendicular to the leg home and vanishes at the bag, so the rules engine’s projection recovers the longitudinal progress there and along the lead. The simulated tag uses the offset position. Leaving the foul line is legal here. Under OBR 5.09(b)(1), the tag-avoidance path is established when a tag is attempted. [Rules projection](../../sim/crates/bb_rules/src/engine.rs#L73) · [Official Baseball Rules](https://content.mlb.com/documents/2/2/4/305750224/2019_Official_Baseball_Rules_FINAL_.pdf)

### P3-2. The first decision uses one tick less coasting than the stated read time

At `sim/crates/bb_game/src/live.rs:5627` and `sim/crates/bb_game/src/live.rs:5710`, the contact tick records without moving, and subsequent decisions run before the movement step. The 0.500 s look therefore reads 29 elapsed coast steps, with 1.45 m gained at 3 m/s. The 0.250 s break reads 14 steps, with 1.167 m gained at 5 m/s. The actual start clock in both traces is the stated time. The displacement used for the first decision is one step behind that time. I verified both cases in the runner traces for `5 183 259` and `0 33 260`. The moving-start unit test also asserts this tick ordering at `sim/crates/bb_game/src/live.rs:10418`.

The cases cover clean catches and give no evidence about how a juggle would affect the first-touch clock. ## Extra-base rates

Center field already had the highest rate on the matched seed-0 baseline. Before moving starts, two-out sends from second were 17/47 to left, 41/56 to center and 23/55 to right. In the final two seed sets they were 72/88 (81.8%), 109/110 (99.1%) and 73/96 (76.0%). Center exceeds the 75–91% band, and all 109 center-field sends scored. The median runner split to third was 2.75 s in each direction. The ball was first secured at a median 3.38 s in center, 3.18 s in left and 3.06 s in right. The benchmark arrival home was 7.23 s from center, 6.60 s from left and 6.38 s from right. The benchmark used its midpoint relay in 70/110 center-field chances, versus 24/88 in left and 8/96 in right. The probe recorded no qualifying actual delivery to the plate in the 110 center-field chances.

The model places center field 320 ft from home and the corners 290 ft away. At possession the runner reads a 89.6 mph grade-50 center-field arm, between left at 87.4 and right at 90.7. The free-ball read uses a generic arm and transfer. Both estimates use distance and can take a hypothetical midpoint relay. The defense instead evaluates a direct throw or a relay through the available cutoff man. This points to pickup depth and throw geometry as explanations for the directional gap that was already present before Q27. The faster start then widened center’s advantage over the throw. The benchmark does not show that a cutoff assignment failed on those plays. The final first-to-third result was 823/2,279 (36.1%) on all singles and 777/1,916 (40.6%) on outfield singles. On matched seeds the outfield figure rose from 269/947 to 381/950. The largest increase in safe advances was with two outs on balls to center and right. The direction split is consistent with the shorter race to the intervening bag and favorable throw geometry.
