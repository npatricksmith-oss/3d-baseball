# Review: swing v3 spec and the contact baseline (2026-11-24, Sonnet)

Range `aa4a31d..2364d55` on `work/swing-rebuild`: `docs/swing-model-v3.md`, `docs/calibration/2026-11-24-swing-contact-baseline.md`, `bb_game/examples/swing_contact_baseline.rs`, and the pointers in agent-models §3.6, physics-spec §6.3 and rules-spec §4.5. I wrote none of it. No source, test, spec, TASKS or observations file was touched. Probes are in `sim/target/review-probes/` (`v3probe.py`, `launch.py`, stdlib Python; they import the v2 oracle `analysis/oracle/swing_oracle.py` for the contact jet).

**Overall.** The anchors are almost all quoted correctly, and nothing from OpenBiomechanics ships. The baseline's arithmetic and its reading of the collision hold up. The implementability is the weak part: one construction the spec calls a plain one-dimensional root find has no root for about one swing in ten, and the claim that v3 equals v2 in the contact window up to a small third-order term is wrong by a factor of 5–7 at the window's edge.

## Findings

### P1

**P1-1. `W_93` has no solution for swings whose contact azimuth is about 25° or more (spec §6.1 last paragraph, §6.3, §9).**
The spec solves, per swing, the hands' point at −93 ms: on the cone 0.146·H from the rear elbow at 126° of flexion, with the clavicle-to-hands vector making λ = 131° with a bat at azimuth A_c − 168°, elevation 39°. I built it with the spec's own numbers (trunk +2°, rear shoulder 49° / −42°, segment fractions of §5.2, the provisional trunk frame of §4.2). Results for A_c = −15 … +24°: two roots each. For **A_c = 27° and above: no root, for any shoulder-point position** (S only translates the shoulder, it does not change the lag geometry). The baseline gives contact azimuth mean +7.7°, sd 13.4°, p95 +29.6°, so roughly **10% of full swings (inside and late ones)** have no `W_93`. §9 says the root find is "a fixed number of bisection steps on a fixed bracket"; with no root it returns an arbitrary point silently, and the lag anchor (131° at −93 ms) is then not met.
*Caveat:* my probe leaves out the trunk's 13° forward flexion and its lateral lean, which move the threshold by a few degrees; they do not make a root certain.
*Fix:* say what happens when there is no root (the nearest reachable lag, flagged and counted; or drop the lag condition at −93 ms and pass only through the arm angles, since λ is meant to be an outcome); or move λ_93 to a free output and fix the hands by the arm angles alone. Settle it in the oracle (step 1) over the whole range of aims before any Rust, and add the over-range cases to the goldens.

### P2

**P2-1. v3 and v2 differ by far more than "third-order" in the last 25 ms, and v3's bat is not "still speeding up" in azimuth at contact (§6.2, §6.5, §2, §12, §14 first choice).**
§6.5 estimates the difference inside the contact window as about 0.1° at −10 ms and 2° at −25 ms. I built the §6.3 polynomials (A: degree 7, E: degree 6) from the stated conditions on the oracle's v2 jet and compared them with v2's own path. The v3 − v2 bat azimuth is:

| aim | at −10 ms | at −25 ms | elevation at −25 ms |
| :-- | --: | --: | --: |
| middle | −0.67° | −7.6° | +2.7° |
| inside-low | −0.40° | −5.8° | +1.9° |
| outside-high | −0.87° | −9.2° | +3.3° |

At the sweet spot (0.7 m) 7.6° is about 9 cm. The cause is simple: the sweep of 168° over −82 ms to contact, with the rate pinned at 0.558·Ȧ_c at −82 ms, cannot be made with v2's rate profile, so the polynomial's azimuth rate goes **above** the contact rate before contact: middle aim, v3 2,522°/s at −25 ms against v2's 1,802°/s (+40%), and 2,477 at −10 ms against 2,289, falling to 2,349°/s at contact (v2's A-acceleration at contact, −8,400°/s², is matched, which makes the rate peak just before contact). The spec's §6.2 sentence "the bat still speeding up at contact (v2's τ_b = +10 ms)" and §14's "Now: still speeding up when it meets the ball" are therefore true of v2 and not of v3's azimuth rate. The lab's peak at −10 to −15 ms is, in effect, partly built in, whichever option the owner takes.
*What goes wrong:* contacts before −10 ms (1,003 in the baseline, 2.3% of contact; the window opens at −25 ms) meet a bat that is turned back several degrees and moving a good deal faster than today's; their sweet-spot speed, attack angle, exit speed and foul share all shift, and which pitches are hit or missed at the window's edge shifts too. The claims "match by construction … the timing table and the slopes" (§2, baseline §5) and §12's "within noise" for the far timing rows are unsupported before about −5 ms. The check list in §11 does include the v2 difference at −10 and −25 ms, which is right, but the numbers the spec states as expected are wrong.
*Fix:* restate §6.5 with measured numbers; add the jerk (or the rate peak) to the contact conditions so v3 and v2 agree to third order, or blend v3 into v2's own path over the last 30–40 ms (the shape the diagnosis's option (a) used); say in §14 that option (a) is not what the polynomial gives unless one of these is done.

**P2-2. The launch pose and the shoulder point are not defined well enough for an independent oracle (§5.3, §6.1, §11 case 1, §5.2).**
Missing or unstated: the height of the mid-hips (the clavicle is "0.28·H above" them; the spec's own S at contact, z = 1.38 m, implies one but never states it); the axes of the 8° and 13° forward flexion and of the 27° + 4° lateral lean (which side, which sign, about which axis); the order of the rotations; the S(τ) curve ("the same kind of curve as the hands, with zero velocity at none of them [E: velocity from the neighbouring anchors]" is a description, not a formula: three positions at three instants, and the instants of contact vs. −93 ms vs. launch need the same s_T scaling); what the lead shoulder's anchors are used for (its angles are given for −170 and −93 ms and never used except in a ±0.19 m check). Golden case 1 lists "S_L, both shoulders, the rear elbow, W_L, K_L, â_L" as outputs; two implementers will not agree on them.
*Fix:* write the S construction as explicit formulas in §5.3 before step 1, with every [E] value named.

**P2-3. §4.2 and §6.1 use the shoulder angles in opposite directions (the provisional reading decides where the hands start).**
§4.2 defines elevation and azimuth on "the vector from the elbow to the shoulder joint centre". §6.1 step 2 places the elbow "0.186·H from the rear shoulder at elevation 71°, azimuth −78°", i.e. applies the angles to the shoulder-to-elbow vector. Reversing a vector turns the azimuth by 180°. I built L both ways (stated numbers, H = 1.85 m). Provisional reading: hands about 0.43 m from the clavicle, 0.14 m toward the plate and 0.23 m toward the catcher of the rear shoulder. Literal reading (azimuth + 180°): hands 0.29 m from the clavicle (or 0.15 m, the other root), on the lead side of the rear shoulder. Both give λ = 121° and both pass the lead-arm check at the primary root, so **§6.1 step 4 and §15's "the lead-arm check also catches a wrong choice" do not hold**: the tolerance (±0.10·H = 0.185 m, 37% of the arm's reach) is too loose to tell the readings apart (distances 0.62 m and 0.42 m against a reach of 0.50 m). Step 0 (Appendix B) is the only thing that settles it, and the spec says so; the fix here is to make §4.2 and §6.1 agree on the direction and to give a check that discriminates (for instance the hands' height against the shoulder's, from the launch pose's figure).

**P2-4. Goldens 1 and 3 test the conditions, not the physics (§11, AGENTS.md "no tautological tests").**
Case 1's λ_L = 121°, A_L, E_L "to 1e-9" and case 3's A, Ȧ, E, λ at τ_93 "to 1e-9" check that the solver reproduces the numbers it was given. They cannot catch the reading, sign or frame errors that matter most (P2-3, the sign of azimuth, the s_T scaling). The checks that are independent (lag maximum 133° at −80 ms, uncocking rate, arm-vector rate, bat speed components, lag at contact by location) are listed as reports; only some are bounds. *Fix:* give the first three of these a bound from [4] and [3] in the tests, and add one hand-computed independent case (a fixed S and bat, the lag and the hands' height worked on paper) to the fixtures.

### P3

- **P3-1. Pelvis at contact "+17 cm" is not in [4] (§5 row "Pelvis forward of the stance").** [4] §4.1.5 states a 5 cm rise and no forward travel; +17 is +12 + 5 + 0 with the 5 cm of §4.1.4 added at −93 ms. The last 93 ms is therefore an assumption (no forward travel), not a measurement; tag it [E].
- **P3-2. Launch trunk turn: [4] says −22°, the spec uses −21° [E] (§5, §6.1).** −22° is the stated value (upper trunk at the start of §4.1.4, and the max counter-rotation at −186 ms); −21° is the pelvis's maximum counter-rotation. Use −22° or say why not.
- **P3-3. "Bat azimuth at contact −8 ± 12°; the jet gives +7.7°: inside both" (§5 table, last-but-one row) is false for azimuth.** +7.7° is 1.3 sd above [4]'s mean; elevation (−29.5° against −30 ± 5°) is inside. Report it as within two sd.
- **P3-4. The checked-swing stop angle differs between documents.** Spec §8.3: about −140° (my arithmetic for a middle aim gives −137° at τ_93 + 20 ms path); baseline §5: "about −150° … instead of −87°". −150° is not supported.
- **P3-5. "The cone and the lag condition give at most two points" (§6.1 step 3).** A circle meeting a cone is a quartic; zero, two or four roots are possible. My runs gave 2 or 0 (P1-1). State the selection rule for four roots and the failure rule for none.
- **P3-6. Elevation is not "held at 39° until −93 ms" (§6.3, §1) and it overshoots [4].** The degree-6 curve starting at 41° and ending at 39° at τ_93 goes up to 45–46° in between (all three aims), above [4]'s own 44° maximum at the end of the stride. It stays inside the stated [−40°, 50°] bound, so the §6.3 verification list passes; the sentence should say "passes through 39°", and an interior condition (Ė = 0 at the start and at τ_93) could keep it down.
- **P3-7. Scale reference for [3]'s rates is undefined (§5, §11).** "Within ±25% of [3]'s 1,588°/s scaled to the contact rate": [3] gives no bat azimuth rate at contact, so the ratio (contact rate of what, from which source) must be stated; today the natural choice is [4]'s 2,233°/s, which makes the bound 1,588 × (v3's Ȧ_c / 2,233).
- **P3-8. Ruling wording (§8.4, rules-spec §4.5).** "Perpendicular to the pitcher–home line … barrel head level with the front of the batter" is a bat direction, and it is today's convention [C]; OBR practice (umpire's judgment, in effect the barrel's crossing the front of the plate or the wrists breaking) is not exactly this, and with a wrapped launch the hands are further back than before, so the same angle is a longer turn of the barrel. The spec is right to keep it a configurable [C]; §14's third choice should list the threshold as an option next to the brake timing (θ_swing < 0°, or a plane test on the barrel head).
- **P3-9. Export for v2 while v2 is the default (§13 step 3).** Step 3 exports L and S "no effect on games" before step 4 switches to v3; v2 has no L or S. State what the export carries until step 4.

**2. Implementable and self-consistent.** Not as written: P1-1, P2-1, P2-2, P2-3. **3. The claim "v3 matches this by construction" (§2, §12, baseline §5) is supported at the planned instant and not beyond a few milliseconds (P2-1).
