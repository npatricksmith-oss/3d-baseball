## 1. Summary

Today's swing (v2) is one rigid plane laid backward from the contact pose. It meets the ball the way MLB hitters do (Statcast) but before about 40 ms ahead of contact it is a different swing: the bat flat and pointing at the catcher where a measured hitter's is 40° up and wrapped behind his head, in line with the arms where his is cocked 120–130° behind them (diagnosis §3).

v3 keeps what v2 gets right and rebuilds the rest:

- **What moves.** The bat (the only physical body), held at the **hands** (mid-wrists, a point on the handle), whose path is built round a **shoulder point S** (the clavicle, the root of [4]'s arm vector) that follows the body. Three elements; the pelvis, trunk turn, legs and head stay presentation keyed to the sim's clocks (§3).
- **Where it starts.** A **launch pose L** per batter, the same on every pitch, at rest. For the average batter it is [4]'s at weight commitment (−170 ms): the bat 41° up and wrapped (azimuth −225°), cocked well behind the arms ([4]: 121°; 129° as built), the hands just in front of the back shoulder at its height (§6.1). A batter with a longer swing (faster bat, slower hands) starts more wrapped and more upright, a shorter one less (§6.7).
- **How it meets the ball.** The **contact jet**, the pose, velocity and acceleration at t_c, is v2's plan exactly: the sweet spot at the aim point at `BatSpeed`, with Statcast's attack angle, attack direction, path tilt and vertical bat angle, and v2's reach rule (§6.2). After t_c the bat is v2's (§6.4). Before t_c the measured shape brings the bat into the contact window differently from today (§6.5).
- **Between.** From L at rest to [4]'s mid-swing anchor (−93 ms: for the average batter 174.0° short of contact and turning at 0.565 of its horizontal contact rate, [4]'s rate shape at the jet's contact rate; elevation 39°; the hands at a per-batter point built from [4]'s arm angles), then [4]'s own measured azimuth rate and elevation (Appendix E, scaled to the jet's contact rate and elevation) into the contact jet; the hands on one smooth curve throughout (§6.3). The lag through the swing is an outcome checked against [4] (121° → 133° max at −80 ms → 65° at contact; at the measured stance and contact 129° → 136° at −89 ms → 63°).
- **Every batter, every effort (D27, §6.7).** The average batter's measured swing, built in its own phase, is the template; a batter's swing is the template scaled: its travel by λ = (`BatSpeed` × his swing time) over the average's (MLB's swing length rises with bat speed in proportion), its time by his `QuickHands` and the swing's effort (an easier swing is the same swing, slower, as a driven chain's is). In the zone every rating and effort at ±3 sd gives a sweet spot that rises into the ball with one surge (at most 1.10× its contact speed, 1.09× on the middle pitch; the zone's low-inside corner reaches 1.11×, an expected failure) and hands and a knob that speed up once (§6.7.4).
- **Checked swings** brake by slowing the swing's own clock, so the bat stays on its path; the ruling measures the bat's turn from L (rules-spec §4.5's wording), so a wrapped bat is no longer "a swing" before it moves (§8).
- **Determinism and noise** are as v2: the same five execution draws in the same order on the same two streams; no new RNG purpose (§9).
- **What changes in games**: every swing takes its own time (0.170 s from rest for the average batter on a middle pitch, which is today's 0.150 s from the bat's start, so he decides when he does today, §4.3; outside and low pitches take longer, inside and high ones less, an easy swing longer). Balls met before the planned instant (late swings; 23% of contact is 3 ms or more before it): 25 ms before t_c the bat is 3.5° further back, 8° higher, its sweet spot 11–14 mph faster than v2's on the same plan and travelling 7–8° more steeply downward; 10 ms before, under 0.5° back, under 1° higher, 3.9–4.2 mph faster and 2–5° more steeply downward; 3 ms before, within 0.3° and 0.5 mph; at and after t_c it is today's bat (§6.7.6). Checked swings change a lot (the braked bat is far back: about 0.6 fewer strikes a game). The batter's move (D28) and the deeper contact (D29), their own step, change what he sees of the pitch (every ball is met 3.6 ms sooner), where a pitch hits him and his run to first (§12). The recalibration should be small to moderate (§12).
- **Where the batter stands** (PLAN D28, §5.3): where MLB's batters stand, his hips 0.71 m behind the plate's front edge (Savant's batting-stance leaderboard), 0.58 m deeper than today's spot, 0.918 m off the plate's middle line, read as his position at the end of the stance, from which [4]'s stride carries him 0.24 m forward by contact. He meets the ball where MLB's batters meet it (PLAN D29): the middle pitch 0.49 m from the plate's rear point [E], 0.14 m further out than today. There the hands end 0.49 m in front of the chest ([4]'s arms about 0.4 m; today's spot 0.08 m behind it), the bat-to-arm angle follows [4]'s curve from 135° at −94 ms to 63° at contact ([4]: 133° at −80 ms, 64° at contact), and the hands rise once where today's stall.
- **What the stance costs** (§6.7.6): with the hands no longer stalled, a ball met late meets a bat as fast as an on-time one or faster (1.2 mph at 9 in deep, 3.5 mph at 16 in, by Savant's depth bins), where MLB's is 2.3 and 4.7 mph slower: an owner's choice is written in §6.7.6, and step 4 measures it. The arms reach v2's contact hands only because the body at contact follows the pitch (§5.3: bent forward for a low pitch, reaching out for an outside one), an [E] no source gives.

## 2. What v3 must keep: today's contact (the baseline)

The collision reads one thing from the swing: the bat's pose and velocity field at the contact instant, inside t_c ± 25 ms (physics-spec §7). Nothing else that decides a game reads the bat before t_c − 25 ms except a checked swing (diagnosis §2: the brake, the braked path, the ruling). The fielders read the bat's horizontal angle at t_c and the swing's lead t_plate − t_s (`game.rs:409-415`).

Today, over the standard run's 600 games (77,434 swings; [baseline](calibration/2026-11-24-swing-contact-baseline.md)):

| At contact (full swings, n 43,590) | mean | sd |
| :-- | --: | --: |
| contact − t_c | +1.2 ms | 5.6 |
| sweet-spot speed | 71.2 mph | 4.3 |
| attack angle / direction | 9.1° / +2.2° | 5.9 / 9.2 |
| bat elevation / azimuth ([4]'s convention) | −29.5° / +7.7° | 3.5 / 13.4 |
| bat against its path (hooked +) | +0.27° | 1.52 |
| knob speed / bat angular speed | 6.4 m/s / 2,046°/s | 1.0 / 142 |
| EV, LA, spray (pull +) of all contact | 84.1 mph, 16.4°, −14.2° | 15.9, 30.6, 47.2 |

Slopes: spray +3.2° and bat-against-path +0.27° per ms of contact − t_c; launch angle +10.1° per cm of undercut; EV 1.01 mph per mph of sweet-spot speed. Checked swings: 13.2% of swings, the brake at −82 ± 32 ms, 3.4% ruled swings (310 touches, 36 on the yaw).

**By construction v3 reproduces** every quantity at the planned contact instant (the jet is v2's), and after it the bat is v2's at the swing's own time (§6.4): within 0.12 mph of today's at +5 ms and 0.45 mph at +10 ms over the family (§6.7.3; 57% of contacts are after t_c). **It moves** contacts before t_c, which meet a bat further back, higher, faster and moving more steeply downward than today's, by an amount that grows quickly with the timing error (§6.7.6: at −10 ms under 0.5° back, under 1° higher, 3.9–4.2 mph faster and 1.5–5.9° more steeply downward over the family; at −25 ms 3.5° back, 8° higher and 11–14 mph faster), and checked swings a lot (§12). It is not "v2 to second order" over the window: the first version of this spec said so, and the review measured otherwise.

## 3. The state: what moves, and why these three

| Element | What it is | Degrees of freedom | Source of its motion |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Bat** | rigid body of revolution (physics-spec §6), the only thing the world reads | 5: direction â (azimuth A, elevation E) and the hands' point W on its axis | [4] angles; the contact jet |
| **Hands** W | the mid-wrists, taken as the point of the bat's axis at u_g = 0.08 m from the knob [E] (between the two hands; the real mid-wrists sit 5–8 cm off the axis, which moves the lag by a few degrees) | (part of the bat's 5) | [4] arm angles at two instants; the contact jet |
| **Shoulder point** S | [4]'s "clavicle", the root of the arm vector a = W − S | 3, prescribed (no dynamics, no decision) | [4] pelvis travel and trunk angles; the stance spot |

Lag λ = ∠(a, â), [4]'s `BatLag()`: 0° = the bat in line with the arms, 180° = pointing back along them ([3] reports the supplement). The arm vector's turn is [3]'s "arms" segment.

**Why not fewer.** Without S there is no arm vector, so no lag: the measured relation of bat to hands, which D25 names, could not be stated or checked. Without a hands path of its own (hands fixed on a circle about one centre, as v2), the hands cannot go from beside the back shoulder at launch to 0.4 m in front of the chest at contact while the bat stays cocked. One plane for the bat (v2) cannot hold the bat 39–44° up while it turns 50° in azimuth ([4] −170 to −93 ms) and then bring it to −30°: no single plane contains that path (diagnosis §1).

**Why not more.** Pelvis, trunk turn, legs and head do not touch the bat once S's path and the hands' path are given, and nothing in the sim reads them. They stay presentation, keyed on the sim's clocks as [player-visuals §13.2](player-visuals.md#132-the-timing-model) designs (the hips and chest peak before the sim's arm and bat). The proximal half of the measured sequence (pelvis 581°/s at −70 ms, trunk 766°/s at −57 ms [4]) is therefore the client's, checked there; the distal half (arm, then bat) is the sim's, checked here (§11). Modelling them in the sim would add state that no outcome reads. If a later model drives the bat from the body (a torque-driven chain), it can replace this one behind the same interface (`BatPose`).

**Elbows** are not sim state. The distance |a| follows from the hands' path; the drawn elbows' angles follow from |a| and the figure's arm lengths and are a check (rear 81°, lead 56° at contact [4]).

## 4. Frames, definitions and time

### 4.1 Frames

The batter's frame, for a right-handed batter (a left-hander is mirrored in x, as now): x̂ toward the plate from the batter, ŷ toward the pitcher, ẑ up. It is the sim frame for a right-hander (AGENTS.md "Units and frames").

### 4.2 Angles ([4]'s definitions, from its Appendix C code)

- **Bat azimuth A and elevation E** (`BatLag()`: `BatPhi − 270`, `90 − BatTheta`): â = (cos E cos A, cos E sin A, sin E). A = 0° the bat across the plate (tip toward the plate), −90° at the catcher, −180° pointing away from the plate behind him, −225° wrapped behind his head toward the pitcher's side. A increases through the swing (counter-clockwise seen from above for a right-hander) and is **unwrapped**: continuous from L, never reduced modulo 360°. The jet's contact azimuth (from `atan2`) is taken on the branch A_L + 180° < A_c ≤ A_L + 540°, that is −45° < A_c ≤ 315° (a bat cannot meet the ball pointing back past the catcher; the planner's contact azimuths run −10° to +24°).
- **Lag λ** (`BatLag()`): the angle between â (knob → cap) and the vector from the clavicle to the mid-wrists, as above.
- **Shoulder elevation θ and azimuth φ** (`PolarShoulder()`): [4]'s code takes the polar angles of the vector **from the elbow to the shoulder** joint centre in the upper trunk's frame, the same formula for both arms: θ = its angle from the trunk's long axis (so 0° = arm hanging, 90° = horizontal), φ = atan2(y, x) − 90° in the upper trunk's own frame (step 0, below). This spec never uses that vector or [4]'s axes directly. It places the **shoulder-to-elbow** unit vector d in its own trunk frame (x_T forward from the chest, y_T toward the lead side, t̂ up the trunk; §5.3):

  **d = −cos θ·t̂ + sin θ·(cos φ·x_T + sin φ·y_T)** (reading 1, **settled by step 0**),

  so φ = 0° is the upper arm pointing forward from the chest and negative φ turns it toward the batter's rear side (the rear arm out to the side and back; the lead arm across the chest). Reading 2, the only other one the code allows, reverses the horizontal part (φ + 180°) and is **ruled out**. *Step 0 (Appendix B, p. 185–186 of the PDF, printed 171–172; Appendix C `PolarShoulder()`, printed 185–188):* the code reads the shoulder and elbow joint centres `V_*_Shoulder_JC_Trunk` and `V_*_Elbow_JC_Trunk`, markers expressed in the upper trunk's own frame, takes shoulder − elbow, θ = acos(Z/|r|) (so Z is the trunk's long axis, up) and φ = atan2(Y, X) − 90°. Appendix B's diagrams draw the angles but label no axes. The axes follow from [4]'s data: the trail arm at launch (elevation 71°, azimuth −78°, elbow out to the side) puts elbow → shoulder along trunk +X with a small +Y, so +X is toward the lead side and, for a right-handed frame with Z up, +Y is **backward**; then azimuth 0° at contact (both arms, −1° and −9°) is the elbow → shoulder vector pointing straight back at the chest, the arms reaching forward, as they must. In the spec's terms this is exactly reading 1 (φ = 0° the upper arm forward, negative φ toward the rear side; forward coordinate of the elbows rising +0.07 → +0.19 m, §11 case 2). Residual doubt: the frame's handedness is inferred, not labelled; the contact check (arms forward, not back) would fail under the mirror image. **No spec number moves** (W_L, W_93, §5.3 unchanged). **The check that told them apart before was [4]'s own prose**: during swing initiation "the trail upper arm dropped and moved forward" while its azimuth rose −78° → −42°, and during acceleration the lead arm went "down and forward" as its azimuth rose −24° → −1°. Under reading 1 the rear elbow's forward coordinate (along x_T, from the rear shoulder) rises +0.07 → +0.19 m from launch to −93 ms and the lead elbow's +0.30 → +0.32 → +0.32 m; under reading 2 both are reversed (the elbows move back). The oracle asserts the sign (§11). Reading 2 would also put the launch hands only 0.15–0.28 m from the clavicle, against the swing-start hands near the back shoulder the sources describe.
- **Elbow and knee**: flexion, 0° straight.

### 4.3 The swing's clock and [4]'s times

*Revised for D27 (§6.7) and its review (P1-1): the time is each swing's own, and every time below names the instant it is measured from.*

**Three instants** start a swing, and they are not the same:

- **The first motion**, from rest: t_s = t_c − T. It maps onto [4]'s **weight commitment, −170 ms** ([4] §4.1.3–4.1.4: "swing initiation", when the bat "finally began to rotate forward"). v3's bat leaves rest here; [4]'s was already unwinding at 236°/s (since its minimum azimuth at −243 ms, 7° in 73 ms), which v3 drops so that the client's load can end on the launch pose exactly; the travel the dropped rate would have made is kept (the template covers [4]'s whole sweep from −170 ms, Î, §6.7.3).
- **The bat's start**: the bat passes 10% of its peak angular speed, about 0.882·T before contact (150 ms for the average batter's middle pitch; 150 to 152 ms over the golden aims). [4]'s own bat passes 10% at −170 ms because it is already turning there. A bat sensor's "time to contact" (Blast's 0.149 s, start undefined) is of this kind.
- **The hands' start**: the hands pass 10% of their peak speed, 158 ms before contact for the average batter. This is v2's definition of its swing start (agent-models §3.6), the instant its 0.150 s was fitted to.

**The swing's time from rest** is T = T_t × (T_b / T̄) / (1 + e): T_t the average batter's time at this aim (§6.7.3), T̄ = 0.170 s [E: [4]'s commitment-to-contact time, at which the template's launch wrap is [4]'s −225°], e the swing's effort draw, and **T_b = `t_swing` × T̄ / 0.150 s**: the batter's own `t_swing` (`QuickHands`, `BatterAttrs::t_swing`, 0.150 s at grade 50, as today) is his middle-middle swing's time from **the bat's start**, and T_b the same swing from rest (§7). [4]'s times are scaled into the swing by **s_T = T / T₄**, T₄ = 0.170 s, so an event [4] puts at τ₄ comes at τ = s_T·τ₄, and [4]'s rates are used only as ratios to the contact rate (§5).

**The decision clock does not change**: t_commit = t̂_arrival − `t_swing` − T_latency (agent-models §3.4, as today): he decides T_latency = 0.05 s before his usual swing's bat start. His first motion from rest then comes (T_b − `t_swing`) = 0.02 s × T_b/T̄ earlier than that start, 0.03 s after the decision for the average batter; a swing whose own T is longer leaves earlier still, inside that margin (§6.7.5).

*History.* Step 1 squeezed [4]'s times into a single T_swing of 0.150 s from rest for every batter (D26, Q48 = a) and kept [4]'s angles, which forced the swing's speed and not its time to absorb every difference between batters and efforts (§6.7.1); D27 superseded that. The first draft of the family (§6.7.7 before its review) compared 0.170 s from rest with today's 0.150 s from the hands' start and put a decision 20 ms earlier to the owner: that was a change of definition, not of the swing.

#### 6.7.1 What makes a real bat speed up into the ball

A real swing is driven. The legs and trunk turn the shoulders, the arms pull the hands round, and the bat, cocked behind the hands, is pulled after them; as the arms slow (the trunk peaks at −57 ms, the lead arm at −43 ms [4]) the bat's lag is released and the bat turns faster than the arms ever did (uncocking at 1,588°/s at −20 ms [3]). The bat speeds up into the ball because something is still pushing it, and the push and the release come in the same order on every swing. A weaker or an easier swing is the same sequence with less push: every segment is slower, so the same path takes longer.

That is a physical fact, not only a description, and the probe shows it with a driven arm-and-bat chain (a planar double pendulum: a torque at the body's axis, the bat hinged at the hands with a wrist stop holding its cock; `driven_chain_probe.py`, not fitted to [4]). Scaling every torque by f from 0.70 to 1.30 gives the same path in the swing's own phase, the time × 1/√f and every speed × √f: time × contact speed is constant (9.290 m to four figures), the bat's speed rises to one peak 1.049 times its contact value at the same phase of every swing (−15.3 ms at f 0.70, −12.8 at 1.0, −11.3 at 1.30, so at a fixed share of the swing's time). Gravity on the bat (applied fully in the swing's plane, the worst case) breaks the similarity by under 1% (T × v 9.355–9.410 m, peak 1.040–1.044). So **a slower swing by the same batter is the same swing, slower, and it takes longer**; MLB's bat tracking says the same (note 10 §2: at the same contact depth a 4 mph slower swing is 0.7% shorter, so it lasts about 5% longer).

**What the chain does and does not show** (review P3-5). The identity is exact because the probe's drive is a constant torque and its wrist stop's stiffness and damping scale with f and √f: equations with only inertial and configuration-dependent forces are self-similar under t → t/√f. A torque profiled in time, muscle's force–velocity relation or an actively timed release would break it, so the chain supports the *within-batter* half (an easier swing, effort ±3 sd being a drive of × 0.75 to × 1.29, is the same path, slower; T ∝ 1/v and T ∝ 1/√f are the same statement, v ∝ √f), and not the across-batter half: a stronger batter with the same body would swing the same path in less time. That **λ ∝ `BatSpeed` at a common time** rests on MLB's swing lengths alone (note 10 §1).

**What the merged path did instead.** Step 1 fitted [4]'s measured shape (angles against time) and then forced every swing into it with fixed numbers: the launch 225° of azimuth behind the ball for everyone, 168° still to turn 82 ms before contact for everyone, 0.150 s for everyone, while the contact speed followed `BatSpeed` and effort. The turn's size and time were fixed and its speed was not, so the measured rate shape could not fit: a slow swing had to turn faster in mid-swing than at the ball (1.24× at three sd slow) and a fast one had to ease off and surge again. No bound can make that realistic; the construction has to put the batter's travel and time where the drive puts them.

**What a model needs for this to come out by construction.** (1) A shape defined in the swing's own phase u = τ/T: angles as shares of the swing's own travel and rates as shares of its own contact rate, so the shape does not depend on how fast the swing is. (2) The travel and the time each following from something: within a batter the travel is fixed (his launch is the same on every pitch, D22, and MLB's slower swing at the same depth is as long) so the time follows the speed, T ∝ 1/(contact speed); across batters MLB's swing length rises in proportion to bat speed (note 10 §1: length ÷ speed flat at 68–70 ms), so the travel follows `BatSpeed` at a common time, and `QuickHands`, a quicker swing at the same speed, is a shorter swing. (3) The whole path scaled together (azimuth, elevation and hands), not one angle: scaling the azimuth alone leaves the elevation's 72° drop and the hands' 0.6 m to be covered in a shorter swing, and a compact batter's sweet spot then overspeeds (1.13× at the middle pitch; §6.7.2, model ii-a).

#### 6.7.3 The model

**The template** is the grade-50 batter's swing (`BatSpeed` 32 m/s, effort 0, his own height and stance) at the aim, built as §6.1–§6.4 with two changes:

- Its launch azimuth is not fixed: A_L,t = A_ref − Ȧ_s,ref·T̄·Î, where A_ref and Ȧ_s,ref are the azimuth and the horizontal azimuth rate (§6.3: Ȧ·cos E / cos e(0)) of his own middle-middle contact jet, T̄ = 0.170 s [E: [4]'s commitment-to-contact time] and **Î = 0.57613** the share of [4]'s rate shape over its 170 ms (its spline integrated from −170 ms to contact, over the contact rate × 170 ms, §5.4; Î_93 = 0.44051 from −93 ms). At H 1.85 m: **A_L,t = −222.81°** (Fortenbaugh's −225°: the measured wrap, at the measured time, at the jet's horizontal contact rate 2,324°/s; −225.26° on the azimuth rate before the D28 refit) and −169.23° at u_93, 174.0° short of contact ([4]'s 168° in its text, 166° by its rate graph and 163° by its azimuth graph at its own contact rate 2,214°/s; × 2,324/2,214 that is 174°).
- Its time at the aim is **T_t = (A_c − A_L,t)/(Ȧ_s·Î)**: the time in which [4]'s rate shape, scaled to this aim's horizontal contact rate, covers this aim's sweep. Then the azimuth is [4]'s shape from launch (from rest, §4.3) to contact but for δ_A, which carries the jet's rate above Ȧ_s and its second derivative (zero value, rate and acceleration at u_93; at 0 (Ȧ_c − Ȧ_s)·T_t and the jet's Ä_c·T_t² less the shape's): the rate stays within +0.03 / −0.06 of [4]'s (as a share of the contact rate), peaking at 1.02–1.06× at −9 ms ([4]'s 1.09× at −10 ms). In the zone (x ±0.15 m, z 0.55–0.95 m) T_t runs from 0.162 to 0.180 s, across the plate's width 0.154–0.184 s; at the edges of the aim range 0.152–0.191 s. Inside pitches are met further round at a faster rate, so sooner (11 ms on the inside edge at mid-height); low pitches later (the bat drops further: 7 ms at the bottom of the zone), high ones sooner (5 ms). That T_t follows the aim is an outcome of the geometry [E-outcome], not a source: the common coaching view has the inside pitch needing an earlier start; no source here measures it (§15). The batter plans it from his own aim at the commit, so nothing is read from the world.

χ_L and χ_93 (§6.1) are computed at the template's own knot azimuths: **χ_L = 305.7409°, χ_93 = 314.5773°** (full precision in `golden/swing_v3.json`; the misfit sees the bat at −222.81° and −169.23°).

**The batter.** His travel factor and his swing's time are

- **λ = (`BatSpeed`/32 m/s) × (T_b/T̄)**, T_b his own swing time (`QuickHands`, agent-models §10: T̄ at grade 50, ρ 1.05);
- **T = T_t × (T_b/T̄) / (1 + e)**, e the swing's effort draw (`SwingEffort`, as now).

His path, for each coordinate X of (A, E, W_x, W_y, W_z), in u = τ/T on [−1, 0]:

**X(u) = X_c + λ·(X_t(u) − X_c) + h(u)·F + δ(u)**,

where X_t is the template's at the aim (§6.3), X_c the contact jet's value (the template's and his are the same pose); δ the correction on [u_93, 0] with zero value, rate and acceleration at u_93 and zero value, the jet's rate × T less λ·X_t′(0) and the jet's acceleration × T² less λ·X_t″(0) at contact (a quintic: the first two are zero to rounding on a planned swing, the third small, from the centripetal parts of the jet's acceleration, which scale with speed squared); and **h(u)·F the launch's fade**: h = 1 − (10x³ − 15x⁴ + 6x⁵), x = (u + 1)/(u_93 + 1), on [−1, u_93] and 0 after it, and F = [X_ref + λ_nom·(X_L,t − X_ref)] − [X_c + λ·(X_L,t − X_c)], with X_ref the batter's middle-middle contact pose and X_L,t the template's launch. So his launch is the template's scaled about his own middle-middle contact pose, at rest, the same on every pitch (D22), whatever the aim and effort (tested to 1e-9, §11): **the bat's angles (A, E) by λ, the hands by min(λ, 1)** (in the fade, λ_nom for A and E, min(λ_nom, 1) for W). A short swing starts with the hands nearer the ball; a long one starts with [4]'s launch hands, as the average batter does, and its extra travel is in the bat (review P2-1: scaled by λ the long swings' hands passed out of the lead arm's reach by 6 to 15 cm). **Along the path the hands' displacement is scaled by λ_W = λ·min(1, 1.10/λ_nom)** [E] (the D28 refit; the bat's by λ): at the measured stance a long swing's hands, pushed back by λ from the contact pose, left the lead arm's reach at −93 ms (by up to 3.7 cm for the slowest hands, λ 1.158); the template's lead arm has 7.4 cm of slack there, about a tenth of the hands' way to contact, so a long swing's hands go back at most 10% further than the average batter's (the lead arm then within 2 cm of straight) and the rest of its travel is the bat's. #### 6.7.5 Timing and the decision clock

**The effort is execution noise** (review P2-4): drawn with the swing at the commit by `motor::execute_swing` on `SwingEffort` (σ 4.5%, truncated at 4σ); the batter does not choose it. The model treats each draw as a whole-swing tempo that the body times for itself: the swing starts at t_c − T for its own T, so an easier swing starts earlier and meets the ball when planned, the timing draw t₀ apart, as today. The alternative, the start held at the intended T and an easier swing arriving late, would make a swing late by about 7.3 ms per sd of effort, more than the game's whole contact-timing spread (sd 5.6 ms), and change games a lot. MLB's tracking bounds it: within a batter, swings 1 mph slower are met 0.71 in deeper (note 10 §2: speed against depth r 0.29, sd 4.3 mph and 10.5 in), where a swing that took its extra 1.4% of time without starting earlier would be 3.5 in deeper; so at most a fifth of an easier swing's extra time shows as lateness, and less, since part of that correlation runs the other way (a ball met deep meets a bat before its peak, so slower). The owner's choice in §6.7.7 keeps this open.

**In step 2 the plan carries the intended time**: T_intended = T_t × T_b / T̄ at effort 0, fixed at the commit from the batter's own aim (decision code, `plan_swing`); `apply_placement` divides it by (1 + e) every time it applies the placement, as it multiplies the rates. A re-plan keeps T_intended; it never reads the executed swing's start (that would put an executed quantity in decision code, invariant 3).

**The decision clock does not change** (§4.3): t_commit = t̂_arrival − `t_swing` − T_latency, the decision T_latency = 0.05 s before his usual swing's bat start, as today. His swing leaves rest (T_b − `t_swing`) = 0.02 s × T_b/T̄ before that start, so 0.03 s after the decision at grade 50; a swing whose own T is longer leaves earlier, and one that would leave before the decision is moved to it (`start_no_earlier_than`, as now) and meets the ball late by the difference. Over the zone (tested per batter, review P3-3; the refitted template, whose low pitches take longer): at −2 sd of effort every batter's swing in the zone starts in time, the slowest hands (`QuickHands` −3 sd) at −1.5 sd; at the plate's outer edge low, the grade-50 batter's has 0.7 ms to spare and the slowest hands' starts 7 ms late; at −3 sd 10 ms late for the grade-50 batter there and 19 ms for the slowest hands. About 2% of swings are easier than −2 sd.

**A mid-swing re-plan** (pa.rs, every 10 ms to the cut-off) keeps T and the launch; the new aim's jet is met by letting the travel follow, λ' = λ·T/T_nominal(new aim) (the contact rate then matches exactly), with the launch held by the fade. An aim moved 5 cm moves T_nominal by up to 1.6% and adds at most 0.6% to a speed valley (10 cm: 3.0%, 1.2%); a re-planned swing keeps [4]'s rate share at −93 ms (0.565 of the horizontal contact rate; holding the travel instead gives 0.572–0.574).

**Checked swings** (review P3-7): the brake is allowed only before t_s + 0.6·T, that is up to 0.4·T before contact: 68 ms for the average batter (0.170 s), from 49 ms (T 0.123 s) to 98 ms (0.244 s) over the family. `pa.rs`'s observation loop stops 70 ms before the aim, so for T under about 0.175 s the loop binds and the last brake is about 20 ms before contact; for longer swings the 0.6 rule closes earlier, up to 48 ms before contact. §8.3's brake stops are the average batter's.

## 8. Early, late, checked swings and the ruling

### 8.1 Early and late swings

Timing is the jet's t_c against the ball, as now: an early swing meets the ball after t_c with the bat further round (pulled, hooked), a late one before it (pushed, sliced), with the slopes of §2. A swing made late by a late decision starts later (`start_no_earlier_than`) and is the same swing shifted, as now. Each swing's own time (§6.7.5) moves its start: an easier swing or an outside pitch starts earlier, inside the latency after the commit; a start that would come before the commit is moved to it and the swing is late by the difference. The client's body timing against Δ = t_c − T_a (player-visuals §13.2) is unchanged.

### 8.2 The brake

A check swing still starts braking at t_b = (the tick when EV_swing − EV_take < −θ_check) + latency, only before t_s + 0.6·T (the swing's own time; as now). v3 brakes by slowing **the swing's own clock**: the pose at t is the path at

σ(t) = t for t ≤ t_b; σ(t) = t_b + s − s²/(2·B) with s = min(t − t_b, B), B = `BRAKE_S` = 0.040 s,

so σ advances by B/2 = 20 ms of path while slowing to a stop, the bat and hands stay on their planned path, and every velocity is the path's times σ̇ = 1 − s/B. (v2 stops each rate bump from its value at t_b, which carries the bat off its path; v3's form is the simpler and the more physical: the same move, slowed.)

### 8.3 What a checked swing does now

The brake comes at −82 ± 32 ms (baseline). Today it never comes after t_c − 10 ms (the abort point, 60 ms before contact, plus the 50 ms latency); since §6.7 the abort point is the swing's own (t_s + 0.6·T, 0.4·T before contact: 68 ms for the average batter), and with `pa.rs`'s observation loop stopping 70 ms before the aim the last brake comes about 20 ms before contact for T under 0.175 s and up to 48 ms before it for the longest swings (§6.7.5). The stops below are the average batter's (T 0.170–0.172 s on the golden aims). At −82 ms v3's bat is about 160° short of its contact azimuth (pointing away from the plate and up), and 20 ms more of path brings it to −120° to −123° (oracle, three golden aims): well back. A brake at −40 ms stops it at −41° to −45°, at −25 ms at −12° to −1°; only brakes later than about 20 ms before contact carry it past 0° (at −12 ms: +16° to +31°, a swing), which the average batter's brake can no longer reach: a check swing ruled a swing on the yaw needs a quick swing (short T). A braked v3 bat almost never touches the ball and almost never passes 0°. Today 3.4% of checked swings are strikes (310 touches, 36 on the yaw), about 0.6 a game; v3 should make nearly all of them balls. No source here measures how often a real check swing is ruled a swing; this is reported and counted (D26, Q47 = a: no change to the brake or the ruling without a source).

3. **If something moves more than expected**, look for a bug first (a planner or path error, AGENTS.md), then the checked-swing mechanism (§8): the brake and the ruling are the only new physics on the game path.
4. **Only then retune**, and only what calibration-targets §2.3 allows: `K_swing` (the miss scale) and the `BatSpeed` population mean, in that order. **Never** the anchors, the jet's Statcast fits, or a checked-swing threshold toward a stat. If late contact is off a Tier M band (speed by depth, spray by timing), the fix is the interior shape from [4]'s curves (step 0), not a knob. Swing length, if it leaves 6–8.5 ft, is a Tier M finding about the path's shape (§15), fixed in the curves' conditions from [4], never through speed.
5. **The batter's move (D28) and the deeper contact (D29) come before this, as their own step** (§13 step 3b), with v2 still the swing: perception, HBP, the run to first and the timing of contact change with them, and K%, BB%, HBP%, infield hits and the batted-ball rates are re-measured there with their own ledger row and calibration report, so that step 4's row shows the swing alone.
